DEPT v18: Placement Discussion and Fuzzer-Validated Bug Detection Support

Classification

This thread concerns a new kernel infrastructure feature: DEPT (DEPendency Tracker), Byungchul Park's 42-patch v18 series aimed at detecting deadlock possibilities by tracking wait/event dependencies — complementing the existing Lockdep infrastructure. This is not an NFS-specific change or bug fix, but it has indirect relevance to NFS client development given the complex sleep/wake patterns in NFS code paths (e.g., RPC call completions, page lock waits, delegation recalls).

Prior Context (Bridged Briefly)

Over prior weeks, the DEPT series underwent detailed review. Most recently (week of 2026-02-26), Byungchul Park resolved outstanding review items on patches 31/42 and 34/42, accepting Petr Pavlu's type-safety refinements for module event site marking and Dirk Behme's __rust_helper attribute convention. The series has been iterating through multiple revisions since its initial introduction in December 2025.

This Week's Activity (Mar 13–19)

This week saw two messages focused on the organizational placement of DEPT within the kernel source tree and a strong endorsement of its practical value from a fuzzer developer.

Placement Question: Where Should DEPT Live?

On March 17, Byungchul Park addressed Linus Torvalds and Ingo Molnar directly, raising the question of where DEPT should be maintained once accepted into the mainline tree:

"If it is to be maintained in the source tree, which subsystem would be the most suitable for its management? Personally, I believe introducing a dedicated dependency subsystem under 'kernel/' would be ideal, though managing it within the already well-maintained scheduler or locking subsystems might be more practical." Byungchul Park

Park explained his reasoning for avoiding placement under the locking subsystem:

"Since DEPT tracks not only locking mechanisms but also general sleep and wake events, I have avoided placing it under the locking subsystem thus far." Byungchul Park

He expressed confidence in DEPT's methodology despite acknowledging room for improvement:

"Although the DEPT tool still has areas for improvement, I am confident it employs the most appropriate method for tracking dependencies within Linux kernel." Byungchul Park

Endorsement from Fuzzer Developer: Real Bugs Detected

On March 18, Yunseong Kim provided a strong endorsement of DEPT's practical value, drawing on his experience running kernel fuzzers:

"I would like to support this proposal by noting that we are currently tracking a number of kernel bugs uniquely detectable by DEPT." Yunseong Kim

Kim highlighted a specific class of bugs that DEPT can uncover but other tools cannot:

"In my experience running kernel fuzzers, many of these issues were previously flagged as hung task and left unresolved because they involved wait/event dependencies for PG_locked and writeback that are difficult to track otherwise. DEPT provides the clarity necessary to address these cases." Yunseong Kim

He concluded with a clear recommendation for mainline inclusion:

"I believe having DEPT in the mainline tree will be a significant asset for identifying and fixing these kinds of hidden, latent bugs." Yunseong Kim

Significance for NFS Client Development

While this thread does not directly modify NFS code, DEPT's maturation has notable indirect importance:

  1. Deadlock detection for complex wait patterns: NFS client operations involve intricate wait/event dependencies (RPC completions, page lock waits via PG_locked, writeback dependencies, delegation recalls) that are invisible to Lockdep. Kim's mention of PG_locked and writeback is directly relevant — these are core mechanisms in the NFS page cache and writeback paths.

  2. Fuzzer-discovered bugs: Many of the bugs DEPT uniquely detects were previously dismissed as "hung task" warnings. For NFS, which has historically struggled with subtle deadlock conditions in its RPC and page locking interactions, DEPT could prove invaluable for uncovering latent issues.

  3. No direct bug fixes this week: This week's activity was purely about organizational placement and endorsement — no code changes or specific NFS-related bug fixes were discussed.

Conclusion

This week marked a shift from technical review to strategic discussion. Byungchul Park raised the question of DEPT's home in the kernel tree, while Yunseong Kim provided compelling evidence of DEPT's real-world value through fuzzer-validated bug detection. The endorsement specifically citing PG_locked and writeback dependencies — mechanisms central to NFS client operation — underscores the tool's potential utility for the NFS subsystem. No resolution on placement was reached this week; the question remains open pending feedback from Linus Torvalds and Ingo Molnar.
