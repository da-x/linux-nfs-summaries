Case Folding v4 Series: Target Kernel Version Clarification (v6.19 ruled out; VFS tree path confirmed)

Summary

Case Folding Behavior: v4 Series Follow-Up — Target Kernel Version Clarification

Type: New feature / protocol enhancement (continued from prior week)

Prior Week Recap

Last week, Chuck Lever posted version 4 of his 16-patch series "Exposing case folding behavior" on January 14th. The series introduces a kernel-wide fileattr_get mechanism for filesystems to report their case sensitivity and case preservation characteristics, enabling NFSD, ksmbd, and NFS clients to accurately convey filename semantics across protocol boundaries. Key review feedback from Jan Kara, Darrick J. Wong, and Christian Brauner focused on struct layout optimization (packing flags into fa_xflags rather than a dedicated field), while FAT maintainer OGAWA Hirofumi flagged an edge case with the nocase mount option. See the prior week's summary for full details.

This Week's Activity

This week saw only two brief exchanges, both focused on a practical question about the patch series' target kernel version.

Target Kernel Version Inquiry

Cedric Blancher from the Institute Pasteur asked a straightforward but important question in [id://CALXu0Uf6B6GPkz5sjpjwqpyojQx7rg3v3UMnLQG4vo1hHiJeoQ@mail.gmail.com]:

"Which Linux version is this patch series targeting? Linux 6.19?"

This question reflects the practical concern of downstream users and testers who need to know when they can expect these case sensitivity improvements to land in a stable kernel release.

Chuck Lever's Response: VFS Tree, Not v6.19

Chuck Lever responded promptly in [id://1de4afee-4cd0-4738-8600-c21ffedd9f24@app.fastmail.com], providing two key pieces of information:

"Because this series is a cross-subsystem change, it is almost certainly going through Christian's VFS tree, not through the NFSD tree. Thus none of us on this mailing list can answer the 'when' part of your question."

He also definitively ruled out the current release cycle:

"I can say definitely that it's not going to be v6.19, though. That release is now closed to new features."

This response clarifies two important aspects of the patch series' lifecycle:

  1. Submission path: The series will go through Christian Brauner's VFS tree rather than the NFSD subtree, which makes sense given that the fileattr_get interface touches core VFS infrastructure and twelve different filesystem implementations.
  2. Timeline: With v6.19 already closed to new features, the earliest realistic target would be v6.20 or later, pending resolution of the open review items (struct layout and FAT nocase edge case).

NFS Client Relevance

While this week's discussion was brief and administrative in nature, it underscores the importance of the underlying work for NFS client development. The series' patch 10/16 — implementing fileattr_get for case sensitivity in the NFS client — is critical for re-export scenarios. As noted in last week's summary:

"An NFS server re-exporting an NFS mount point needs to report the case sensitivity behavior of the underlying filesystem to its clients."

Without this capability, Linux NFS servers acting as re-export proxies cannot accurately convey case handling semantics to their own clients, potentially causing incorrect filename collision assumptions and lookup failures on case-insensitive shares.

Status and Outlook

The v4 series remains under review with two open items:

  • Struct layout: Whether to pack case sensitivity flags into the existing fa_xflags field (as suggested by Jan Kara) or keep a dedicated fa_case_behavior field.
  • FAT nocase mount option: OGAWA Hirofumi's observation that MSDOS with nocase preserves rather than uppercases filenames may require refinement in the FAT implementation.

The series is expected to proceed through the VFS tree, with a target kernel version of v6.20 or later.
