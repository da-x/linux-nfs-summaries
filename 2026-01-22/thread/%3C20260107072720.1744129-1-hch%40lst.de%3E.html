NFS Delegation LRU Series Endorsed for Merge by Anna Schumaker

Summary

NFS Delegation LRU: Anna Schumaker Endorses Hellwig's Series for Merge

Overview

This week marked a quiet but decisive conclusion to the review of Christoph Hellwig's 24-patch series introducing an LRU-based eviction policy for NFS client delegations. After weeks of technical debate over delegation recall strategy and architectural design, Anna Schumaker provided formal acknowledgment of the patches, signaling readiness for integration into the mainline kernel tree.

Prior Weeks (Brief Recap)

The thread originated on 2026-01-07 when Hellwig posted his series addressing a long-standing inefficiency in NFS client delegation management: delegations freed from closed files were returned immediately to the server, even if other delegations had been idle far longer. The LRU-based approach delivers dramatic performance improvements — up to a 97% reduction in on-the-wire operations and ~40% speedup for Python module import workloads.

The previous two weeks saw extensive discussion around:

  • Eviction policy design: Whether LRU was sufficient or more sophisticated heuristics (e.g., LFU, mtime-based) were warranted. Hellwig defended the simplicity of LRU, noting its alignment with kernel-wide cache eviction conventions.
  • Client vs. server recall responsibility: Sagi Grimberg argued that servers are better positioned to drive delegation recalls, while Trond Myklebust countered with practical concerns about destructive workloads like rm -rf causing excessive recall storms if clients hoarded delegations too aggressively.
  • A latent bug identified: The NFS client ignores the "keep" parameter in RECALL_ANY operations, returning all delegations indiscriminately rather than respecting the server's request to retain a specified number.

This Week: Formal Endorsement and Merge Preparation

The sole message this week came from Anna Schumaker, who responded directly to Hellwig's lighthearted plea from last week [20260115162437.GA17257@lst.de]:

"Any comments on this series except how to make it more complicated? :)"

Anna Schumaker responded:

"The patches look good to me. Thanks for doing this cleanup! I just pushed them out to my linux-next branch for the next merge."

This endorsement is significant: Schumaker's inclusion of the series in her linux-next branch indicates that the patches have passed review and are on track for upstream integration in the next kernel merge window. Her characterization of the work as "cleanup" reflects the series' dual nature — it both simplifies a tangled delegation management codebase and introduces a performance-critical architectural improvement.

Key Technical Arguments (Consolidated from Full Thread)

  1. LRU over LFU: Hellwig rejected more complex eviction heuristics, arguing that LRU aligns with kernel-wide cache management practices and avoids the overhead of tracking usage frequency across data structures prone to cache-line thrashing.

  2. Client-side proactivity remains essential: Trond Myklebust emphasized that clients must continue to return delegations proactively, warning that excessive retention could trigger costly recall storms during destructive operations:

    "Imagine an 'rm -rf' operation if you have to recall delegations from various clients on all the files in the tree because they are caching read delegations 'just in case'."

  3. RECALL_ANY implementation gap: Sagi Grimberg identified that the client ignores the "keep" argument in RECALL_ANY recalls — a bug that could be addressed as a follow-up once the LRU infrastructure is in place, since Hellwig noted the LRU would naturally support reclaiming from its tail when the server requests it.

  4. Data-driven policy decisions: The community agreed that future delegation retention changes should be justified by empirical workload data rather than assumptions about application behavior.

Classification

This thread concerns a new feature (LRU-based delegation eviction) with significant code cleanup implications. The discussion also surfaced a bug fix opportunity (the RECALL_ANY "keep" parameter handling) for future work.

Final Conclusions

  • Hellwig's 24-patch LRU delegation series has been formally endorsed by Anna Schumaker and queued for the next merge window via her linux-next branch.
  • No substantive objections remained; the previous weeks' debates centered on design philosophy rather than concrete flaws in the implementation.
  • The RECALL_ANY "keep" parameter bug was identified as a natural follow-up improvement, enabled by the new LRU infrastructure.
  • The series represents a major step forward for NFS client delegation management, delivering both performance gains (up to 97% fewer RPCs for certain workloads) and code simplification.
