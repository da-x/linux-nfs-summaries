NFS: Intense Debate Over Filesystem Opt-In Flag for nfsd Export — Naming, Semantics, and Scope in Dispute

Summary

NFS: Debate Over Filesystem Opt-In Mechanism for nfsd Export Support

Overview

This week saw an intense and at times heated continuation of the discussion around Jeff Layton's 29-patch series [PATCH 00/29] fs: require filesystems to explicitly opt-in to nfsd export support [id://20260115-exportfs-nfsd-v1-0-8e80160e3c0c@kernel.org]. This is a hardening / bug fix initiative targeting the NFS server (nfsd) subsystem, with broad implications across many kernel filesystems.

Last week, the series was introduced with the EXPORT_OP_STABLE_HANDLES flag and received initial reviews from filesystem maintainers. The naming debate between "stable" vs. "persistent" handles was largely resolved in favor of "stable," after Chuck Lever argued that "persistent" could confuse matters with NFSv4 VOLATILE handles. This week, however, the discussion deepened significantly — evolving into a fundamental disagreement about what problem the flag is actually solving, and whether the naming (and indeed the approach) correctly reflects the semantics.

Key Technical Arguments

1. The check_export() Warning Addition

Early in the week, Jeff Layton addressed Amir Goldstein's prior-week question about whether the guard should also be placed in check_export():

"It certainly won't hurt to add a check for this to check_export(), and I've gone ahead and done so. To be clear, doing that won't prevent the filesystem from being exported, but you will get a warning like this when you try:

exportfs: /sys/fs/cgroup does not support NFS export" [id://8e4c3df4828351c677186bf018061f2b1fd1b48e.camel@kernel.org]

Amir acknowledged this was "better than no warning at all" [id://CAOQ4uxhkZNueydP0tTCAj6tuzKWPTYB7=JR_hb4gaavSKQ8C2w@mail.gmail.com]. Jeff noted, however, that the warning wouldn't catch all scenarios — such as wildcard exports via / *(rw,crossmnt,...) in /etc/exports — and lamented that "it's not possible to completely vet the export table" [id://0cb769af0673ea1c28df7fa9a1cefe3ec23bc367.camel@kernel.org].

2. NeilBrown's Challenge: Is "Stable Handles" the Right Problem Statement?

The most significant development this week was NeilBrown's sustained challenge to the premise of the patch series. He argued that EXPORT_OP_STABLE_HANDLES is a misnomer because file handle stability is not actually the problem:

"I don't like PERSISTENT. I'd rather call a spade a spade. EXPORT_OP_SUPPORTS_NFS_EXPORT or EXPORT_OP_NOT_NFS_COMPATIBLE. The issue here is NFS export and indirection doesn't bring any benefits." [id://176877859306.16766.15009835437490907207@noble.neil.brown.name]

NeilBrown later provided a detailed taxonomy of the actual issues at play [id://176890126683.16766.5241619788613840985@noble.neil.brown.name]:

  • Filehandle stability: "As far as I know all filesystems provide stable filehandles when the 'subtree_check' export option is not used."
  • Filehandle uniqueness: Filesystems like cgroupfs may reuse handles across reboots.
  • GETATTR always reporting file size of 0: The concrete symptom Jeff observed with cgroupfs exports.
  • Maintainer feature choice: A maintainer may simply choose not to support NFS export due to locking/lease complexity or lack of use case.

NeilBrown concluded: "my intuition was always that file handle stability was never the real issue, and maintainer choice was. Hence my rejection of the 'STABLE_HANDLES' name."

3. Christoph Hellwig's Pushback: Naming After Semantics, Not Users

Christoph Hellwig strongly opposed naming the flag after NFS or nfsd:

"No, it absolutely is not [the issue]. And the whole concept of calling something after the initial or main use is a recipe for a mess. Pick a name that conveys what the flag is about, and document those semantics well." [id://aW3SAKIr_QsnEE5Q@infradead.org]

HCH emphasized that the flag has uses beyond nfsd:

"This flag needs to be propagate to userspace through statx or the file attrs. As I said before there is plenty of code in userspace that does rely on the traditional file handle semantics." [id://aW3cdlSjcXqcV1VO@infradead.org]

He also argued against "arbitrary, selective and very narrow choices about use cases without technical backing":

"The maintainer has no way to disallow exporting through nfs. They can at best disallow exporting using the kernel nfs daemon if we provide that facility." [id://aXCg-MqXH0E6IuwS@infradead.org]

4. Christian Brauner's Hard Line: Pseudo-filesystems Must Not Be Exported

Christian Brauner made his position clear and unyielding:

"It is very much fine for a filesystems to support file handles without wanting to support exporting via NFS. That is especially true for in-kernel pseudo filesystems." [id://20260120-hacken-revision-88209121ac2c@brauner]

"I will hard NAK anything that starts mandating that export of filesystems must be allowed simply because their file handles fit export criteria. I do not care whether pidfs or nsfs file handles fit the bill. They will not be exported." [id://20260120-hacken-revision-88209121ac2c@brauner]

Brauner expressed frustration at the circular debate: "And we are reliving the same arguments over and over" [id://20260120-hacken-revision-88209121ac2c@brauner].

5. Tensions in the Discussion

The exchange between NeilBrown and Christoph Hellwig grew notably tense. After NeilBrown suggested that Christian Brauner was recommending "we give in to bullying," HCH responded sharply:

"I find your suggestion that anything you disagree with is bullying extremely offensive." [id://aW8w2SRyFnmA2uqk@infradead.org]

Christian Brauner attempted to de-escalate, noting his earlier comment was meant as a willingness to compromise on naming: "It's not a hill worth dying on" [id://20260119-kanufahren-meerjungfrau-775048806544@brauner], but later expressed annoyance at the "lengthy and to me somewhat pointless debate" [id://20260120-bindung-eselsfell-fadec0d95909@brauner].

6. Jeff Layton's Evolving Position

By week's end, Jeff Layton appeared to be reconsidering the approach. He acknowledged NeilBrown's analysis:

"You're quite right that this covers more than handle stability." [id://f4c9cd498571f5bf976579ad33239408b1324258.camel@kernel.org]

He conceded he had been conflating "uniqueness" with "stability" regarding cgroupfs:

"cgroupfs does have stable handles, by your definition above. What it does not provide is proper uniqueness since it can end up reusing a filehandle after a reboot." [id://f4c9cd498571f5bf976579ad33239408b1324258.camel@kernel.org]

Jeff proposed renaming the flag to EXPORT_OP_UNIQUE_HANDLES as a potential compromise, or alternatively fixing the filesystems to generate unique handles via random i_generation values. He also suggested the matter might be better discussed at LSF:

"At this point I'm debating whether to pursue this further, or just drop this for now until we can come to a better consensus. Maybe we need a discussion about this at LSF?" [id://ccb32c576cc4ebf943d5ec35e3d7ba4ae8892acd.camel@kernel.org]

In the short term, he deferred to Amir Goldstein's existing patch: "In the near term, I'm fine with Amir's patch" [id://f4c9cd498571f5bf976579ad33239408b1324258.camel@kernel.org].

7. Filesystem-Specific Reviews

Several filesystem maintainers provided reviews this week:

  • Jan Kara reviewed the core exportfs patch and ext2/ext4 patches, providing Reviewed-by tags but noting that the comment "required for nfsd export" didn't match the flag name [id://6bajjyslarqrjr2brzyy6bgrmqrdxyhc42q7pfmz42d4y4kjtn@fod6fi4uf6qv]. He also reviewed patches for tmpfs, udf, ocfs2, and isofs.
  • Theodore Ts'o provided Acked-by for ext4 [id://20260116022203.GE19200@macsyma.local].
  • Richard Weinberger acked the jffs2 patch [id://2119146172.135240.1768750563673.JavaMail.zimbra@nod.at].
  • Ryusuke Konishi acked nilfs2 [id://CAKFNMomS-8MMAjy8yuFwzuLBuQQA8r7gPJeJh1ci6RvVc9u4EA@mail.gmail.com].
  • Amir Goldstein clarified that his concerns about FUSE and overlayfs export operations were "purely semantic" — those variants don't implement ->fh_to_dentry() and are not decodable by design [id://CAOQ4uxg+dC1o+6V7Nvxf8UW3H=0OvsGjEe76LNY6q8ZcpGDDJw@mail.gmail.com].

Evolution of the Discussion

The thread evolved through several distinct phases this week:

  1. Early week (Jan 16): David Laight contributed historical context about NFS file handles and generation numbers [id://20260115224018.2988ca25@pumpkin]. Filesystem maintainers provided reviews. Jeff Layton addressed the check_export() question.

  2. Mid-week (Jan 18–19): NeilBrown entered the discussion with a fundamental challenge to the flag's naming and purpose. Christoph Hellwig pushed back strongly, insisting on semantics-based naming. Christian Brauner made his hard line clear regarding pseudo-filesystems. Jeff Layton provided concrete details about cgroupfs behavior — files return empty content when read over NFS [id://9fdf7a8c04861306b453a78233f4bd6004c465f4.camel@kernel.org].

  3. Late week (Jan 20–21): The debate intensified, with tensions rising between NeilBrown and Christoph Hellwig. Christian Brauner expressed frustration at the circularity. NeilBrown provided his detailed taxonomy of issues. Jeff Layton appeared to shift toward NeilBrown's analysis, acknowledging the distinction between stability and uniqueness, and proposed alternative approaches including renaming to EXPORT_OP_UNIQUE_HANDLES or fixing the underlying filesystems.

Final Conclusions

The thread ended without resolution. Key takeaways:

  1. No consensus on naming: The debate over EXPORT_OP_STABLE_HANDLES vs. EXPORT_OP_SUPPORTS_NFS_EXPORT vs. EXPORT_OP_UNIQUE_HANDLES remains open. Christoph Hellwig insists on semantics-based naming; NeilBrown argues for NFS-specific naming; Christian Brauner doesn't care about the name but demands pseudo-filesystems be excluded from export.

  2. Fundamental disagreement on scope: Christoph Hellwig views this as a general file handle quality issue with userspace implications (statx, etc.). NeilBrown and Christian Brauner view it primarily as an nfsd-specific opt-in mechanism for maintainer choice.

  3. Short-term path forward: Jeff Layton indicated he is "fine with Amir's patch" in the near term [id://f4c9cd498571f5bf976579ad33239408b1324258.camel@kernel.org], suggesting a pause on this series until broader consensus can be reached, potentially at LSF.

  4. Filesystem-specific patches largely uncontroversial: The individual filesystem patches received reviews and acks without significant pushback, aside from the overlayfs/FUSE edge cases noted last week.

  5. Open question for NFS client impact: While this series targets nfsd (the server), the implications for NFS clients are significant — preventing silent stale-filehandle errors when clients attempt to access exports from filesystems with unstable or non-unique handles. The check_export() warning Jeff added provides user-space administrators with visibility into which filesystems lack export support.
