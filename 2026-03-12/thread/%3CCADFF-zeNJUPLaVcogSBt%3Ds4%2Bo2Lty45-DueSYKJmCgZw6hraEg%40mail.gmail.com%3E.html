fscache/NFS Re-export Lockup Resolved: Tuning and XFS Switch, Not an FS-Cache Bug

Summary

fscache/NFS Re-export Server Lockup: Issue Resolved via Tuning and Filesystem Switch

Classification: Bug investigation concluded — root cause identified as environmental/tuning issue, not an NFS client or FS-Cache bug.

Background (Prior Weeks)

This thread originated in December 2025 when Mike Owen reported persistent server lockups on Ubuntu 24.04-based NFS re-export servers using fscache to cache data from Isilon and NetApp filers over NFSv3, while serving clients over NFSv4. Symptoms included complete system deadlocks with hung tasks in kcompactd, kjournald2, and nfsd threads, followed by cache corruption (terabytes of cached data lost after reboot).

In January 2026, Mike Snitzer identified a specific upstream fix — Trond Myklebust's commit cce0be6eb497 addressing a deadlock in nfs_release_folio() — that resolved similar deadlocks in LOCALIO-based re-export scenarios. At the time, it was unclear whether Owen's fscache-specific stack trace (blocked in folio_wait_private_2 rather than wait_on_commit) represented the same bug or a distinct fscache-related issue. Snitzer noted:

"OK, my deadlock was blocked in wait_on_commit whereas you've consistently shown folio_wait_private_2 in your stack traces... devil is in the details, but maybe its your use of fscache?"

Mike Owen committed to testing the patch and advocating for distribution backports.

This Week's Activity

On March 9, Mike Owen posted a closing update here, providing the final resolution to this months-long investigation:

"For anyone following this thread, the solution was a broad set of parameter tuning (mkfs/mount options, block device tuning, and VM sysctls) combined with switching from EXT4 to XFS."

He further clarified:

"Some sizeable performance jumps were gained as well across the stack. No issue with FS-Cache here as previously implied."

Key Technical Arguments and Evolution

  1. Initial Hypothesis (Dec 2025 – Jan 2026): The lockups were suspected to be caused by a deadlock in the NFS client's nfs_release_folio() path during memory compaction in an NFS re-export scenario with fscache/cachefiles.

  2. Proposed Fix (Jan 2026): Mike Snitzer identified Trond Myklebust's commit cce0be6eb497 as a potential fix, which transforms nfs_release_folio() to avoid synchronous waits by using asynchronous writeback/commit kicks via a new nfs_wb_folio_reclaim() helper.

  3. Open Question (Jan 2026): Whether Owen's stack trace represented a variant of the known bug or a distinct fscache-related issue requiring David Howells' attention.

  4. Final Resolution (Mar 2026): The root cause was determined to be environmental — a combination of suboptimal filesystem and system tuning rather than an NFS client or FS-Cache bug. Switching from EXT4 to XFS, along with comprehensive parameter tuning across mkfs/mount options, block device settings, and VM sysctls, resolved the lockups entirely.

Final Conclusions

  • The issue was not a bug in the NFS client or FS-Cache subsystem. As Mike Owen concluded: "No issue with FS-Cache here as previously implied."
  • The resolution involved switching from EXT4 to XFS for the cachefiles backing store, combined with broad system parameter tuning.
  • Significant performance improvements were observed alongside the stability fix.
  • This conclusion closes out a months-long investigation that initially pointed toward a kernel deadlock in nfs_release_folio(), demonstrating the importance of thorough environmental diagnostics alongside code-level analysis in complex NFS re-export scenarios.
