NFS Client: REMOVEXATTR Missing GETATTR Causes xfstest Failures with Delegated Attributes

Summary

NFS Client: xfstest Failures with Delegated Attributes — REMOVEXATTR Timestamp Bug Identified

Classification: Bug Fix (NFS Client / Attribute Delegation)

This thread concerns ongoing investigation into xfstest failures that manifest differently depending on whether the delegated_attributes mount option is enabled or disabled. The discussion, originally initiated by Olga Kornievskaia, centers on client-side timestamp handling inconsistencies in NFSv4 attribute delegation scenarios.

Prior Context

Olga Kornievskaia had previously reported xfstest failures that depended on the delegated_attributes setting, raising concerns about correctness of the NFS client's attribute caching and delegation behavior. Jeff Layton took up the investigation this week.

This Week's Activity: Root Cause Analysis for generic/751

On February 26, Jeff Layton followed up on Olga's bug report, cc'ing it to a wider audience for visibility ce051694987719b387563ae2a91ab0f3a32a1e66.camel@kernel.org.

Layton reported progress on two specific xfstest cases:

"generic/407 passes for me with delegated timestamps. Can you look into that one a bit more?"

He then identified what he believes is a client-side bug affecting generic/751:

"I suspect this is a client-side bug too: The REMOVEXATTR compound doesn't contain a GETATTR, so the client doesn't update its timestamps after issuing one. The SETXATTR compound does contain one, so one probably just needs to be added."

Technical Analysis via Wire Capture

Layton provided detailed packet captures comparing NFS wire traffic with and without delegated timestamps:

With delegated timestamps (failing case): After a SETXATTR operation followed by a REMOVEXATTR, the client does not issue any subsequent GETATTR. The compound for REMOVEXATTR lacks a GETATTR operation, meaning the client's cached timestamps are never refreshed after removing an extended attribute.

Without delegated timestamps (passing case): The same sequence shows the client issuing an explicit on-the-wire GETATTR after the REMOVEXATTR:

"It looks like without delegated timestamps, the client does an on-the-wire GETATTR after the REMOVEXATTR"

This confirms that when delegation is disabled, the client naturally refreshes its attribute cache via a separate GETATTR call. When delegation is enabled, the absence of a GETATTR in the REMOVEXATTR compound leaves stale timestamps in the client's cache, causing test failures.

Key Technical Argument

The core issue is an asymmetry in how the NFS client constructs compound RPCs:

  • SETXATTR compounds do include a GETATTR, ensuring timestamps are updated after setting an extended attribute.
  • REMOVEXATTR compounds do not include a GETATTR, leaving timestamps stale when delegated attributes are in use.

Layton's proposed fix is straightforward: add a GETATTR operation to the REMOVEXATTR compound, mirroring the behavior of the SETXATTR path.

Final Conclusions

  1. generic/407: Appears to pass for Layton with delegated timestamps; Olga was asked to investigate further on her side.
  2. generic/751: Identified as a client-side bug where the REMOVEXATTR compound lacks a GETATTR, preventing timestamp updates under delegated attributes. The suggested fix is to add a GETATTR to the REMOVEXATTR compound, consistent with how SETXATTR already operates.
  3. No patch submitted yet: This week's activity represents diagnostic analysis and root cause identification. A fix patch has not been proposed within this window.

Significance for NFS Client Development

This finding highlights a subtle but important correctness issue in the NFS client's handling of extended attribute operations under delegation. The asymmetry between SETXATTR and REMOVEXATTR compound construction could lead to stale metadata on the client side, potentially causing test failures and possibly real-world inconsistencies in attribute-dependent workloads.
