VFS Skepticism Blocks Superblock Splitting Proposal for NFS Remounts

Overview

This week, the discussion around Shyam Prasad N's LSF/MM/BPF topic proposal to "Support to split superblocks during remount" received a decisive and skeptical response from VFS core maintainers. The proposal is classified as a new VFS feature/protocol change, not a bug fix, though it aims to resolve a practical problem affecting NFS and other network filesystems where remount option changes inadvertently propagate across all mounts sharing a superblock.

Background (Bridged from Prior Week)

In the previous week, Shyam Prasad N introduced the topic, explaining that filesystems use sget/sget_fc at mount time to share superblocks based on mount options. The problem is that remount operations are associated with a mount point, not the superblock itself:

"Filesystems today use sget/sget_fc at the time of mount to share superblocks when possible to reuse resources. Often the reuse of superblocks is a function of the mount options supplied." Shyam Prasad N

He proposed allowing the VFS mount API to let filesystems call sget during remount to obtain a new superblock and perform a put_super on the old one. Aleksa Sarai responded with an external reference, which Shyam acknowledged:

"Thanks for sharing this. Will go over the details shared to understand more about what you said." Shyam Prasad N

This Week's Discussion: Strong VFS Resistance

This week, the conversation escalated as two key VFS maintainers — Al Viro and Christian Brauner — expressed profound skepticism about the feasibility of splitting superblocks during remount.

Al Viro's Technical Rebuttal

Al Viro delivered a detailed technical argument against the proposal, emphasizing that fundamental kernel invariants assume that struct inode, struct dentry, and struct mount instances belong to the same superblock for their entire lifetime:

"That's putting it way too mildly. A lot of places rely upon the following: any struct inode instance belongs to the same superblock through the entire lifetime. ->i_sb is assign-once and can be accessed as such." Al Viro

He further noted that relaxing this invariant would require prohibitive engineering effort:

"Exclusion that would required to cope with the possibility of the above being violated would cost far too much, and that's without going into the amount of analysis needed to make sure that things wouldn't break." Al Viro

Viro also asked which specific filesystem Shyam had in mind, prompting further clarification.

Christian Brauner's Agreement

Christian Brauner echoed Viro's concerns:

"I'm very very skeptical about all of this and would really do everything we can do avoid this..." Christian Brauner

Shyam's Clarification on Affected Filesystems

In response to Viro's question, Shyam clarified that the following filesystems use sget* with test functions and can share superblocks: AFS, Btrfs, Ceph, FUSE, GFS2, NFS, UBIFS, and SMB/client. He emphasized that NFS and SMB clients in particular base their superblock-sharing decisions on mount options, which can change during remount:

"If those test functions use mount options to decide whether to share a superblock (at least nfs and smb clients do this), those mount options can change during remount. At this point, the filesystems do not even have visibility into other mounts that share the superblock. Hence they cannot even fail such remounts." Shyam Prasad N

He concluded with a key observation about the unintended side effects:

"Side effect of such remounts is that changes to mount options will apply to all mounts that share the superblock, whether the user intended to do that or not." Shyam Prasad N

Significance for NFS Client Development

This discussion is directly relevant to NFS client development because:

  1. The NFS client uses sget_fc with test functions that consider mount options when deciding whether to share superblocks across mounts.
  2. Remount option changes currently propagate globally to all mounts sharing the same superblock, which can lead to unintended behavior.
  3. Filesystems lack visibility into other mounts sharing a superblock, making it impossible to safely reject problematic remounts.

Final Conclusions

As of this week, the proposal faces strong resistance from VFS core maintainers. Al Viro and Christian Brauner both expressed deep skepticism, citing fundamental kernel invariants that assume superblock membership is immutable for the lifetime of inodes, dentries, and mounts. The consensus among VFS maintainers is that the engineering cost of relaxing these assumptions would be prohibitive.

While the underlying problem — unintended propagation of remount options across shared NFS superblocks — remains valid, the proposed solution of splitting superblocks during remount appears unlikely to gain acceptance in its current form. Future work may need to explore alternative approaches that do not violate core VFS invariants.
