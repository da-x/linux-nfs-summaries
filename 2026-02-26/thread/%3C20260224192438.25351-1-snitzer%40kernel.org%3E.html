NFS/NFSD: nfs4_acl Passthrough RFC Sparks Architectural Debate

Summary

NFS/NFSD: Debate Over nfs4_acl Passthrough for Reexport and Server Compliance

This week saw a significant technical debate regarding Mike Snitzer's RFC PATCH v2 series, which aims to enable NFSv4.1 ACL passthrough for NFSv4 reexport scenarios. The discussion, primarily between Snitzer and NFSD maintainer Chuck Lever, highlighted a clash between immediate functional requirements for specific use cases (Hammerspace) and the broader architectural goals of long-term server compliance and maintainability.

Patch Series Overview

Mike Snitzer submitted an 11-patch series titled "[RFC PATCH v2 00/11] NFS/NFSD: nfs4_acl passthru for NFSv4 reexport" [id://20260224192438.25351-1-snitzer@kernel.org]. The goal was to allow an NFSv4.1 client to interact with an NFSD v4.1 export that reexports an NFSv4.2 filesystem, ensuring full support for NFSv4.1 ACLs (both DACL and SACL).

The series introduced:

  • A new EXPORT_OP_NFSV4_ACL_PASSTHRU flag in exportfs.
  • New .setacl and .getacl operations in the export_operations structure.
  • Mechanisms for NFSD to pass raw ACL payloads directly to the underlying NFSv4 client without decoding or identity mapping, preserving the wire format.

Snitzer noted that the series was marked RFC because he "expect[ed] there will be suggestions for possible NFSD implementation improvements" [id://20260224192438.25351-1-snitzer@kernel.org].

Key Technical Arguments

Chuck Lever's Critique: Architectural Concerns and Compliance

Chuck Lever responded with a detailed critique, arguing that the proposed "raw byte pass-through" design was architecturally flawed and incomplete. He stated:

"Passing pages of raw wire data between client and server is awkward, however, and has resulted in correctness bugs and architectural issues (found during review)." [id://3251bc70-6efd-4cc7-9060-28853f047d53@app.fastmail.com]

Lever argued that the series added "incomplete support for the DACL and SACL attributes," which was "not sufficient for a properly compliant server implementation." He warned that this approach would create "significant technical debt" and likely result in future bug reports, describing it as "considerable effort for the community to have to deal with long-term" [id://feb25fdb-0409-498c-a519-91eba0ddb4f6@kernel.org].

Lever proposed an alternative path: first implementing proper native support for DACL and SACL attributes in NFSD, including decoding and encoding these attributes correctly. He outlined a potential patch sequence involving adding acl_flags to struct nfs4_acl, handling the nfsacl41 wire format, and updating supported/writable attribute masks.

Furthermore, Lever strongly objected to the architectural change of adding .setacl and .getacl operations to export_operations:

"Adding .setacl/.getacl operations to the export_operations structure is simply wrong, architecturally speaking. So NACK to that implementation. All of that needs to be redone once we have agreed on a proper cross-system API." [id://feb25fdb-0409-498c-a519-91eba0ddb4f6@kernel.org]

He suggested an alternative API where NFSD could set "system.nfs4_acl" xattrs on NFS client inodes, though he acknowledged the 64K byte limit as a potential issue to be addressed later.

Mike Snitzer's Defense: Use-Case Specificity and Passthrough Necessity

Snitzer defended the design by emphasizing that the goal was not local filesystem support but strict passthrough for reexport scenarios. He clarified:

"Yes, I'm using the NFSv4 ACL payload that is provided by the NFSv4 client. And then NFSD passes that through to the NFSv4 client it is reexporting." [id://aZ8pDX2LWU5Qgxku@kernel.org]

He argued that the NFSv4 client intentionally acts as a passthrough mechanism to avoid identity mapping or other transformations of the ACL payload. Consequently, any intermediate decoding by NFSD would be counterproductive:

"Given the way the NFSv4 client handles ACLs, any extra NFSD processing into intermediate ACLs actively works against ACL support for the NFSv4 reexport usecase." [id://aZ8pDX2LWU5Qgxku@kernel.org]

Snitzer questioned the relevance of Lever's proposed native DACL/SACL implementation to the reexport case, stating:

"This line of work seems outside the scope of what is needed, so I won't be pursuing it unless you can help me better understand how this stepping stone of NFSD having native support for DACL and SACL will begin to offer more capable ACL pass-through support for NFS reexport." [id://aZ8pDX2LWU5Qgxku@kernel.org]

Final Conclusions and Status

The discussion concluded with Chuck Lever maintaining a firm stance against the current implementation. He framed his request not as a narrow focus on Hammerspace's needs but as a matter of community responsibility:

"This is not about what narrow use case Hammerspace happens to need... I'm asking you to do the right thing and provide a clean and complete implementation of the feature." [id://feb25fdb-0409-498c-a519-91eba0ddb4f6@kernel.org]

Lever reiterated his NACK on the export_operations changes and emphasized that while Snitzer's series served as a useful prototype, it was "not acceptable in the long run" [id://feb25fdb-0409-498c-a519-91eba0ddb4f6@kernel.org].

Status: The patch series remains in RFC status with no consensus reached. The primary blockers are:

  1. Architectural Objection: Lever's NACK on adding .setacl/.getacl to export_operations.
  2. Compliance Concerns: Lever's requirement for complete DACL/SACL support in NFSD before accepting passthrough mechanisms.
  3. API Design: Disagreement on the proper cross-system API for ACL handling between NFSD and the NFS client.

Further development will likely require a compromise on the API design or a separate implementation of native DACL/SACL support in NFSD as a prerequisite.
