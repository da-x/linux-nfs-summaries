IS_ERR_OR_NULL Refactor: Al Viro Condemns Treewide Approach

Summary

This week, the long-running discussion regarding Philipp Hahn's 61-patch series to replace manual IS_ERR() and NULL checks with IS_ERR_OR_NULL() received a definitive and harsh critique from core kernel maintainer Al Viro.

Classification: Code cleanup / refactoring. This is not a protocol change, new feature, or bug fix.

Bridge from Prior Weeks

The series was submitted on March 10th Philipp Hahn. In the weeks following, individual subsystem maintainers reviewed their respective patches.

For the NFS subsystem, the activity was limited to [PATCH 30/61] net/sunrpc, which modified net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/svc_rdma_transport.c. This patch was accepted in March with positive reviews:

  • Jeff Layton provided a Reviewed-by tag.
  • Chuck Lever also added his Reviewed-by.

Subsequent weeks saw mixed reactions from other subsystems: some maintainers accepted the changes for consistency, while others (e.g., Robin Murphy, Alex Williamson) criticized the mechanical nature of the patches and argued that IS_ERR_OR_NULL() often obscures distinct error handling paths or signals poor interface design.

This Week's Activity: Al Viro's Rebuke

On April 9th, Al Viro entered the thread to deliver a strong condemnation of the refactoring effort as a whole. Responding to Hahn's original cover letter, Viro argued that the macro is rarely justified and that the series promotes bad coding habits.

"valid uses of IS_ERR_OR_NULL are rare as hen teeth. Most of those are 'I'm not sure how this function returns an error, let's use that just in case'." Al Viro

Viro concluded with a direct plea to the community:

"Please, do not introduce more of that crap." Al Viro

Key Technical Arguments

The discussion has evolved from technical nitpicking about specific call sites to a broader philosophical disagreement about kernel coding standards:

  1. Consistency vs. Clarity: Proponents (and some early reviewers) argued that standardizing on IS_ERR_OR_NULL() reduces stylistic variance. Critics, including Viro, Murphy, and Williamson, argue that explicit checks are clearer because IS_ERR() (error pointer) and NULL often represent semantically different failure modes that should be handled—or at least acknowledged—separately.
  2. Mechanical Refactoring: The use of Coccinelle to generate patches automatically drew criticism for lacking contextual awareness. As Robin Murphy noted last week, mechanical changes are useful for finding cleanup opportunities but dangerous when applied blindly without human review of the surrounding logic.
  3. Interface Design: A recurring theme among critics is that functions returning both error pointers and NULL indicate a flawed API design. Viro's comment suggests that IS_ERR_OR_NULL() is often used as a band-aid by developers who are unsure of a function's contract, rather than a deliberate design choice.

Final Conclusions

  • NFS Impact: The NFS/sunrpc patch was already accepted in March with dual Reviewed-by tags. No further NFS-specific activity occurred this week.
  • Series Status: While individual patches may still be merged by subsystem maintainers who value consistency, Al Viro's strong opposition signals significant resistance at the core kernel level. His characterization of the macro as "crap" and his warning against its proliferation suggest that future similar refactoring efforts will face heightened scrutiny.
  • No Bug Fixes or Protocol Changes: This thread remains purely about code style and error-checking idioms, with no functional impact on NFS client development or protocol behavior.
