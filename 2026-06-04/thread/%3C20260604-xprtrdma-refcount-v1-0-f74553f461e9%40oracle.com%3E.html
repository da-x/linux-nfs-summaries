xprtrdma: Eight fixes harden connect and reply handling

Status this week: New patch series posted, no replies yet. On Thursday (2026-06-04), Chuck Lever submitted an eight-patch series titled "[PATCH 0/8] Harden xprtrdma connect and reply handling" that lands squarely in the NFS/RDMA client. It is a pure bug-fix effort — no new feature, no protocol change — addressing reference-counting leaks, NULL-pointer dereferences, and Receive-queue draining defects across the xprtrdma connect and reply paths. A list-wide search found no follow-ups or reviews within the week, so this is an open series awaiting discussion.

The headline: an LLM-assisted audit

The most newsworthy detail is in the cover letter. Lever frames the work as "the result of an LLM-assisted review of the xprtrdma code base, addressing eight defects in the xprtrdma connect and reply-handling paths." Three of the eight patches carry explicit Assisted-by: tags — two from Chris Mason (Meta) citing kres:claude-opus-4-7 and kres:openai-gpt-5 — making this one of the more prominent examples yet of AI-assisted kernel bug hunting reaching a real mailing list. The series applies "on top of 7.1-rc4" plus an earlier xprtrdma patch, touching rpc_rdma.c, verbs.c, and xprt_rdma.h for a net +111/-19 lines.

A unifying design thread

Lever's cover letter lays out the conceptual spine of the reply- and send-path fixes: "in this transport, WR-associated resources are reclaimed only by completions." That single invariant explains most of the bugs. On the receive side, every exit from rpcrdma_reply_handler() must either hand off ownership of a reply buffer or recycle it and post a replacement — exits that do neither leak the buffer and let "a misbehaving peer drain the Receive queue (patches 5 and 7)." On the send side, "a sendctx slot is reclaimed only when a Send completion sweeps past it, so a preparation failure that posts no Send WR has to rewind the ring head explicitly instead of waiting for a completion that will never arrive (patch 8)."

The cover letter also flags a deliberate ordering constraint between patches: "the credit clamp moves ahead of every branch that can reach the repost tail (patch 6) before the malformed-reply exits are rerouted through that tail (patch 7)," because the rerouted exits refill against rb_credits, and "the earlier clamp guarantees that value is bounded by re_max_requests rather than taken raw from the wire."

The connect-path fixes (patches 1–4)

Patch 1 — ep kref imbalance on ADDR_CHANGE (Chris Mason). An RDMA_CM_EVENT_ADDR_CHANGE arriving before ESTABLISHED falls through to a rpcrdma_ep_put() "with no matching get," so the reference count underflows during teardown and "rpcrdma_xprt_disconnect() operates on a freed ep." The fix dispatches on the prior connect status via xchg(): pre-ESTABLISHED events wake the connect waiter and return without a put; established ones force a disconnect.

Patch 2 — Initialize re_id before removal registration (Chris Mason). A local admin can "race an NFS/RDMA mount against RDMA device removal." If removal is observed before ep->re_id is assigned, the tracepoint callback dereferences a NULL id and "can crash the kernel with a NULL pointer dereference." The fix stores the rdma_cm_id in ep->re_id immediately before publishing the registration.

Patch 3 — Check frwr_wp_create() during connect. rpcrdma_xprt_connect() ignored the return value of frwr_wp_create(), which builds the singleton Memory Region used to pad unaligned Write chunks. On failure, a later NFS/RDMA read "passes the NULL write-pad MR to encode_rdma_segment(), and dereferences it." Lever notes the trigger is "locally triggerable on an NFS/RDMA client after a connect or reconnect hits a local MR allocation, DMA-map, MR-map, or post-send failure; a remote peer alone cannot force the local MR setup failure." The fix fails the connect as -ENOTCONN.

Patch 4 — Resize reply buffers before reposting receives. Because commit 0e13dd9ea8be made reply objects survive disconnects, their receive buffers keep their original size. If a reconnect target advertises a larger inline send size (e.g., 4096 vs. the default 1024), "a larger inline Reply hits a receive length error and forces another disconnect," and — critically — "the transport flaps without making forward progress for as long as the peer keeps advertising the larger inline size." The fix resizes undersized buffers at repost time rather than freeing all replies, which "would reintroduce the allocation churn that commit 0e13dd9ea8be removed."

The reply- and send-path fixes (patches 5–8)

Patch 5 — bcall rep leak and unbounded peek (Chris Mason). Two defects in rpcrdma_is_bcall(): a zero-byte speculative peek lets "five subsequent __be32 reads ... walk up to 20 bytes past the wire payload into stale regbuf contents," and the short-header arm "returns true without calling rpcrdma_bc_receive_call()," orphaning the DMA-mapped reply buffer. Lever's framing: "This completion reposts nothing, so its slot is reclaimed only when a later forward-channel reply reaches out_post ... absent that traffic the Receive queue drains and the peer's Sends draw RNR NAKs."

Patch 6 — Sanitize the reply credit grant after parsing. The out_norqst exit skipped the credit clamp, so "a peer that sends a well-formed reply carrying an unknown XID and an inflated credit grant therefore drives rep allocation and Receive posting past re_max_requests on every such reply." This is one of the more clearly remote-triggerable issues in the set. The fix moves the clamp to immediately after the credit field is parsed.

Patch 7 — Repost Receive buffers for malformed replies. The bad-version and short-reply exits "never reach that refill: they recycle the rep and return without calling rpcrdma_post_recvs()." As a result, "a remote peer can therefore drain the client's posted Receive queue by sending a sustained stream of replies that are shorter than the fixed transport header or that carry an unrecognized RPC/RDMA version." The fix routes both exits through the shared repost tail, refilling against rb_credits. Lever explains why not to refill less: "a single RNR NAK terminates the connection" because the client connects with rnr_retry_count of zero.

Patch 8 — Return sendctx slot after Send preparation failure. A run of SGE-mapping failures can advance the ring head until it appears full, at which point "rpcrdma_sendctx_get_locked() returns NULL and no Send can be posted to produce the completion needed to recover the ring." The fix adds rpcrdma_sendctx_unget_locked() to rewind rb_sc_head when a just-acquired slot is canceled before ib_post_send().

Trigger model: mostly local, some remote

A recurring theme worth highlighting for readers is the careful distinction Lever and Mason draw between locally- and remotely-triggerable defects. Patches 1, 2, 3, 4, and 8 are repeatedly described as requiring local admin authority (mount setup, device removal, DMA-fault injection), with explicit notes that "a remote peer alone cannot" force them. Patches 5, 6, and 7, by contrast, are the ones a misbehaving or malicious remote peer can exploit to drain the Receive queue or drive unbounded allocation — making them the most security-relevant of the eight.

Bottom line

This is a tightly reasoned, well-documented hardening pass over the xprtrdma client's connect and reply machinery, built around a single resource-reclamation invariant and explicitly produced with AI assistance. The series is fresh (posted 2026-06-04) and had attracted no review or discussion by the close of this week; watch for feedback from the RPC/RDMA maintainers in the coming days.
