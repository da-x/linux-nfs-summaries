Howells defends bvec partial-kmap helper against Hellwig's review in netfs bvecq series

Summary

Howells defends a partial-bvec kmap helper against Hellwig's review

Context

This thread is a single-message reply from David Howells to Christoph Hellwig's review of patch 5/21 in the long-running "netfs: Keep track of folios in a segmented bio_vec[] chain" series (v2, posted May 18). The patch adds a helper, kmap_local_bvec(), that maps a single page out of a multipage bio_vec by offset. Although filed under linux-nfs, the work is really about AFS client directory-cache handling — specifically Howells' plan to replace the folio-based directory cache (folioq) with a segmented bio-vector chain (bvecq).

The thread root is Hellwig's review, posted May 25 (outside this week's window). This week's only in-window activity is Howells' reply on June 4.

Nature of the discussion

This is not a bug fix and not a protocol change — it is review feedback on a new internal helper function supporting a client-side refactor. The exchange is about API naming, code style (bitwise masking vs. division), and an architectural question about iteration strategy.

Key technical arguments

Hellwig's review raised three points:

  1. Naming: the proposed kmap_local_bvec() "is rather confusing for something that does not map the entire bvec, and is an anagram of the existing bvec_kmap_local." He asked it be renamed to something like bvec_kmap_partial.
  2. Style/efficiency: the line computing the mapping was "overly long," and he suggested using bitwise shifts/masking rather than division by PAGE_SIZE, "matching the rest of the bvec code."
  3. Architecture: he asked for the users of the helper, and — the substantive question — "why you're not simply using a bvec_iter at page granularity, which is what other block kmap code does."

Howells' reply addresses each:

  • On naming: he concedes the rename ("Sure...") but notes a tension — "it then differs in pattern from the various kmap_local_*() that we have." He floats an alternative: "I could just give bvec_kmap_local() an offset parameter and use that instead."
  • On style: he's more resistant to the efficiency argument, reasoning that "PAGE_SIZE is a constant power-of-2, so it shouldn't make a difference if the compiler is doing its job properly, but okay" — i.e., he'll comply but believes the compiler already optimizes the division.
  • On users: the helper "is used four times in patch 10 ('afs: Use a bvecq to hold dir content rather than folioq')." He explains the standalone patch was just for convenience when iterating on the series ("it's just been more convenient for it not be in a later patch when flipping backwards and forwards to cut down on recompiling") and offers to merge it into another patch.
  • On iteration strategy (the meat of the exchange): Howells rejects linear bvec_iter traversal because "for the most part I don't want to do linear iteration." He enumerates four access patterns:
    • Read-link / follow-link: symlinks are a single block that "will fit within PAGE_SIZE."
    • Readdir and dir checking: iterates via iov_iter and iterate_bvecq() (though he concedes a bvec_iter loop could step the chain).
    • Lookup: "Follows the hash chain in the directory rather than searching linearly."
    • Editing of local directory copy: "Doesn't do linear iteration, but rather does random access of blocks in the directory. (This is to avoid having to reload the dir from the server when doing mkdir, rename, etc.)"

He closes with a notable architectural aside and an open question: "Note that directories are 2K, not PAGE_SIZE, and, when editing, I may need to modify more than one block in a directory operation and they may share a page (which may be part of a higher order page). Can we drop 32-bit arches yet — or, at least, highem support?"

Evolution and conclusion

The discussion is unresolved as of this week's window — it ends on Howells' reply with no further messages. He has accepted the rename (with a caveat about naming-pattern consistency) and the style change (grudgingly), justified the helper's existence by its four uses in patch 10, and defended the random-access design against Hellwig's suggestion of linear bvec_iter iteration. The trailing question about dropping 32-bit/highmem support is left open and signals a potential follow-up dependency for the series.

Significance for NFS client development

While this particular patch targets AFS rather than NFS, it sits in the shared netfs layer that both filesystems use for segmented bio-vector page tracking — infrastructure directly relevant to how clients manage cached file/directory content in memory. The random-access directory-editing pattern Howells describes (avoiding a server round-trip on mkdir/rename) is a client-performance design choice that the netfs bvecq work underpins.
