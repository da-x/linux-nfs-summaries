RWF_DONTCACHE Writeback (v7): Merge Confirmation

Context from Prior Weeks

This thread traces back to Jeff Layton's Patch Series v7 posted on May 11, which overhauls the RWF_DONTCACHE writeback path in the Linux VFS/mm subsystem. The series — comprising three patches (preserve PG_dropbehind during folio split, per-bdi_writeback dirty tracking via a new WB_DONTCACHE_DIRTY counter, and replacing inline filemap_flush_range() with a background flusher kick) — received broad Reviewed-by tags from Jan Kara, Ritesh Harjani, and David Hildenbrand. Christian Brauner agreed to fold his own simplification of patch 3/3 before merging, saying:

"I'll just fold it. I already have 1157 mails. I don't need more. :D"

The series was expected to land in the v7.2 merge window, with significant performance gains for RWF_DONTCACHE workloads — particularly relevant to NFS clients that rely on this writeback mode for high-throughput, low-latency sequential writes.

This Week's Activity (May 29 – Jun 4)

This week brought a short but important two-message exchange confirming the series' merge status.

Mike Snitzer Follows Up on Merge Status

On June 3, Mike Snitzer — whose device-mapper and block-layer work depends on RWF_DONTCACHE performance — reached out to Christian Brauner to check whether the series had actually been picked up:

"Did you happen to pick this series up from Jeff? Not seeing it in any of your vfs-7.2 topic branches." "I could easily just not be looking in the correct place."

This was a gentle nudge, acknowledging the possibility that he simply hadn't found the right branch. The fact that a downstream consumer like Snitzer needed to ask underscores how critical this series is for real-world DONTCACHE workloads.

Brauner Confirms: In vfs-7.2.writeback

Christian Brauner responded the next morning (June 4) with a brief but definitive confirmation:

"No, you were right to remind me! vfs-7.2.writeback!"

The exclamation and the phrase "you were right to remind me" suggest the series had been sitting in his queue without being pushed to a visible topic branch. The answer — vfs-7.2.writeback — places the series squarely in the v7.2 development cycle, under a dedicated writeback topic branch, confirming it is on track for the upcoming merge window.

Significance for NFS Clients

While this week's exchange was minimal, its significance is substantial. The RWF_DONTCACHE improvements directly benefit NFS client workloads:

  • Tail latency collapse: p99.9 write latency drops from ~8.9 seconds to 28 ms, eliminating the worst-case stalls that plague NFS clients under sequential write pressure.
  • Throughput parity: Dontcache writes now match buffered I/O throughput (4,532 vs 4,616 MB/s in multi-writer benchmarks), removing a longstanding performance gap.
  • Fair scheduling: The targeted dirty tracking (WB_DONTCACHE_DIRTY) prevents dontcache writers from starving buffered writers and vice versa — a critical concern for mixed-workload NFS servers.

The confirmation that the series is in vfs-7.2.writeback means these fixes are now locked into the v7.2 development cycle. No further technical debate or revision appears to be pending; the remaining work is integration and testing during the merge window.

Conclusion

This week's thread activity was a brief but decisive merge confirmation. The RWF_DONTCACHE writeback performance series (v7) — a bug fix / performance optimization for the VFS writeback path with major implications for NFS client I/O — has been confirmed as picked up in Christian Brauner's vfs-7.2.writeback topic branch. No new technical arguments were raised; the discussion concluded with the practical question of "where is it?" answered definitively.
