RDMA: ib_device_attr capability fields int→u32 (v5) — wrong tree, min_t() pushback

Summary

RDMA: Change capability fields in ib_device_attr from int to u32 — v5 posted, but mis-targeted and met with min_t() objections

This week's activity on the thread is brief but pointed: a fifth revision of Erni Sri Satya Vennela's (Microsoft) patch series landed on the list, the author immediately self-corrected a submission error, and Andy Shevchenko (Intel) pushed back hard on one specific coding pattern in the diff. The discussion has not yet reached an agreed-upon v6.

What the patch is about — a type-boundary bug fix with NFS/RDMA fallout

The series is fundamentally a bug fix / type-correctness cleanup, not a new feature or protocol change. As Vennela frames it in the cover letter:

"The capability counter fields in struct ib_device_attr are declared as signed int, but these values are inherently non-negative. Drivers maintain their cached caps as u32 and assign them directly into these int fields; if a cap exceeds INT_MAX the implicit narrowing yields a negative value visible to the IB core."

The fix widens those fields to u32 and then ripples through every consumer — IB core, ULPs (iSER/iSERt, SRP/SRPT, RTRS, iPOIB), NVMe-oF host/target, RDS, SMBdirect, and the NFS/RDMA stack (net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/verbs.c, frwr_ops.c, svc_rdma_transport.c, plus include/linux/sunrpc/svc_rdma.h). That NFS/RDMA surface is what puts this thread in front of linux-nfs readers even though it targets the RDMA tree.

The v5 changelog (from the cover letter) shows how much of the work is defensive hardening around the new u32 values:

"Add U8_MAX clamps in iser_verbs, nvme/host, nvme/target, isert, rds/ib_cm, smbdirect/connect and smbdirect/accept where u32 capability fields were directly narrowed into u8 rdma_conn_param fields without clamping"

"Guard the inline_sge_count calculation in nvmet_rdma_find_get_device() to prevent u32 underflow when both max_sge_rd and max_recv_sge are zero"

"Fix min_t(int,...) in svc_rdma_transport; min_t(u32,...) in ipoib, srpt, nvme/target, rds/ib, rtrs-clt, rtrs-srv, xprtrdma/verbs"

"Fix frwr_ops.c u32 underflow guard (reorder check before subtraction)"

The frwr_ops.c reordering is the most concrete NFS/RDMA bug-fix in the series — it ensures the bounds check happens before a subtraction that could otherwise underflow now that the operands are unsigned.

The mis-targeting: "net-next" vs "rdma-next"

Eight minutes after posting v5, Vennela replied to himself with a correction:

"Sorry for the incorrect prefix in v5 (used net-next instead of rdma-next). Please considerthe next version v6."

So the series as posted is aimed at the wrong tree entirely, and a v6 will be needed regardless of any other feedback. (The typo "considerthe" is left as-is from the original.)

Andy Shevchenko's pushback on min_t()

The substantive technical objection came from Andy Shevchenko on 2026-06-02, who zeroed in on a specific hunk — the rxe driver's max_qp_rd_atom calculation:

"Just no. min_t() usage has to be very well justified. It's a beast which may stub one in the back. As Linus said in most of the cases one wants clamp() rather than min(). Please, redo this and similar pieces."

This is the key open item for v6: the author's approach of sprinkling min_t(u32, ...) (and min_t(int, ...)) across a dozen-plus files to paper over signed/unsigned comparisons will need to be reworked into explicit clamp() calls or other self-documenting bounds checks. Shevchenko's "redo this and similar pieces" signals the objection is series-wide, not limited to rxe.

State of play / what to watch next

  • Classification: bug fix (type-boundary / signedness), with a large mechanical consumer update across the RDMA and NFS/RDMA stacks.
  • Open items for v6: (1) re-target to rdma-next, (2) replace the min_t() pattern with clamp() or equivalent per Shevchenko's request, (3) presumably keep the U8_MAX clamps and the frwr_ops.c underflow guard, which drew no objection.
  • NFS/RDMA relevance: the series touches xprtrdma/verbs.c, xprtrdma/frwr_ops.c, xprtrdma/svc_rdma_transport.c, and svc_rdma.h; the frwr_ops.c underflow reordering is a genuine correctness fix for NFS over RDMA clients, worth tracking through to merge.
