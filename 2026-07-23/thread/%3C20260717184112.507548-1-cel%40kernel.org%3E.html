NFSD header hygiene: Chuck Lever's six-patch NFSv4 tidying series wins a Reviewed-by

Summary

NFSD header hygiene: Chuck Lever's six-patch NFSv4 tidying series wins a Reviewed-by

Type: Server-side (NFSD) cleanup / header-dependency reduction — not an NFS client change, not a protocol change, and not a functional bug fix (aside from deleting two already-dead constants).

Week of 2026-07-17 to 2026-07-23 | Thread: [PATCH 0/6] NFSv4-related tidying

Overview

On Friday, 17 July, nfsd maintainer Chuck Lever posted a six-patch series whose sole purpose is to shrink fs/nfsd/nfsd.h and stop every NFSD translation unit from parsing NFSv4-only types, prototypes, and XDR helpers. The cover letter is explicit about both the timing and the goal:

"Now that Neil's vfs_create() rework has been applied to nfsd-testing, I feel more comfortable sending along more of these clean-up patches. The goal is to reduce dependencies on single large individual headers that pull in items that are irrelevant in many cases."

That "vfs_create() rework" is NeilBrown's 18-patch nfs4_create_file() refactor, which landed in nfsd-testing the same day. Lever waited for that merge before sending more header churn, so the two series would not fight over the same files.

The diffstat is the story: 31 files, 250 insertions, 615 deletions. Two new headers appear (nfserr.h, nfs4ctl.h); xdr4.h and nfsd.h lose large, widely-included blocks. Three days later, Jeff Layton signed off the whole series with a one-line "LGTM" and a Reviewed-by. No other comments, no v2, no objections.

This is housekeeping, not a client-side development thread and not a protocol change. Its value is compile-time isolation: NFSv2/v3 paths no longer ingest NFSv4 constants, XDR helpers, or lifecycle prototypes they never call.

The six patches

1/6 — Move XDR encoding helpers out of xdr4.h

Patch 1 relocates a block of static inline XDR encoding helpers from xdr4.h into the only two files that actually use them: nfs4xdr.c and blocklayoutxdr.c. The helpers depend on the nfserr_resource macro, which in turn pulls in the entire NFS status-code table. Leaving them in a widely-included header therefore forced that dependency on every NFSv4 XDR consumer.

Lever is candid about why they were in the header in the first place, and why he is not converting them to xdrgen now:

"These helper were originally placed in xdr4.h because I thought they would be utilized in the rest of the NFSv4 XDR code, but XDR translation is eventually to be subsumed by xdrgen instead. I'm not converting them now because that is much more churn than this patch is."

Net effect: 139 lines leave xdr4.h; the nfserr macro is no longer a global tax on every include of that header.

2/6 — Carve pre-XDR'ed status codes into nfserr.h

Patch 2 is the largest mechanical change, and the one that actually severs nfsd.h from <linux/nfs4.h>. nfsd.h is included by nearly every NFSD translation unit, so its include of nfs4.h reached all of them — v2 ACL paths, v3 XDR, lockd glue — whether or not they touched NFSv4. That include existed solely for the block of pre-XDR'ed nfserr_* values at the end of the file (several of which, such as nfserr_delay and nfserr_admin_revoked, are built from NFS4ERR_* constants).

Lever's argument is precise:

"nfsd.h is included by nearly every NFSD translation unit, so its include of <linux/nfs4.h> reaches all of them, whether or not they touch NFSv4. That include existed solely for the block of pre-xdr'ed nfserr_* values at the end of the file."

The pre-XDR'ed block and the internal error enum (NFSERR_EOF and friends) move into a new, version-neutral fs/nfsd/nfserr.h, which includes nfs4.h itself. Each translation unit that actually references those codes includes nfserr.h directly. Units that include nfsd.h without using the error block no longer pull in nfs4.h at all. Git records this as copy fs/nfsd/{nfsd.h => nfserr.h} (50%).

3/6 — Delete two unused NFSv4 constants

Patch 3 is the only patch that removes code rather than moving it. Two macros in nfsd.h have no remaining users:

  • COMPOUND_SLACK_SPACE lost its last reference in commit ea8d7720b274 ("nfsd4: remove redundant encode buffer size checking"), which deleted the encode-buffer-space check the macro fed. The comment above it still described that departed check.
  • NFSD_COURTESY_CLIENT_TIMEOUT is likewise unreferenced. The courteous-server code expires clients through the laundromat's reaper and conflict paths, never a fixed 24-hour timer.

Seven lines gone. This is dead-code removal, not a functional fix — the constants were already unused.

4/6 — Relocate NFSv4-internal constants to state.h

Patch 4 moves the remaining live constants — COMPOUND encode-slack sizes and the state-management timeouts — out of nfsd.h and into state.h. They are evaluated only by NFSv4 code (nfs4state.c, nfs4proc.c, nfs4xdr.c), yet they sat in a header that every v2/v3 translation unit had to parse. All three consumers already reach state.h through xdr4.h, so the move adds no new include edges. nfsd.h keeps the NFSv4 prototypes for one more patch; only the pure constants move.

5/6 — Evacuate NFSv4 entry-point prototypes into nfs4ctl.h

Patch 5 is the most carefully argued of the series. Two blocks of NFSv4 lifecycle and control prototypes live in nfsd.h but are referenced by only seven of more than two dozen translation units. The remaining consumers — including the NFSv2 and NFSv3 ACL and XDR paths — parse those declarations for no benefit.

They cannot simply move into an NFSv4-only header such as state.h. Lever explains why:

"The declarations cannot simply move into an NFSv4-only header such as state.h: nfssvc.c, nfsctl.c, vfs.c, and export.c call the routines unconditionally and rely on the CONFIG_NFSD_V4=n stubs, and pulling the heavy state.h types into those lean translation units to obtain a handful of prototypes would trade one form of coupling for a worse one."

The solution is a new, thin fs/nfsd/nfs4ctl.h (83 lines) that carries just the prototypes and the CONFIG_NFSD_V4=n stubs. The seven actual callers include it; everyone else stops seeing them. This is the patch that most clearly illustrates the series' design rule: do not replace a fat ubiquitous header with a different fat header.

6/6 — Move nfsd_v4client() out of nfsd.h

Patch 6 is the capstone. nfsd_v4client() is the last user in nfsd.h of XDR-defined item references. Once the helper moves into nfssvc.c (its only caller), <linux/nfs.h> and the other XDR-specific headers nfsd.h includes have nothing left to provide and can be dropped:

"nfsd_v4client() is the last user in nfsd.h of XDR-defined item references. Once this helper moves out, <linux/nfs.h> and the other XDR-specific headers nfsd.h includes have nothing left to provide, and can be dropped."

Together with patch 2, this is what actually makes nfsd.h version-neutral again.

Community response and status

The discussion did not evolve. There was no technical debate, no requested respin, and no competing design. On Monday, 20 July, Jeff Layton replied to the cover letter:

"LGTM"

"Reviewed-by: Jeff Layton jlayton@kernel.org"

That is the entire review record. No Acked-by from NeilBrown appeared in-thread this week, and Lever has not yet posted an "applied to nfsd-testing" notice in this thread (unlike the same-day merge of NeilBrown's nfs4_create_file() series). As of the end of the window (23 July), the series sits reviewed and ready, waiting for the maintainer — who is also the author — to queue it.

Why this matters (and what it is not)

For NFS client developers, this thread is a no-op: no client code, no wire-protocol change, no interoperability impact. For NFSD, it is the next increment of a longer header-hygiene campaign that Lever has been running in parallel with functional work (the global DRC clean-ups posted the same morning, the lock-owner UAF series already in nfsd-testing). The concrete payoffs are:

  1. Compile isolation. A v2/v3-only translation unit that includes nfsd.h no longer pulls in nfs4.h, XDR helpers, COMPOUND slack constants, or NFSv4 lifecycle prototypes.
  2. Dead-code deletion. COMPOUND_SLACK_SPACE and NFSD_COURTESY_CLIENT_TIMEOUT are gone, along with a stale comment describing a check that commit ea8d7720b274 already removed.
  3. A reusable pattern. New headers (nfserr.h, nfs4ctl.h) are thin and opt-in. Lever explicitly rejected dumping the prototypes into state.h because that would "trade one form of coupling for a worse one."
  4. xdrgen foreshadowing. Patch 1 leaves the encoding helpers as file-local inlines rather than converting them, on the grounds that "XDR translation is eventually to be subsumed by xdrgen." That conversion is called out as future churn, not this series.

Bottom line: a well-motivated, low-risk NFSD cleanup series, reviewed by Jeff Layton, with no outstanding objections. It does not fix a client bug and does not change the protocol; it makes the server tree cheaper to compile and safer to extend. Expect it to follow NeilBrown's create-path refactor into nfsd-testing once Lever queues it.
