NFSD lock-owner UAF fix: merge timeline and stable backport debate

Summary

NFSD lock-owner UAF fix: merge timeline and stable-backport debate

Type: Bug fix — follow-up discussion on the already-reviewed v4 series that fixes a use-after-free (NULL-deref) in NFSD's client-teardown path. This week's activity is not new code, but a process/merge-timeline conversation about how quickly the fix should reach mainline and stable trees.

Bridge from last week

Last week (week ending 2026-07-09), Chuck Lever posted v4 of a nine-patch series fixing a family of use-after-free and NULL-dereference races in NFSD's client-teardown, delegation/lock/open-state revocation, and laundromat-reaping paths. The thread root is patch 1/9, which fixes the original reported bug: __destroy_client() walks the owner hash and calls remove_blocked_locks() without holding a reference on each lock owner, so a concurrent per-net laundromat can free the owner first, producing "a NULL dereference in remove_blocked_locks." The series was reviewed (Jeff Layton granted Reviewed-by on patches 7–9; NeilBrown and Jeff Layton on patch 1/9) and pushed to nfsd-testing.

What happened this week

This week the thread re-opened with a question from outside the core NFS maintainer group. Thorsten Leemhuis, who tracks kernel regressions, prodded for the merge plan on Wednesday, 2026-07-22 his first message. Framing it as a user-reported regression that had also landed in stable trees, he asked:

"What's the plan to merge this change? From the outside it looks like a fix for a user reported regression from this merge window, as Wolfgang afaics blamed commit 516403d4d85607 ('Revert "NFSD: Defer sub-object cleanup in export put callbacks"'), which also made it to various stable trees."

"So shouldn't this change ideally be on it's way to Linus by now? Ideally with a stable tag to ensure quick backporting? Or is it not a good idea to have this in without the follow-up patches from this series?"

Chuck Lever's plan of record

Chuck Lever replied the same day, laying out the concrete path his reply:

"This commit is currently in nfsd-testing. It will matriculate to nfsd-next in a week or so and then be sent to Linus in the initial nfsd-7.3 pull request."

"A stable tag is on this commit, so it will get picked up by stable automation as soon as Linus merges it. That will include 7.2.y, which will still be open at that point."

He also clarified two points of process that explain why the fix had not yet rushed to mainline. First, the Fixes: tag pointed at a v4.16 commit:

"The Fixes tag refers to a commit in v4.16. That makes it technically a fix for a bug that was introduced not in the most recent merge window."

Second, he defended his deliberate pacing of even urgent fixes:

"My general policy is to subject fixes to the same review and CI that is mandated for any other change regardless of urgency, since historically getting such fixes in quickly has been the source of defects in released kernels. Both CI and review latency are subject to forces outside of my control so I leave a lengthy window for both."

Thorsten's concession

Leemhuis acknowledged the explanation in a follow-up his second message, noting he had re-read Chuck's earlier reply to Wolfgang on the stable list and understood better. He still felt the situation was one "where Linus would want the fix to be included in mainline quickly" — citing both avoiding another buggy release and the fact that a user reported it — but chose not to press further:

"Ahh, I see, the change gained a stable tag somewhere along the way, great!"

"Anyway, if Wolfgang is the only one that seems to hit this and is happy with the state of things I guess it's not worth arguing further about."

He footnoted the regression-handling documentation as his basis for wanting faster turnaround.

The original reporter weighs in

The thread closed on Thursday, 2026-07-23 with Wolfgang Walter — the user who originally reported the NULL-deref and is presumably the only one hitting it — confirming he was satisfied with the pace his message:

"I'm very happy having this patch and especially that I got it so fast. If I build a new version of a stable kernel I just rebase this patch on top of it. Git makes carrying such patches very easy."

"So I'm pefectly fine if this goes into stable/lts a bit later."

Conclusions

  • The fix is on track. Patch 1/9 (the lock-owner UAF) is in nfsd-testing, will move to nfsd-next within about a week, and rides the initial nfsd-7.3 pull request to Linus.
  • Stable backport is secured. The commit carries a stable tag, so stable automation will pick it up for 7.2.y (and other open stable trees) as soon as it merges — addressing Leemhuis's core concern about quick backporting.
  • The debate resolved without conflict. Leemhuis's regression-tracker urgency was met with Chuck Lever's principled "same review and CI regardless of urgency" stance, and the original reporter explicitly signed off on the timeline, ending the discussion amicably.

For NFS server developers, the substantive engineering work (the seven UAF/NULL-deref races plus the svc_export leak) was completed last week; this week's thread is a useful record of how a user-reported NFSD regression moves through review, testing, mainline, and stable backport in practice.
