NFS Direct I/O folio debate: author pivots to writing iov_iter_extract_folios; Hellwig endorses series split

Summary

NFS Direct I/O folio debate: author pivots to writing iov_iter_extract_folios; Hellwig endorses the series split

Classification

This week's two messages are a continuation of the review discussion around patch 6/7 ("Optimize direct I/O to use folios for requests") in Pranjal Shrivastava's (Google) seven-patch series "nfs: Modernize Direct I/O path". The series is an architectural refactor of the NFS client O_DIRECT path — migrating from the legacy iov_iter_get_pages_alloc2() API to the modern iov_iter_extract_pages() API and making requests folio-based — with two embedded bug fixes (a data-corruption fix in nfs_direct_extract_pages() and an early-unpin / bytes-accounting defect). It is groundwork for PCI Peer-to-Peer DMA (P2PDMA), not a protocol change.

Bridge from prior weeks

The central dispute, opened when the v2 series was posted on June 16, concerns whether patch 6's assumption — that every page returned by iov_iter_extract_pages() belongs to a valid folio so page_folio() is safe — holds across all memory types. Trond Myklebust, Christoph Hellwig, and Matthew Wilcox pushed back, noting the MM invariant "every page is part of a folio" has important exceptions (slab, vmalloc, iomem).

Over the preceding three weeks the discussion narrowed considerably:

  • Week 1 (June 16–18): Reviewers flagged the page_folio() assumption as a minefield; Hellwig advised to "stick to what the block code does or even better reuse as much as possible of that code."
  • Week 2 (June 19–25): Pranjal accepted the critique but surfaced a wrinkle — the block layer's own iov_iter_extract_bvecs() path also calls page_folio() via get_contig_folio_len() without additional slab/vmalloc checks. Hellwig de-escalated for NFS specifically (GUP-pinned user pages are always valid folios) but flagged a latent kvec-iterator gap: "Not that I'd want to rely on that in the long run."
  • Week 3 (July 3–8): The author and Hellwig converged on a concrete plan. Hellwig endorsed reusing the block layer's get_contig_folio_len() — "Sounds like the best way forward for now" — and suggested splitting the series so the uncontroversial parts could land first. Pranjal committed to that plan: "Ack. I'll reuse get_contig_folio len and move the nfs extractor to iov_iter for v3," and asked whether breaking out Patches 6 & 7 into a standalone folio series would make sense.

This week: two messages (July 9)

The thread's July 9 exchange is a short but consequential coda to that plan. Both messages reply to Pranjal's July 8 commit, and together they refine the v3 design and close out the process question about splitting the series.

July 9 — Pranjal: "On a second thought" — pivot to iov_iter_extract_folios

In his first message of the day, Pranjal revisits his own commitment from the previous day and proposes a stronger, more general solution:

"On a second thought, I guess I'd try taking a stab at writing iov_iter_extract_folios and handle the vmalloc / slab cases for kvecs. I believe that would be the right way to go, unless I'm missing something obvious? Can I take a stab at it in the next version?"

This is a meaningful escalation of scope. Rather than merely reusing get_contig_folio_len() (which — as Pranjal noted earlier this week — "still relies on page_folio()" and therefore inherits the same latent slab/vmalloc fragility), he now proposes to write a new iov_iter_extract_folios() helper in lib/iov_iter.c that explicitly handles the vmalloc and slab cases for kvec iterators. That would directly address the architectural concern Matthew Wilcox raised three weeks ago — that filesystems should not have to reason about folio semantics at all — and would close the kvec gap Hellwig flagged but did not want to "rely on in the long run."

The phrasing is appropriately hedged ("unless I'm missing something obvious? Can I take a stab at it in the next version?"), which is the standard kernel-reviewer etiquette for proposing a scope change after the maintainer has already signaled a narrower path. It also implicitly acknowledges that this is more work than Hellwig's "for now" framing suggested, and asks for explicit permission before committing to it.

July 9 — Hellwig: "Sounds good."

Three minutes later, Hellwig replies with a one-word endorsement of the series-split question Pranjal had raised in his July 8 message:

"Apologies for being unclear. I meant I could post a standalone series that moves NFS Direct I/O to folio, i.e. break out Patches 6 & 7 to another series, if that makes sense?"

"Sounds good."

This is the final process decision on the table. Hellwig's terse "Sounds good" confirms that the folio-related work (Patches 6 & 7, now including the proposed iov_iter_extract_folios() helper) will be broken out into a standalone series, while the rest of the modernization series — the API migration from iov_iter_get_pages_alloc2() to iov_iter_extract_pages(), the two embedded bug fixes (the data-corruption fix in nfs_direct_extract_pages() and the early-unpin / bytes-accounting defect), and the pin-aware infrastructure — can proceed independently. This is exactly the merge-oriented signal Hellwig had hinted at on July 8 ("In doubt if you think you can get a series merged quicker without a later part I'd split it"), now made concrete.

Key technical arguments this week

  1. The get_contig_folio_len() reuse plan is superseded by a stronger proposal. Pranjal's "On a second thought" message pivots from reusing the block layer's helper (which still calls page_folio() internally) to writing a new iov_iter_extract_folios() that explicitly handles the vmalloc and slab cases for kvec iterators. This is a more complete fix for the architectural concern Wilcox raised, and it would benefit all filesystems, not just NFS.

  2. The series split is confirmed. Hellwig's "Sounds good" closes the process question: Patches 6 & 7 (the folio move, now including the new iov_iter_extract_folios() helper) will be posted as a standalone series, unblocking the rest of the modernization work for independent review and merge.

  3. The kvec-iterator gap is now being addressed head-on. Hellwig's earlier caveat — "there is no such thing [a check] for kvec vectors, although no one is using them for direct I/O right now. Not that I'd want to rely on that in the long run" — is directly answered by Pranjal's proposal to "handle the vmalloc / slab cases for kvecs" in the new helper. The latent fragility is being converted into an explicit, tested code path rather than left as a convention.

Evolution of the discussion (three-week arc)

  • Week 1 (June 16–18): Series posted; Trond Myklebust, Hellwig, and Wilcox push back on the page_folio() assumption. Reviewers advise reusing block-layer code.
  • Week 2 (June 19–25): Author accepts the critique but surfaces a wrinkle: the bvec extractor has the same risk. Asks whether iov_iter should export a safe helper. Hellwig de-escalates for NFS specifically (GUP-pinned pages are safe) but flags the kvec gap.
  • Week 3 (July 3–8): Author and Hellwig converge on reusing get_contig_folio_len(). Kernel-side direct I/O confirmed as a real use case (loop, zloop, nvmet, scsi target, nfsd). Series splitting endorsed to unblock merge.
  • Week 4 (July 9, this week): Author escalates the plan to writing a new iov_iter_extract_folios() helper that explicitly handles slab/vmalloc kvec cases. Hellwig confirms the series split ("Sounds good"). The thread is now in a clear "author will post v3 / standalone folio series" state.

Final conclusions and where things stand

The technical and process questions are both resolved. The agreed path forward for v3 is:

  1. A new iov_iter_extract_folios() helper in lib/iov_iter.c that explicitly handles the vmalloc and slab cases for kvec iterators, rather than relying on page_folio() or reusing get_contig_folio_len().
  2. Patches 6 & 7 broken out into a standalone series ("move NFS Direct I/O to folio"), with the rest of the modernization series (API migration + two bug fixes + pin-aware infrastructure) proceeding independently.

No new patches have been posted this week. The expectation is that Pranjal will post:

  • A standalone folio series containing the refactored Patches 6 & 7 plus the new iov_iter_extract_folios() helper.
  • A v3 of the main modernization series with the folio patches removed, updated CC lists (linux-rdma, linux-pci), and full xfstests quick-suite results in the cover letter.

The thread remains open pending those postings and subsequent review.
