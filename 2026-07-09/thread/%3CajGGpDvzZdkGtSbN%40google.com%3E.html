NFS Direct I/O folio debate: Hellwig warns of "longer discussions" as author commits to v3 plan

Summary

NFS Direct I/O folio debate: Hellwig warns of "longer discussions" as author commits to v3 plan

Classification

This week's single in-window message is a continuation of the review discussion around patch 6/7 ("Optimize direct I/O to use folios for requests") in Pranjal Shrivastava's (Google) seven-patch series "nfs: Modernize Direct I/O path". The series is an architectural refactor of the NFS client O_DIRECT path — migrating from the legacy iov_iter_get_pages_alloc2() API to the modern iov_iter_extract_pages() API and making requests folio-based — with two embedded bug fixes (a data-corruption fix in nfs_direct_extract_pages() and an early-unpin / bytes-accounting defect). It is groundwork for PCI Peer-to-Peer DMA (P2PDMA), not a protocol change.

Bridge from prior weeks

The central dispute, opened when the v2 series was posted on June 16, concerns whether patch 6's assumption — that every page returned by iov_iter_extract_pages() belongs to a valid folio so page_folio() is safe — holds across all memory types. Trond Myklebust, Christoph Hellwig, and Matthew Wilcox pushed back, noting the MM invariant "every page is part of a folio" has important exceptions (slab, vmalloc, iomem).

Over the preceding three weeks the discussion narrowed considerably:

  • Week 1 (June 16–18): Reviewers flagged the page_folio() assumption as a minefield; Hellwig advised to "stick to what the block code does or even better reuse as much as possible of that code."
  • Week 2 (June 19–25 / week ending 2026-07-02): Pranjal accepted the critique but surfaced a wrinkle — the block layer's own iov_iter_extract_bvecs() path also calls page_folio() via get_contig_folio_len() without additional slab/vmalloc checks. Hellwig de-escalated for NFS specifically (GUP-pinned user pages are always valid folios) but flagged a latent kvec-iterator gap: "Not that I'd want to rely on that in the long run."
  • Week 3 (July 3–8, earlier this week): The author and Hellwig converged on a concrete plan. Pranjal proposed two paths forward — expose get_contig_folio_len() from the block layer, or move the NFS helper into lib/iov_iter.c — and Hellwig endorsed the first option: "Sounds like the best way forward for now," while also endorsing splitting the series to unblock merge ("In doubt if you think you can get a series merged quicker without a later part I'd split it."). Pranjal then committed to both halves of the resolution: "Ack. I'll reuse get_contig_folio len and move the nfs extractor to iov_iter for v3."

This week's message (July 9)

The single in-window message is Christoph Hellwig's reply to Pranjal's July 8 commitment. The trimmed quote block in the message recaps the prior exchange, ending with Pranjal's question about whether there was "something obvious" he had missed and offering: "Can I take a stab at it in the next version?"

Hellwig's response is brief but carries real weight for how the v3 will be received:

"That does sound good, but it might get you into some longer discussions."

This is a significant nuance. On one hand, Hellwig explicitly endorses the author's plan ("That does sound good"), which keeps the green light on the get_contig_folio_len() reuse and the extractor move into lib/iov_iter.c. On the other hand, the warning about "longer discussions" is a clear signal that the second half of the commitment — moving the NFS-specific extraction helper into the generic iov_iter library — will not be a rubber-stamp change. It touches shared kernel infrastructure used by every filesystem and storage stack (loop, zloop, nvmet, scsi target, nfsd — the very list Hellwig gave earlier this week when confirming that kernel-side direct I/O is a real use case), so expect scrutiny from block, MM, and other filesystem maintainers on exactly how folio semantics should be exposed at the lib/iov_iter.c level.

Key technical arguments (this week)

  1. The author's v3 plan is endorsed in principle. Hellwig's "That does sound good" confirms that reusing get_contig_folio_len() and moving the NFS extractor into iov_iter is the agreed direction, closing out the three-week page_folio() debate for patch 6 as written.

  2. The iov_iter move will be a cross-subsystem discussion. The "longer discussions" caveat flags that hiding folio semantics inside lib/iov_iter.c (the option Matthew Wilcox originally favored — "it is no business of the filesystem what the iov_iter refers to") is a larger API change with broader blast radius. This is the natural next frontier of the debate, and it will likely pull in block, MM, and other fs maintainers.

  3. No new patches this week. The thread remains open pending Pranjal's v3 posting, which per his July 8 commitment will incorporate both the get_contig_folio_len() reuse and the extractor move into iov_iter.

Evolution of the discussion (four-week arc)

  • Week 1 (June 16–18): Series posted; Trond Myklebust, Hellwig, and Wilcox push back on the page_folio() assumption. Reviewers advise reusing block-layer code.
  • Week 2 (June 19 – July 2): Author accepts the critique but surfaces a wrinkle: the bvec extractor has the same risk. Hellwig de-escalates for NFS specifically (GUP-pinned pages are safe) but flags the kvec gap.
  • Week 3 (July 3–8): Author and Hellwig converge on a concrete plan: expose and use get_contig_folio_len(). Kernel-side direct I/O confirmed as a real use case (loop, zloop, nvmet, scsi target, nfsd). Series splitting endorsed to unblock merge. Author commits to reusing the helper and moving the extractor into iov_iter for v3.
  • Week 4 (July 9, this week): Hellwig endorses the plan but warns that the iov_iter move "might get you into some longer discussions," setting expectations for a broader cross-subsystem review of v3.

Final conclusions and where things stand

The central technical dispute over patch 6 is resolved in principle — the agreed path forward is to reuse the block layer's get_contig_folio_len() helper rather than hand-rolling folio grouping or relying on the page_folio() invariant, and to move the NFS-specific extraction logic into lib/iov_iter.c so that filesystems do not have to reason about folio semantics at all.

However, Hellwig's July 9 caveat makes clear that the v3 will face a second round of scrutiny focused on the iov_iter API change itself, since it touches shared infrastructure used by every kernel-side direct-I/O consumer. The "for now" qualifier from earlier in the week and today's "longer discussions" warning together suggest the maintainers are comfortable unblocking the immediate NFS series but want the broader architectural question (how much folio semantics should lib/iov_iter.c expose) to be settled through a wider discussion rather than bundled into this patch.

No new patches have been posted this week. The expectation is that Pranjal will post a v3 incorporating:

  • Refactored patch 6 using get_contig_folio_len().
  • The NFS extractor moved into lib/iov_iter.c (the piece most likely to draw the "longer discussions" Hellwig anticipated).
  • Updated CC lists (linux-rdma, linux-pci) as agreed in a prior week.
  • Full xfstests quick-suite results in the cover letter.

The thread remains open pending that v3 posting and the broader cross-subsystem review it will trigger.
