RDMA ib_device_attr int→u32 (v10): Vennela posts final revision, Shevchenko grants Reviewed-by

Summary

RDMA ib_device_attr int→u32 — v10 posted and reviewed: the long type-correctness saga nears closure

This week at a glance

Two messages landed in this window (both July 9), and together they mark the most consequential beat yet in this ten-revision series. Erni Sri Satya Vennela posted v10 — the revision he promised on July 3 to fold in Andy Shevchenko's INT_MIN/0x80000000 edge-case fix — and Shevchenko replied just under three hours later with a formal Reviewed-by.

"Assuming the functionality is left untouched, from code perspective LGTM, Reviewed-by: Andy Shevchenko andriy.shevchenko@linux.intel.com"

That tag — combined with Stefan Metzmacher's standing Acked-by for the smbdirect hunks carried since v8 — puts the patch in a clean state for rdma-next maintainer pickup. No new technical objections emerged; Shevchenko's reply is approval plus two minor style notes and one open-ended question about further refactoring.

What v10 changes

The core defect has been stable across all ten revisions: capability counter fields in struct ib_device_attr are declared as signed int despite being inherently non-negative, so drivers caching caps as u32 can implicitly narrow values above INT_MAX into negative numbers visible to the IB core. Vennela's cover message frames it plainly:

"Change the signed int capability fields to u32 to match the underlying nature of the data. Also update consumers across the IB core, ULPs, NVMe-oF target, RDS, and NFS/RDMA so the new u32 values are not forced back through signed int or u8 via min()/min_t() or narrowing local variables."

The v10-specific deltas (per the changelog) are small but complete:

  • max_srq converted to u32 — the last of the counter fields still signed. Because its consumer in nvmet-rdma clamps against ib_device.num_comp_vectors, which stays a signed int, that site now uses min_t() rather than plain min():

    "The nvmet-rdma consumer of max_srq clamps it against ib_device.num_comp_vectors, which stays a signed int, so that site uses min_t() instead of min() to handle the signed/unsigned mismatch."

  • rdma_restrack_count() now returns u32, and fill_res_info()'s curr/max locals widened to u64.
  • Commit message refreshed.

The diffstat spans 27 files (129 insertions, 133 deletions) across the IB core (cq.c, nldev.c, restrack.c), hardware drivers (qedr), soft-RoCE (rxe_qp.c, rxe_srq.c), ULPs (ipoib, iser, isert, rtrs, srp, srpt), NVMe-oF host and target, SMB direct, RDS, and the NFS/RDMA transport.

NFS/RDMA client relevance

The NFS-over-RDMA hunks have been stable since v5–v7 and are unchanged in v10; they remain the most operationally significant part of the series for this list:

  • net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/frwr_ops.c — unsigned underflow guard: reorders the check before the subtraction so a device reporting zero max_sge_rd cannot produce an SGE count of UINT_MAX − 1 and break RDMA transport negotiation for NFS clients. This is the one hunk that fixes a real bug rather than a latent type mismatch.
  • net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/svc_rdma_transport.c — min_t(int, …) → plain min() once both operands are unsigned.
  • net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/verbs.c / include/linux/sunrpc/svc_rdma.h — sc_max_send_sges to unsigned int, inline_sge_count to u32, and %d → %u in diagnostic prints.

These are the pieces that keep NFS/RDMA clients from mis-negotiating SGE limits when a hardware device reports large (but legitimate) capability values; they ride along with the broader type migration but would matter on their own if the rest of the series stalled.

Shevchenko's review notes

Beyond the Reviewed-by, Shevchenko raised three small items — none blocking:

  1. Xmas-tree declaration order in ipoib_cm_dev_init():

    "This can keep the reversed xmas tree ordering."

  2. srq_size_set() error handling — a side note he explicitly flags as possibly belonging in a separate patch:

    "Side note (perhaps another patch?)"

    He sketches splitting the kstrtouint() failure from the range check and returning -ERANGE for the latter rather than collapsing both into -EINVAL.

  3. An open question on further cleanup:

    "Have you considered also replacing min_not_zero() or do some refactoring around there?"

None of these alter the Reviewed-by; they are follow-up polish items that Vennela can address in a v11 if he chooses, or defer to a separate patch.

Bridge from prior weeks

As summarized last week and the week before, the thread had entered its final wind-down: v8 was posted June 19, Shevchenko confirmed on July 2 that the INT_MIN scenario "needs to be fixed" (while noting it's unlikely "IRL"), and Vennela promised on July 3 to fold it into a ninth version. This week's v10 is effectively that promised revision — posted under a new thread root (v10 rdma-next) rather than as a reply in the v8 thread, which is why it appears here as a fresh thread summary rather than a continuation of the v8 one.

The series has now run from v1 (June 1) through v10 (July 9) — ten revisions over roughly six weeks — and is a textbook example of a bug fix / type-correctness cleanup rather than a new feature or protocol change. The NFS/RDMA consumers are safe under the unsigned semantics Vennela confirmed in earlier revisions ("Yes I have checked that for all the variables I updated"), and v10 closes the last open item from the review cycle.

Assessment

The thread is at its natural conclusion point: the patch carries a Reviewed-by from the primary reviewer, an Acked-by from the smbdirect maintainer, and no outstanding objections. The remaining forward-looking items are Shevchenko's two style notes and his min_not_zero() question — all optional polish that can be handled in a v11 or a follow-up patch without blocking rdma-next pickup. For NFS/RDMA specifically, the frwr_ops.c underflow guard is the hunk worth remembering: it prevents a real transport-negotiation failure on devices reporting zero max_sge_rd, and it lands as part of this broader type migration.
