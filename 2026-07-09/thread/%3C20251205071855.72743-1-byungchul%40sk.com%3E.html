Summary

DEPT v18: Recover-Events Tracking Deferred to a Follow-Up Series

Classification

This thread is part of Byungchul Park's 42-patch v18 series introducing DEPT (DEPendency Tracker), a new kernel subsystem for detecting deadlock possibilities by tracking wait/event dependencies — complementing Lockdep. This week's activity concerns the module-support portion of the series (patches 33/42–35/42) and is classified as a new feature (kernel infrastructure), not an NFS-specific change or bug fix. Its relevance to NFS client development is indirect but meaningful: DEPT targets exactly the class of wait/event deadlocks that surface in NFS RPC completions, PG_locked page-lock waits, and writeback dependencies — paths Lockdep cannot see.

Prior Context (Bridged Briefly)

The series has been under iterative review since its December 2025 posting. The specific sub-thread here traces Petr Pavlu's (SUSE) January 7 module-loader review of patch 34/42 — covering the extern coding-style nit, type-safety concerns about .dept.event_sites section declarations, a .init-prefix lifetime question, an indirection design question, and whether "used" checks could move to build time via objtool/modpost. Over February, Park accepted most of this feedback (removing extern, exploring direct storage in the section, typed struct dept_event_site_init ** signatures), with Petr providing concrete code examples on Feb 18. In March the discussion shifted to where DEPT should live in the tree, and in May it pivoted to a proposal to encode DEPT's model as an AI code-review prompt. No final merge decision has been reached.

This Week's Activity (July 3–9)

A single message landed this week: Byungchul Park's July 6 reply to Petr Pavlu's January review. It is a quiet but consequential moment — rather than continuing the line-by-line refinement of the module-support patches, Park makes a strategic scoping decision about the series itself.

The Decision: Defer Recover-Events Tracking

Park thanks Petr for the review and announces that he will pull the recover-events-tracking portion out of the initial patchset:

"I appreciate your feedback and it was so helpful, but I decided to defer the part supporting recover events tracking - 33/42 ~ 35/42 in this series - to a follow-up patch later with the feedback applied, as it unnecessarily complicates the initial DEPT patchset and significantly increases the review burden." Byungchul Park

This is a notable shift in approach. Rather than absorbing every piece of feedback into an ever-larger v19 revision, Park is carving the series into two: a leaner initial DEPT patchset that can be reviewed and merged first, with the more intricate recover-events-tracking machinery (patches 33/42 through 35/42) reworked in a follow-up series once Petr's module-loader feedback has been applied.

Key Technical Arguments

  1. Review burden as a first-class concern. Park explicitly frames the deferral around maintainability of the review process itself — "it unnecessarily complicates the initial DEPT patchset and significantly increases the review burden." This is a pragmatic, merge-oriented argument: a smaller, cleaner first series is more likely to land than a maximalist one carrying unresolved design questions.

  2. Feedback will not be lost. The deferral is not a dismissal of Petr's module-loader concerns (type safety, .init lifetime, build-time checking). Park commits to applying that feedback in the follow-up: "to a follow-up patch later with the feedback applied." The recover-events work simply moves out of the critical path for initial acceptance.

  3. Continuity with prior review. This decision directly resolves the open threads from January–February — the type-safety and section-lifetime questions on patch 34/42, which had been the most technically involved part of the module-support discussion. By deferring 33/42–35/42 as a unit, those design debates are parked alongside the code they concern rather than being forced into the initial series.

Evolution of the Discussion

The sub-thread's arc over several months has moved from detailed technical review (Jan–Feb: extern removal, typed signatures, .init prefixing, build-time vs runtime checks) to strategic de-scoping (July: splitting the series). The latest message is a closing move on this particular patch cluster — Park is not proposing new code or answering new questions; he is deciding what will be in the next revision and when.

Significance for NFS Client Development

  • No direct NFS bug fix this week. The activity is purely about structuring the DEPT series toward merge.
  • Indirect benefit to NFS deadlock detection. Recover-events tracking (the deferred 33/42–35/42) concerns how DEPT handles wait/event recovery semantics — precisely the machinery that would be needed to reason about NFS RPC completion and page-lock (PG_locked) / writeback dependencies. Deferring it means the initial DEPT merge will not yet carry full recover-event coverage, so its immediate usefulness for uncovering latent NFS deadlocks is somewhat reduced; that capability arrives in the follow-up series.
  • A leaner first merge is good news for adoption. If the initial DEPT patchset lands sooner (without the complicating recover-events patches), NFS developers gain an earlier foothold for a tool that fuzzer developer Yunseong Kim has already validated as catching real PG_locked/writeback deadlocks invisible to Lockdep.

Conclusion

This week was brief but decisive: Byungchul Park chose to split the DEPT v18 series, deferring the recover-events-tracking patches (33/42–35/42) — the cluster that had drawn Petr Pavlu's most detailed module-loader review — into a follow-up series where the feedback will be applied. The stated rationale is reducing the review burden and simplifying the initial patchset, a pragmatic move aimed at getting a core DEPT merged first. No new code or NFS-specific bug fixes were introduced; the thread remains open pending the reworked follow-up patches and the still-unresolved question of where DEPT will be maintained in the kernel tree.
