nfsd race between client_info_show() and free_client()

Summary

This week's linux-nfs traffic included a concise nfsd bug-fix from TrueNAS: a general protection fault in /proc/fs/nfsd/clients/<id>/info caused by a lock mismatch between the seq_file reader and client teardown. The patch was posted, reviewed by maintainers in the usual b4 flow, and applied to nfsd-testing two days later. There is no protocol change and no NFS client work in this thread; the impact is server-side observability and crash avoidance under concurrent client expiry.

The race

Ameer Hamza reported that client_info_show() walks clp->cl_sessions under clp->cl_lock to print each session's slot counts, while free_client() is "the only unlocked mutator of cl_sessions" and tears the same list down without taking that lock. get_nfsdfs_clp() pins the nfs4_client for the procfs reader, but not its sessions. Because free_client() frees every session before nfsd_client_rmdir(), an in-flight cat of the info file can follow a list_del()'d node whose ->next has already been poisoned:

"a reader can observe a client mid-teardown because get_nfsdfs_clp() pins the nfs4_client but not its sessions: free_client() frees every session before calling nfsd_client_rmdir(), so an in-flight seq_file reader can follow a list_del()'d node whose ->next now holds LIST_POISON1 and take a general protection fault"

The oops is a GPF on a non-canonical address (0xdead00000000014c / LIST_POISON1), with RIP in client_info_show() on a cat of the procfs file, then a kernel panic when panic_on_oops is set. Hamza tagged the regression to 601c8cb349c2 ("nfsd: add session slot count to /proc/fs/nfsd/clients/*/info"), the commit that first made client_info_show() walk the session list.

The reproducer is deliberately small: an NFSv4.1 loopback mount, a flock of readers spinning on cat /proc/fs/nfsd/clients/*/info, and a writer that lses the export and writes expire into each client's ctl file. Hamza was explicit that reader-only load is not enough:

"The "expire" write is what drives free_client(); a reader-only loop does not reproduce this. panic_on_oops is needed because the fault happens while the reader holds cl_lock."

Nicholas Wolff of TrueNAS is credited as the reporter.

The fix

The change is a 12-line adjustment in fs/nfsd/nfs4state.c. Instead of freeing sessions in place, free_client() now detaches cl_sessions onto a local reap list while holding cl_lock, drops the lock, and only then frees the sessions. Hamza framed this as matching existing nfsd teardown idioms rather than inventing a new one:

"Removing entries from cl_sessions under cl_lock matches unhash_session(), and the detach-then-reap shape matches how __destroy_client() reaps cl_delegations. The sessions cannot be freed while cl_lock is held, since free_session() calls nfsd4_del_conns(), which re-acquires it."

That last constraint is the reason the patch does not simply hold cl_lock across free_session(): doing so would deadlock on the nested lock grab in nfsd4_del_conns(). The result is a classic list-reap pattern: serialize unhashing with the procfs walker, then drop the lock before the work that itself needs cl_lock.

Outcome

Two days later, Chuck Lever applied the patch with no requested revisions:

"Applied to nfsd-testing, thanks!"

The applied commit on nfsd-testing is fe521a9894e7ffed2a2eda9383622d4533ad655f. There was no further technical debate in-window: the diagnosis, lock-ordering rationale, and reproducer were accepted as-is. For operators, the practical takeaway is that concurrent expire of NFSv4.1 clients while reading /proc/fs/nfsd/clients/*/info could panic the server after the session-slot-count procfs addition; the reap-under-lock change closes that window without altering the NFSv4.1 wire protocol or client behavior.
