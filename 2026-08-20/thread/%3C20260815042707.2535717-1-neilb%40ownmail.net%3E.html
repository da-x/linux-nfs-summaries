NeilBrown v2 RFC: easy bits of the negative-dentry problem

Summary

This week NeilBrown posts RFC/RFT v2 of an 18-patch series that tries to fix the easy symptoms of the well-known negative-dentry problem — parent d_lockref overflow, and soft lockups walking ->d_children — without shrinking the negative-dentry population itself. The series is a VFS/dcache bug-fix and refactor, not a protocol change and not a new NFS feature. The NFS client is touched only so nfs_clear_verifier_directory() can use a common, later-sleepable child iterator.

Al Viro, back from a month of illness, immediately challenges the cover letter’s claim that d_walk() already schedules. Chuck Lever, five days later, reviews patch 13/18 and concludes it is not merely preparation for the iterator: it is a standalone tmpfs readdir correctness fix. The series remains RFC; nobody has taken it.

Brief bridge from prior weeks

v1 was a 12-patch RFC posted on 3 August. Last week’s discussion of that series concentrated on v1 03/12, which tried to drop mark->lock before fsnotify_recalc_mask(). Jan Kara rejected that approach as violating connector lifetime rules; Amir Goldstein supplied a narrower mark.c split so the children-flag walk never runs under that spinlock. v2 still contains the original “don’t hold a spinlock across recalc” patch as 06/18, so that fsnotify debate has not yet been absorbed.

Miklos Szeredi’s v1 review is credited in the new cover letter. The last three autofs patches exist because Miklos noticed positive_after() could live in common code — “in fact it already was :-)” — once scan_positives() is exported.

What v2 actually tries to fix

Neil is explicit about scope:

"So I didn't try to reduce the number of negative dentries or handle them differently to positive dentries. I just tried to address specific symptoms."

Two symptoms, two strategies.

1. Parent refcount overflow. A directory can have an unbounded number of negative children, so counting a dget through every ->d_parent can overflow ->d_lockref.count. Patch 01/18 stops counting per-child parent refs. Instead the parent is dget()’d when d_children becomes non-empty and dput()’d when it becomes empty. Inactive DCACHE_DENTRY_CURSOR dentries are not on that list, but they already hold a separate parent reference. __d_move() is handled by a conservative dget/dput around the list surgery, and a new dput_dlock() warns rather than dropping a count to zero under the lock. New hlist_move_behind() / hlist_move_before() helpers move cursors without treating them as first children.

Neil calls this “the easiest easy bit. The first patch is all that is needed.”

2. Soft lockups walking d_children. Walking an arbitrarily long sibling list, especially in fsnotify_set_children_dentry_flags(), has been reported to lock up (the LWN piece at https://lwn.net/Articles/1079407/). Neil does not bound the walk. He does teach a common helper to drop the lock, insert an on-stack cursor, and cond_resched(). That “only takes another 11 patches,” because:

"nothing should be holding any other lock while walking ->d_children" "dentries shouldn't be moved around needlessly, else things might get missed." "quite a bit of refactoring in libfs for readdir code."

d_walk() is deliberately left alone: “I thought it was big enough for now.”

He asks for testers who can actually trigger excess negative dentries. His own testing is “the ‘notify’ parts of ltp, but that is all.”

The NFS client change

The NFS-facing work is small and preparatory.

Patch 04/18 splits nfs_clear_verifier_directory(). Today the directory lock covers both nfs_unset_verifier_delegated() on the parent and the walk of d_children. Those are independent needs. The patch drops and retakes the lock between them so a later helper is allowed to schedule in the middle of the walk:

"Subsequent patches will use a helper for walking the d_children list and that helper may drop the lock temporarily. To simplify this transition, split the locked range..."

Patch 05/18 then converts fs/nfs/dir.c (and autofs, ceph, coda, fsnotify) onto a new d_for_each_positive_child / _continue family. The iterators take the parent d_lock, skip non-positive dentries (so cursors, which are never positive, are invisible), and use scoped_guard() so break/goto still drop the lock. _continue requires the start dentry not to move before the parent lock is taken — typically ->i_rwsem.

That is the entire NFS client delta this week: unlock between the parent verifier clear and the child walk, then use the common iterator. The sleepable behaviour itself arrives later, in 14/18. Until every other lock is gone from the walk, nfs_clear_verifier_directory() on a directory with a pathological number of negative children remains a client-side soft-lockup hazard — which is why the fsnotify and autofs lock-dropping patches exist.

Clearing other locks off the walk

The middle of the series is lock-hygiene so patch 14 can might_sleep().

  • 02/18 moves d_lock from caller to callee in autofs positive_after(). Autofs does not support rename, so ->d_parent is stable without the lock.
  • 03/18 drops a redundant rcu_read_lock() in coda_flag_children(): a spinlock already blocks the RCU grace period.
  • 06/18 is the v1 fsnotify patch that Jan Kara is still disputing on the v1 thread: dnotify_recalc_inode_mask() no longer calls fsnotify_recalc_mask() under a spinlock; it returns a flag and the caller recals after dropping the lock. After this, Neil says the only remaining non-d_lock spinlock across a d_children walk is i_lock.
  • 07/18 removes that i_lock hold from fsnotify_set_children_dentry_flags() by d_find_alias() + dget(), walking after the lock is dropped. dput() can sleep; that is now safe because no lock is held across the function.
  • 12/18 drops autofs ->lookup_lock from get_next_positive_subdir() / get_next_positive_dentry(). That lock only protects sbi->expiring and sbi->active_list, neither of which these functions touch. Neil thinks the lock is “a hold-over from a previous locking scheme.”

libfs is then rewritten so scan_positives() is the single walker: drop explicit hlist_node use (08), switch it onto d_for_each_positive_child_continue() (09), allow calling it without a cursor (10), and replace find_positive_dentry() (11). Patch 11 adds lockref_get_not_dead_nested() so a child ref can be taken while holding the parent lock, and it notes that once cursors appear on d_sib, offset lookup must step over them under parent->d_lock because cursors are not RCU-safe.

Same-parent __d_move and the sleepable iterator

Patch 13/18 stops __d_move() from rotating both dentries to the head of d_children when they already share a parent. That rotation “achieves nothing useful,” and once the iterator can drop d_lock it would let a still-present dentry be moved in front of the cursor and skipped. After the change, the only dentries a walk can miss are those moved out of the directory, or those moved in after the walk started — “unavoidable and should not be unexpected.”

Patch 14/18 is the actual lockup fix. d_for_each_positive_child*() now call d_next_sibling_sched(), which plants an on-stack cursor (~330 bytes) and cond_resched()s. Cursors have been NORCU since commit 3df5153c5f12 (“make cursors NORCU”), and nothing takes a reference on a cursor it does not own. __d_init() is split out of __d_alloc() so d_init_cursor() can initialise that stack dentry. The iterators now might_sleep(). d_walk() still does not use the helper.

15/18 then deletes the now-redundant cond_resched() from scan_positives(). 16–18 export it as d_scan_positives(), switch autofs onto it, and stop get_next_positive_dentry() accepting NULL as a start-up sentinel. Autofs still needs no rename protection: it does not support rename.

Al Viro: d_walk cannot schedule under rename_lock

Minutes after the cover letter, Al Viro — “getting through the piles of mail at the moment (sick for a month)” — objects to the claim that most of d_walk() already schedules:

"How the hell could it possibly schedule, when it might very well have grabbed rename_lock? Confused..."

Neil walks it back:

"I should have said: Most d_walk() instances abort when there is a need to schedule() so the caller can do that and retry."

He then admits that really only applies to shrink_dcache_tree(), “which it is a bit of stretch to call ‘most’.” The other d_walk() callers (except path_has_submounts()) run after that shrink, so they should no longer see masses of negative dentries. For path_has_submounts() he sketches walking the mount tree instead of descendants, but is not sure it is worth it:

"As it is only used by autofs which could have lots of mounts that might be slower in the common case. But it isn't clear there is problem here that needs fixing. autofs certainly could collect lots of negative dentries, but does it?"

That is the only VFS-maintainer technical pushback in-window, and it is about a path the series explicitly does not change.

Chuck Lever: patch 13 is a tmpfs readdir bug fix

On 20 August Chuck Lever reviews 13/18 and gives it Reviewed-by. He first confirms the two new guards:

"Both new guards are correct. In the exchange branch target->d_parent has already been set to old_parent, so testing it against dentry->d_parent asks whether the two dentries started in the same directory. In the common branch dentry->d_parent has been set to target's parent, so testing it against old_parent asks the same question."

He wants the commit message tightened: for an ordinary move only dentry->d_sib is relocated; “both dentries” is the exchange path. IS_ROOT() still needs the move because a root dentry has an unhashed d_sib.

The larger point is that the patch already fixes libfs, and should say so, “because it makes the patch worth applying on its own.” offset_readdir() recovers a stale cookie with mas_find_rev() and then walks d_children from that dentry. That is only correct while offset order is the reverse of d_children order. d_alloc() inserts at the head and mtree_alloc_cyclic() issues increasing offsets, so a create-only directory agrees. A same-directory rename breaks it today:

"simple_offset_rename() gives the surviving dentry the offset of the entry it replaced, and d_move() then sends that dentry to the head of d_children. Create a, b and c in that order and the offsets are 3, 4 and 5, with d_children holding c, b, a. After rename("a", "b") the dentry for a carries offset 4 and sits at the head, so d_children holds a, c while offset order still says c, a. Stop a readdir with a reported and c pending, remove c, and the next call resolves the cookie to a and reports a a second time."

With 13/18, a stays put, d_children holds c, a, and the two orders agree again.

"Thus this is a fix for tmpfs readdir, not just preparation for 14/18."

Neil has not replied in-window.

Why this matters for NFS client development

The NFS client does not grow a feature here. It inherits a safer directory-verifier walk.

nfs_clear_verifier_directory() is the NFS-side analogue of the fsnotify children walk: a directory with a huge d_sib list of negative dentries can soft-lock the client while it unsets verifiers. v2’s NFS patches do not yet insert cond_resched() themselves; they put that function onto the common iterator and split its locked regions so the iterator may drop d_lock. The actual schedule point is in VFS, shared with autofs, ceph, coda, fsnotify, and libfs readdir.

Two merge risks remain for that NFS change:

  1. fsnotify patch 06/18 is still the v1 approach Jan Kara rejected. Until the series absorbs Amir’s narrower mark.c split (or Jan takes it separately), the claim that “no spin_lock is held across the walk except d_lock” is contested on the v1 thread.
  2. d_walk() is out of scope, and Viro has already flagged the scheduling story around rename_lock. NFS does not call d_walk() for the verifier clear, but any later attempt to “address the arbitrarily long part” will have to confront that.

Chuck’s tmpfs finding does not change NFS behaviour, but it does mean patch 13 can plausibly land on its own as a directory-cookie correctness fix, which would also make the later sleepable NFS walk safer: a same-directory rename would no longer rotate a still-present child in front of a dropped-lock cursor.

Final conclusions (as of 20 August)

  • Classification: VFS/dcache bug-fix RFC (refcount overflow + soft-lockup walks), with a small NFS client conversion of nfs_clear_verifier_directory() onto a common iterator. Not a protocol change.
  • v2 is incremental on v1: bug fixes, clarification, patch splits, plus three autofs patches that reuse exported d_scan_positives(). No significant design change, and the disputed fsnotify recalc patch is still present.
  • Al Viro has started review and rejected the cover-letter wording about d_walk() scheduling under rename_lock. Neil agrees the “most instances schedule” claim was wrong; shrink_dcache_tree() aborts-and-retries, and path_has_submounts() is the leftover question, possibly uninteresting for autofs.
  • Chuck Lever R-by’s 13/18, asks for a commit-message qualifier, and reclassifies it as a tmpfs offset_readdir duplicate-entry bug fix after same-directory rename.
  • Disposition: still RFC/RFT. Neil wants testers who can reproduce excess negative dentries. No maintainer has queued the series. The NFS client patches are not mergeable until the fsnotify lock story from v1 is resolved and the iterator’s might_sleep() path has more than LTP-notify testing.
