Neil posts v3 RFC: NFS verifier walk drops i_lock, last RFC hoped

Summary

On 25 August NeilBrown posted v3 of his “easy bits of the negative dentry problem” series — now 11 patches, down from v2’s 18 — and asked for review and testing with a clear timetable:

"Here is v3 which I hope will be the last RFC. I'd like to submit this for inclusion in a couple of weeks."

This is still a VFS/dcache bug-fix RFC, not a protocol change and not a new NFS feature. The NFS client delta is small but material: two patches in fs/nfs/dir.c split the verifier walk’s locks and, crucially, drop i_lock before walking ->d_children. That second change is the direct answer to Chuck Lever’s sleep-in-atomic splat on v2. There are no replies in this thread this week. The series sits as RFC/RFT.

Brief bridge from prior weeks

v1 (3 August, 12 patches) and v2 (15 August, 18 patches) attack two symptoms of the LWN negative-dentry write-up without shrinking the negative population: parent d_lockref overflow, and soft lockups walking ->d_children. The NFS-facing work is converting nfs_clear_verifier_directory() onto a common d_for_each_positive_child() iterator that is only allowed to cond_resched() after every other spinlock has been stripped from the walk.

Last week Chuck’s xfstests run against v2 produced BUG: sleeping function called from invalid context at fs/nfs/dir.c:1497 on an NFSv4 setattr/delegation-return path: nfs_clear_verifier_delegated() still held i_lock across the now-might_sleep() iterator. Neil promised d_find_alias() and “another RFC tomorrow.” In parallel he kept the fsnotify update_children split against Amir Goldstein, and rejected Chuck’s claim that the same-parent __d_move change is a complete tmpfs offset_readdir fix. Al Viro’s earlier d_walk/rename_lock objection is still unaddressed in this series.

Classification

Bug-fix RFC (VFS dcache + fsnotify locking + NFS client locking). Two reported symptoms, still:

  1. Parent refcounts can overflow if there are billions of (negative) children.
  2. Walking ->d_children can take arbitrarily long and trigger soft lockups.

Neil is explicit about scope:

"So I didn't try to reduce the number of negative dentries or handle them differently to positive dentries. I just tried to address specific symptoms."

"I haven't addressed the "arbitrarily long" part, but the "soft lockups" only takes another 11 patches. With these in place I would feel more confident addressing the "arbitrarily long" part."

He still does not touch d_walk() or scan_positives().

What changed in v3

The cover letter lists four deltas from v2:

"- drops the changes to libfs.c. They aren't strictly needed here and serve to bloat the series. I still want to pursue those but will do it separately.

  • revises the first fsnotify patch to be more cautious about what I take outside of existing locks
  • fixes the nfs i_lock problem Chuck reported
  • fixes a few things sashiko.dev found"

Dropping libfs is why the series shrank from 18 to 11. The tmpfs/offset-cookie follow-on Chuck offered last week is therefore out of this RFC; Neil is keeping the same-parent __d_move change only as cursor-safety for the sleepable iterator, which is the justification he defended against Chuck’s “tmpfs readdir fix” reading.

The core idea is unchanged:

"The core idea is to use a common helper for all walking of ->d_children, and teach it to drop the lock and schedule when needed, inserting a cursor to keep its place. This simple idea requires:

  • nothing should be holding any other lock while walking ->d_children
  • dentries shouldn't be moved around needlessly, else things might get missed."

He still wants testers who can actually trigger the lockups:

"I would also be very happy if someone who can trigger the soft lockups can try this code and confirm that the locks are gone, and nothing bad happens."

Testing so far is still only “the 'notify' parts of ltp.”

The NFS client patches

Two patches touch fs/nfs/dir.c. Together they are the client-side half of “never walk d_children under a spinlock.”

04/11 — nfs: separate locked regions in nfs_clear_verifier_directory() is the same preparatory split as v2 04/18. nfs_clear_verifier_directory() currently holds the directory lock both for nfs_unset_verifier_delegated() on the parent and for the child walk. Neil drops and retakes the lock between those two needs:

"These are two separate needs and they don't need to both be in the same locked region. Subsequent patches will use a helper for walking the d_children list and that helper may drop the lock temporarily."

05/11 then puts that walk on d_for_each_positive_child(), along with autofs, ceph, coda, and fsnotify. Cursors are never reported (they are never positive). scoped_guard() makes break/goto drop the parent lock.

08/11 — nfs: Don't hold i_lock while walking ->d_children list is the new NFS-visible bug fix, and the reason this RFC exists this week. It is the d_find_alias() remedy Neil promised Chuck on 25 August, matching the fsnotify pattern already in 07/11.

The commit message is the whole locking contract for the client:

"Instead of taking i_lock in nfs_clear_verifier_delegated(), take it only when it is actually needed. This means i_lock isn't held while testing the type S_IFMT of the inode, but that cannot change so it doesn't matter.

In nfs_clear_verifier_file(), simply take i_lock for the whole function while enumerating the aliases.

In nfs_clear_verifier_directory(), use d_find_alias() (which takes i_lock) to find and get the one alias, then dput() it when we are done.

The import result here is that we don't hold any other spinlock when walking the d_children list."

Until this lands, applying the sleepable iterator to v2 makes any SETATTR that returns a directory delegation a might_sleep()-under-i_lock BUG, independent of how many negative dentries exist. With 08/11, the NFS verifier walk is supposed to hold only the parent d_lock (which the iterator itself may drop), so cond_resched() in 11/11 is legal on the chmod/delegation-return path that Chuck hit.

The rest of the series, in service of that walk

01/11 is still the “easiest easy bit”: stop counting every child through ->d_parent, so billions of negative dentries cannot overflow d_lockref.count. The parent is dget()’d when d_children becomes non-empty and dput()’d when it becomes empty; inactive DCACHE_DENTRY_CURSOR children already hold a separate parent reference. dput_dlock() is added to warn rather than decrement to zero. New hlist_move_behind() / hlist_move_before() helpers move cursors without being the first child (so they never adjust the parent refcount on insert).

02/11 and 09/11 strip autofs locks from the walk. positive_after() now takes d_lock itself; autofs does not support rename, so ->d_parent is stable without the caller’s lock. get_next_positive_* no longer holds ->lookup_lock at all:

"The only data structures that are protected by ->lookup_lock are the sbi->expiring and sbi->active_list lists of inodes. Neither of these are accessed in these functions, so the lock cannot be needed. I think the presence of these locks is a hold-over from a previous locking scheme."

03/11 drops a redundant rcu_read_lock() in coda_flag_children() (a spinlock already blocks the RCU grace period).

06/11 is the revised fsnotify patch — the one Neil said is “more cautious about what I take outside of existing locks,” and the design he defended against Amir last week. fsnotify_recalc_mask_noupdate() skips fsnotify_conn_set_children_dentry_flags() and returns whether that walk is still needed. Callers that hold a spinlock (dnotify_recalc_inode_mask() / fcntl_dirnotify() / dnotify_flush()) do the walk after mark->lock is dropped, while fsnotify_group_lock() still pins the connector. dnotify_handle_event() never walks:

"dnotify_handle_event() only ever clears flags (for one-shot events), never sets them."

After 06/11, “the only spin_lock held across the d_children walk other than the parent's d_lock is the inode's i_lock,” which 07/11 then removes with d_find_alias() — the same pattern 08/11 applies to NFS.

10/11 is the same-parent __d_move change, now without the libfs/tmpfs story. Same-directory rename currently rotates survivors to the head of d_children, which is useless and would let a dropped-lock iterator miss a dentry that never left the directory. After this patch:

"the only dentries that d_for_each_positive_child() might miss are those moved out of the directory, or those moved in after the iteration started. These are unavoidable and should not be unexpected."

That is Neil’s original justification, not Chuck’s tmpfs-cookie one. The rename("a","c") ordering hole from last week is simply out of scope here.

11/11 is the payoff: d_for_each_positive_child() calls cond_resched() via an on-stack cursor (~330 bytes), d_init_cursor() / __d_init() split out of __d_alloc(), relying on commit 3df5153c5f12 (“make cursors NORCU”). The iterators now might_sleep(). d_walk() is still not converted:

"Some instances of d_walk() do abort when a resched is needed, others do not. This patch does not help with those."

Why this matters for NFS client development

This is the week the NFS client half of the series becomes a targeted bug fix rather than just lock-splitting preparation.

  • Chuck’s regression is patched, not argued. v2 made nfs_clear_verifier_delegated() sleep under i_lock on the NFSv4 setattr / delegation-return path (chmod_common → nfs_setattr → nfs4_inode_return_delegation → …). 08/11 takes i_lock only where it is needed, uses d_find_alias() for the directory alias, and leaves the child walk with no foreign spinlock. That is the same d_find_alias() + dput() pattern as fsnotify 07/11, applied to the NFS verifier.
  • The original soft-lockup hazard is now supposed to be gone for the NFS walk. Once 04/11, 05/11, 08/11, 10/11, and 11/11 are stacked, nfs_clear_verifier_directory() can drop d_lock, insert a cursor, and schedule while walking a huge d_sib list of negative children — the directory-verifier analogue of fsnotify_set_children_dentry_flags().
  • No NFS protocol change. This is purely client dcache/locking. Change-attribute verifiers are still unset the same way; only the locks around that unset move.
  • What is still missing for the client. Nobody has re-run Chuck’s xfstests against v3 in this thread. Neil’s own testing is still LTP notify. Al Viro’s d_walk concern is still open, and d_walk is still not on the new iterator. The libfs/tmpfs cookie-order follow-on is explicitly deferred, which does not block the NFS walk but does mean v3 will not pick up Chuck’s stacked readdir fix.

Final conclusions (as of 27 August)

  • Classification: VFS/dcache bug-fix RFC/RFT, with an NFS client locking bug-fix (drop i_lock before the sleepable verifier walk) and an fsnotify locking split. Not a protocol change, not a new NFS feature.
  • v3 is the condensed series. Libfs is gone; fsnotify recalc is more conservative (fsnotify_recalc_mask_noupdate() + caller-owned children-flag walk); Chuck’s i_lock splat is addressed in 08/11; sashiko.dev nits are folded in. Neil wants this to be the last RFC and to submit “in a couple of weeks.”
  • NFS client: 04/11 splits the directory lock in nfs_clear_verifier_directory(); 05/11 converts the walk to d_for_each_positive_child(); 08/11 is the important client bug fix — nfs_clear_verifier_delegated() no longer holds i_lock across a might_sleep() walk. That is the SETATTR/delegation-return path Chuck hit last week.
  • Disposition: posted, no review in-thread this week. Still RFC/RFT. Soft-lockup testers and a re-run of Chuck’s xfstests are the explicit next step. d_walk() and the “arbitrarily long” part of the negative-dentry problem remain future work.
