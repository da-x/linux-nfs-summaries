Stepien bows out; Layton eyes userland rpcbind

Summary

Stepien bows out of the hung-task patch; Layton now wants rpcbind in userland

Summary

This week the syzbot-AI patch for nfsd_nl_listener_set_doit() effectively died as a merge candidate, and the durable fix shifted from “make kernel rpcbind asynchronous” to moving most rpcbind registration into userland — a netlink protocol rev with no timeline.

This is still a server-side hung-task / DoS bug-fix thread, not NFS client code. The hang lives in the nfsd generic-netlink control plane (nfsd_nl_listener_set_doit() holding nfsd_mutex while talking to local rpcbind). By Sunday, though, Jeff Layton was describing the long-term work as a protocol change: rev the nfsd netlink API so userland does the rpcbind SET/UNSET. Until that lands, NFSv2/v3 clients keep depending on a kernel path that can stall the entire nfsd admin lock, and syzbot will keep filing hung-task reports.

Brief recap from last week

On 20 August, syzbot posted an AI-generated patch from Slawomir Stepien that blamed sequential request_module() calls under nfsd_mutex and proposed a 128-listener cap plus break-on-create-failure. Layton rejected that root cause the same morning: module upcalls are fast; the stall is synchronous rpcbind registration (a local rpc_ping that can sit for tens of seconds when rpcbind is down). He pointed at his already-posted nfsd-nl-hang v2 series for the short-term cap/timeout/extack work, and invited help on the bigger project: make registration asynchronous and non-fatal.

This week: the author declines, the plan changes

On Sunday, Stepien replied as the human signer of the AI patch. He accepted Layton’s diagnosis without argument:

"Oh, OK. Thanks for clearing that up!"

He then explicitly declined the follow-on that Layton had floated — asynchronous, non-fatal rpcbind so it no longer runs under nfsd_mutex:

"I think I'm not that good to do it correctly and in some reasonable time. I might take a 2nd look at this and understand the root cause (also, based on your comments in your series) but I think in the end it might be too hard for me."

He praised the hardening series (“Nice series, I've learnt something from it!”) and said he would watch for v3. There is no v2 of the syzbot patch, no Fixes: applied, and no Acked-by. The 128-cap / break-on-error AI change remains superseded by Layton’s in-flight series (which uses a 1024 cap, pre-mutex transport-name checks, a 1s local rpcbind timeout, and richer extack).

Monday, Layton did not push Stepien to take the kernel-side async work. After talking to Chuck Lever, he changed the long-term design:

"Fair enough. It's a non-trivial project, and after some discussion with Chuck, I'm leaning toward moving most of the rpcbind registration into userland. That'll mean having to rev the netlink protocol, etc."

That is a larger scope than last week’s “make svc_register() async in the kernel.” Kernel nfsd would still create listeners, but advertising [program, version, proto, port] to local rpcbind — the path NFSv2/v3 clients use for getport — would become a userspace job via an updated nfsd netlink command set. He was also clear that this is not imminent:

"Right now, I don't have a timeline for working on this, so we're probably just going to have to suffer with more syzbot reports until that can come to fruition."

So the near-term posture is live with the hung-task reports. The nfsd-nl-hang series can shorten the rpcbind wait and stop treating registration failure as fatal for NFSv2/v3, but it cannot remove the synchronous call from under nfsd_mutex. Until userland registration exists, a slow or missing rpcbind can still pin the global nfsd lock long enough for the watchdog.

NFS client relevance

No client patches land here, but the stall and the proposed protocol rev are client-visible:

  1. Control-plane stall for every client. While listener_set holds nfsd_mutex, other nfsd netlink and /proc/fs/nfsd writes block — versions, threads, exports, cache flush. New listeners do not come up; admin ops that mounts depend on sit in D state until the hung-task watchdog fires. That hits NFSv4 clients as well as v2/v3, because they still need the listener and a live control plane even though they never talk to rpcbind.
  2. rpcbind is how NFSv2/v3 clients find the server. Last week’s plan (async, non-fatal kernel registration) would have let a v2/v3 listener come up even when rpcbind was down; clients would then fail at mount/getport until the admin noticed printk/extack, rather than wedging nfsd. This week’s plan — userland registration plus a netlink rev — pushes that advertisement out of the kernel entirely. That is better isolation for the mutex, but it means nfs-utils (or equivalent) must SET/UNSET with rpcbind, and older netlink userspace will not know the new commands until the protocol is revved and tools catch up.
  3. No timeline means more client-visible hung tasks. Layton’s closing line is that syzbot reports will continue. Operators of mixed v2/v3/v4 farms should treat a wedged nfsd_nl_listener_set_doit as “rpcbind is slow or dead,” not as a transport-module load storm.

Short-term mitigation remains the nfsd-nl-hang series; expect further syzbot hung-task mail until the userland work exists.
