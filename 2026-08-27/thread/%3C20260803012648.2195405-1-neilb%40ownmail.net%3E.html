Neil keeps the update_children split; Amir’s mark.c patch is not enough

Summary

This week the fsnotify locking argument around patch 03/12 of NeilBrown’s negative-dentry RFC finally gets a direct answer from Brown. The discussion remains a VFS/fsnotify locking bug-fix, not a protocol change and not an NFS client feature. No fs/nfs/ code is touched, but the patch is still the merge blocker for the sleepable d_for_each_positive_child() walk that patch 02 already put under nfs_clear_verifier_directory().

Two mails land in-window. NeilBrown rejects the idea of dropping the children-flag update from fcntl_dirnotify(), insists on keeping his “return update_children and let the caller walk d_children after the spinlock” design, and tells Amir Goldstein that last week’s mark.c patch does not solve the lockup he is actually chasing. Amir replies three days later, but the linux-nfs archive captured only headers — no body — so the week ends without a recorded maintainer verdict.

Brief bridge from prior weeks

Brown’s 12-patch RFC (3 August) tried to drop mark->lock before fsnotify_recalc_mask() so a later cond_resched() can run while walking d_children. Last week Jan Kara rejected that as a violation of the connector/connector->obj lifetime comments, Amir suggested simply deleting the dnotify event-path recalc (it only clears bits), and Brown countered that only fsnotify_conn_set_children_dentry_flags() needs to leave the spinlock. Amir then posted a fs/notify/mark.c patch (73bbfcd1b21a0fc723999aee36af662f36ba4dd2) that Jan ACKed and offered to take immediately, while correcting Amir that dnotify does depend on child dentry flags being installed from fcntl_dirnotify() because it sets FS_EVENT_ON_CHILD. The merge question — fold Amir’s patch into v2, or let Jan take it now — was still open.

Neil: do not skip the add-path walk, but do not walk under a spinlock

On 23 August Neil replies to Amir’s 19 August mail and accepts Jan’s add-path correction:

"Thanks for catching that. I agree we shouldn't skip here, but also we mustn't call fsnotify_conn_set_children_dentry_flags() at this point because a spinlock is held."

That is the whole of his technical position this week. Child dentry flags still have to be installed when a dnotify watch is added; they just cannot be installed while mark->lock is held. He argues the delay is already legal on the add path:

"In fcntl_dirnotify() is it safe to delay that call until after the lock is dropped because fsnotify_group_lock() is still held."

So he wants to keep the part of original patch 03 that Jan had treated as the controversial bit: fsnotify_recalc_mask() and dnotify_recalc_inode_mask() return an update_children flag, and the caller decides when (or whether) to invoke fsnotify_conn_set_children_dentry_flags():

"So I would like to keep that part of my patch - the part where fsnotify_recalc_mask() (and dnotify_recalc_inode_mask()) returns the "update_children" flag and the caller is responsible for calling fsnotify_conn_set_children_dentry_flags()."

He then spells out the three call-site policies the series actually needs:

"Some callers call fsnotify_conn_set_children_dentry_flags() immediately, some wait until a spinlock is dropped, dnotify_handle_event() wouldn't call it at all."

That last clause is the dnotify event-path invariant from last week: the event handler only clears bits, so it never walks d_children. The add path (fcntl_dirnotify()) still walks, but only after mark->lock is gone and while fsnotify_group_lock() pins the connector. Other recalc sites that are not under a spinlock can keep doing the walk immediately.

Amir’s ACKed patch is “a good addition” but not the fix

Brown is polite about Amir’s mark.c change, and does not ask Jan to drop it, but he is explicit that it does not unblock the iterator:

"Your patch might be a good addition but it doesn't block the d_children walk in all cases where a spinlock is held, so it doesn't help my immediate need."

“Immediate need” is the original reason patch 03 exists: until every d_children walk that currently sits under a spinlock is moved off that lock, d_for_each_positive_child() cannot be annotated might_sleep(), and the NFS client’s nfs_clear_verifier_directory() walk of a huge sibling list remains a soft-lockup hazard. Amir’s patch rearranges the recalc so the children-flag walk is no longer the thing dnotify does under mark->lock; it does not, in Brown’s reading, close every other locked walk. That is why he will not replace his update_children return-value API with Amir’s narrower mark.c tweak.

Amir’s 26 August reply is missing from the archive

Amir Goldstein replies on 26 August, In-Reply-To Neil’s 23 August mail. The linux-nfs archive has the Message-ID, From, Date, and Subject, but an empty body. There is no recorded ACK, NAK, or counter-patch from Amir (or from Jan Kara) in this thread this week. The design dispute therefore stands where Neil left it: keep the caller-owned update_children flag; treat Amir’s patch as optional extra, not a substitute.

Why this matters for NFS client development

Still no NFS client code in the diff. The client still cares for the same two reasons as last week.

First, patch 02 already converted nfs_clear_verifier_directory() onto d_for_each_positive_child() and already drops the parent d_lock between nfs_unset_verifier_delegated() on the parent and the same call on every child. That iterator is only allowed to cond_resched() after patches 03–11 have stripped every spinlock from the walk. Neil’s 23 August mail is him refusing to ship a version of patch 03 that leaves any of those walks under a spinlock.

Second, the locking contract is now sharper than last week’s “don’t recalc under mark->lock.” The contract Brown is defending is:

  • mask recalc may stay under mark->lock;
  • the d_children / DCACHE_FSNOTIFY_* walk must not;
  • fcntl_dirnotify() can delay that walk until after the spinlock because fsnotify_group_lock() is still held;
  • dnotify_handle_event() must not walk at all.

Until that split is in the tree, the NFS verifier walk cannot schedule, and a directory with a pathological number of negative children remains a client-side soft lockup.

Final conclusions (as of 27 August)

  • Classification: VFS/fsnotify locking bug-fix inside the negative-dentry RFC. Not a protocol change, not an NFS feature, still a merge blocker for the NFS client iterator conversion.
  • Neil will not drop the update_children return-value API. Callers, not fsnotify_recalc_mask(), own the children-flag walk. fcntl_dirnotify() must still update dentry flags (Jan was right), but only after the spinlock is dropped.
  • Amir’s ACKed mark.c patch is not accepted as a replacement. Brown calls it “a good addition” that “doesn’t help my immediate need” because it does not stop every d_children walk that currently runs under a spinlock.
  • Disposition: no ACK/NAK from Amir or Jan is on the list this week (Amir’s 26 August reply has no archived body). The original patch 03 is still not mergeable as posted; Neil’s revised split is the design he intends to keep.
  • NFS client impact this week: none in code. Practically, v2 of the series still has to land a “never walk children under a spinlock” fsnotify change before nfs_clear_verifier_directory() can sleep.
