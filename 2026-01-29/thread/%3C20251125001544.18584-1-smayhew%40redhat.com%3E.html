Jeff Layton Clarifies Lease Export vs Open Flags for NFS Delegation Control

NFS Delegation Control: Jeff Layton Clarifies Userspace Lease Mechanism vs Open Flags

Summary

Discussion type: New feature / design exploration (NFS client delegation control mechanism)

Overview

This thread, originally initiated by Scott Mayhew in November 2025 with an RFC to add sysfs knobs for disabling NFS delegations, saw a single but significant contribution this week from Jeff Layton. His response directly addresses Trond Myklebust's suggestion that the ongoing work on exporting NFSv4 delegation functionality to userspace via lease mechanisms might obviate the need for additional control interfaces.

Prior Context (Brief)

The debate has revolved around three potential approaches to give administrators and applications fine-grained control over NFS delegations:

  1. Sysfs knobs (Scott Mayhew's proposal): Per-server delegation disable toggles, useful for troubleshooting
  2. Open flags (Trond Myklebust's counter-proposal): Per-application O_NODELEGATION-style flags in open()
  3. Userspace lease export: Exporting delegation management through the VFS lease framework

Last week, Olga Kornievskaia raised POSIX compatibility concerns about NFS-specific open flags and noted the value of both mount options (pre-mount) and sysfs knobs (post-mount troubleshooting). Trond Myklebust responded by pointing to Jeff Layton's work on exporting delegations via leases as a potential alternative path.

This Week's Activity

Jeff Layton Clarifies the Lease Mechanism

On January 27th, Jeff Layton responded to Trond's suggestion, providing important technical clarification about how the userspace lease export mechanism works:

"It's not quite the same thing, as they aren't acquired at open time. Similar to leases, you have to call fcntl(..., F_SETDELEG, ...) to get one. It doesn't seem like it really meshes with the idea of an O_NODELEGATION flag."

This is a critical distinction. The lease-based delegation export operates via fcntl() calls after file opening, not through open() flags at acquisition time. This means:

  • The userspace lease mechanism and open-flag approaches address different points in the lifecycle. An O_NODELEGATION flag would suppress delegation requests at open time, whereas the lease export mechanism allows applications to explicitly request delegations via fcntl(F_SETDELEG) after opening a file.
  • These mechanisms are not mutually exclusive — they serve complementary purposes rather than competing ones.

Layton then endorsed a pragmatic middle ground:

"Being able to disable delegations on a per-mount basis seems like it could be useful though."

This comment signals support for a mount-option-based approach, which would provide system-wide control without the POSIX concerns of custom open flags and without the granularity limitations of sysfs knobs.

Key Technical Arguments

The Lifecycle Mismatch

Layton's contribution clarifies an important technical nuance: the userspace lease export mechanism operates at a different stage than what an O_NODELEGATION flag would address. Delegations are not acquired automatically at open time through this interface — applications must explicitly request them via fcntl(). This means:

  • The lease export mechanism is opt-in for delegation acquisition
  • An O_NODELEGATION flag would be opt-out from automatic delegation requests
  • These are fundamentally different control paradigms

Emerging Consensus on Per-Mount Control

Layton's endorsement of per-mount delegation control suggests a potential convergence point. A mount option approach would:

  • Avoid POSIX compatibility concerns (mount options are filesystem-specific by design)
  • Provide broader scope than per-application open flags
  • Offer more granularity than sysfs knobs that affect all mounts to a server

Status and Outlook

The discussion remains exploratory with no patch under active development. However, Layton's clarification helps separate the userspace lease export work from the delegation control debate, while his endorsement of per-mount control points toward a potentially viable compromise between the sysfs knob and open flag approaches. For NFS client developers, the ability to selectively disable delegations — whether via mount options, sysfs knobs, or application-level controls — remains an important operational requirement driven by real-world support scenarios.
