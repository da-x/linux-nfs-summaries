SUNRPC: wait for in-flight client TLS handshake callback

Summary

Jérémy Jean posted a bug fix for a use-after-free race in the NFS client's RPC-with-TLS handshake path. The one-commit patch, SUNRPC: wait for in-flight client TLS handshake callback, touches only net/sunrpc/xprtsock.c (+11/−1) and is tagged Fixes: 75eb6af7acdf ("SUNRPC: Add a TCP-with-TLS RPC transport class") with Cc: stable@vger.kernel.org. This is not a protocol change and not a new feature: RFC 9289 framing and the TCP-with-TLS transport class stay as they are. The change closes a cancel-versus-completion race that can free the lower transport while xs_tls_handshake_done() is still writing xprt_err.

No replies landed in-window. There is no prior summary for this thread; the discussion begins and, for this week, ends with Jean's posting on 9 August.

The race: cancel loses to completion, waiter drops the callback's reference

The client already pins the lower transport for the handshake callback. Jean restates that contract first:

"xs_tls_handshake_sync() gives xs_tls_handshake_done() a reference to the lower transport before submitting the handshake request. On timeout or signal, the synchronous waiter drops that reference after calling tls_handshake_cancel()."

That drop is the bug. handshake_req_cancel() — the kernel handshake core behind tls_handshake_cancel() — returns false once handshake_complete() has already marked the request complete. False does not mean "the callback never ran." It means "completion won; hp_done() may still be in flight." Jean's diagnosis is the client analogue of the server-side cancel-loser window Chuck Lever closed in May:

"handshake_req_cancel() returns false when handshake_complete() has already marked the request complete. In that case the completion callback can still be running, so dropping the callback-owned reference in the waiter can free the lower transport before xs_tls_handshake_done() stores xprt_err or drops its own reference."

The write-after-free is on the lower rpc_xprt that the TLS connect worker uses as the inner TCP transport. xs_tls_handshake_done() still needs that object to store xprt_err and to drop the reference it was given at submit time. If the waiter has already put the last reference, the callback writes through freed memory. The trigger is ordinary: an NFS-over-TLS mount whose handshake wait is interrupted by a signal, or whose handshake times out, overlapping tlshd delivering the completion downcall. No peer misbehavior is required.

The fix: drain the callback, keep the timeout/signal return

Jean's 11-line change is deliberately small. When tls_handshake_cancel() returns false, the waiter no longer drops the callback-owned reference itself. It waits until xs_tls_handshake_done() signals handshake_done and lets the callback release its own pin:

"If cancellation loses to completion, wait until xs_tls_handshake_done() signals handshake_done and let the callback release its reference. This mirrors the server-side handshake lifetime handling and keeps the timeout or signal return value unchanged."

Two policy choices matter for NFS client developers:

  1. The waiter still reports timeout or -ERESTARTSYS. Completing the handshake in the race does not turn a killed or timed-out xs_tls_handshake_sync() into success. RPC tasks and the mount path keep seeing the interrupt they already observed; only the lifetime of the lower transport is corrected.
  2. The callback is the sole owner of its reference after a lost cancel. That is the same split Lever used on the server: successful cancel means the callback will not fire, so the waiter may drop; a lost cancel means the callback will fire, so the waiter must drain and must not drop.

The Assisted-by: Codex:gpt-5 tag is on the commit. Reviewers will want to treat that as provenance, not as a substitute for reading the cancel-loser path against xs_tls_handshake_done().

How this sits next to earlier RPC-with-TLS lifetime work

This posting is the missing client half of a race the server already fixed.

In May, Chuck Lever and Chris Mason landed a two-patch series on svcsock.c. Patch 1 pinned svc_xprt across the asynchronous TLS handshake callback; patch 2, sunrpc: wait for in-flight TLS handshake callback when cancel loses race, waited for svc_tcp_handshake_done() after a false tls_handshake_cancel(). Jean's subject line and commit message are the client transcription of that second patch: the client already took the pin (the analogue of Mason's svc_xprt_get()), but xs_tls_handshake_sync() still dropped that pin on the cancel-loser path.

The May client series ([PATCH 0/2] Fix a few memory bugs in RPC-with-TLS) is a different lifetime bug. Lever's rpc_hold_client() pin and the lower-rpc_clnt leak fix closed use-after-free of the upper client across connect_worker and a leak of the temporary lower client on a killed XPRT_LOCKED wait. Those patches do not serialize xs_tls_handshake_done() against tls_handshake_cancel(). Jean's change is the remaining handshake-callback race on the lower transport itself.

Same-week TLS work on the list is Chuck Lever's v3 CMSG control-record series, which hardens how xprtsock.c classifies Alert records after handshake. That series is receive-path policy. This patch is handshake-lifetime. They share a file and a transport class; they do not share a race.

What this means for NFS client development

For anyone mounting NFS with xprtsec=tls / mutual TLS:

  • A Ctrl-C during mount, a handshake timeout, or any other interruptible wait that races with tlshd completing the hello can currently free the lower TCP-with-TLS transport while xs_tls_handshake_done() still stores xprt_err. That is a write-after-free on a structure the NFS client uses for every subsequent RPC on that socket, not a benign refcount warning.
  • The patch does not change reconnect policy, does not add a KeyUpdate path, and does not alter how xs_sock_process_cmsg() treats post-handshake control records. Those remain Lever's CMSG series.
  • Because the Fixes: tag points at the original TCP-with-TLS transport class, the race is latent on every LTS kernel that carries RPC-with-TLS. The Cc: stable@vger.kernel.org is therefore load-bearing: once reviewed, this is a candidate for backport independently of the CMSG work.

Status at week close

Posted, not reviewed, not merged. No Acked-by, no Reviewed-by, no nits. Consensus in-window is Jean's alone: when client TLS handshake cancel loses to completion, wait for xs_tls_handshake_done() and let the callback drop its own reference, matching the server-side handshake lifetime handling without changing the timeout or signal return. The NFS client maintainers have not yet spoken.
