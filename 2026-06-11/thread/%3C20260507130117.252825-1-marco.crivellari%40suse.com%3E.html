xprtrdma long-work queue patch lands in linux-next after a ping

Summary

xprtrdma: long delayed work finally merged into linux-next

What this thread is about

This week's activity on the xprtrdma workqueue thread is short but satisfying: a small, already-reviewed performance patch that had been sitting in review since early May finally made it into the merge pipeline. There is no new technical debate here — the substantive argument was settled back in the original submission and Chuck Lever's same-day approval (covered in the prior week's summary). This week is purely about the patch getting shipped.

Background (bridging from last week)

The underlying change, submitted by Marco Crivellari of SUSE Labs as PATCH v2, is a one-line optimization in the NFS/RDMA transport layer (net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/transport.c). It swaps the per-CPU system_long_wq workqueue for the unbound system_dfl_long_wq, so that long-running delayed RPC/RDMA work can be placed by the scheduler across CPUs. As Crivellari framed it at submission time:

"Unbound works could benefit from scheduler task placement, to optimize performance and power consumption. Long work shouldn't stick to a single CPU."

NFS maintainer Chuck Lever signed off immediately:

"Reviewed-by: Chuck Lever chuck.lever@oracle.com"

This week's activity

The thread went quiet for about four weeks after the review approval, then reawakened with a nudge from the author. On 2026-06-05, Crivellari sent a gentle ping back to the original patch:

"Gentle ping."

NFS maintainer Anna Schumaker responded on 2026-06-09, confirming the patch had in fact been queued and was now heading for mainline. In her reply she wrote:

"Thanks for the ping! I've had the patch in my testing branch for a bit, and just pushed it out to my linux-next for the next merge."

Crivellari closed the loop the following morning with a brief thank-you:

"Many thanks Anna!"

Conclusions

  • Classification: This is a performance and power-consumption optimization in the NFS/RDMA client transport — not a new feature, protocol change, or bug fix.
  • Status: The patch is now pushed to Anna Schumaker's linux-next tree for the next merge window, meaning it is on track to land in mainline.
  • Takeaway: A textbook example of a clean, low-risk one-line improvement: technically justified at submission, approved within hours by the maintainer, and merged into the testing pipeline shortly after a routine author ping. The remaining work — actual inclusion in a stable kernel release — is now just a matter of the merge window passing without regression.
