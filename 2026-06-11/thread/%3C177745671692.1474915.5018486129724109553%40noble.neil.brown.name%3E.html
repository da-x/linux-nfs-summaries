NFSv4 mkdir retry fix lands in linux-next ahead of merge window

Summary

NFSv4: mkdir(2) Retry Regression Fix Lands in linux-next

This Week's Development: The Patch Finally Moves

After nearly a month of silence, the long-stalled regression fix for spurious -EEXIST on mkdir(2) retries finally made its way into the kernel pipeline. This week brought three messages that closed out what had been a frustrating period of waiting.

On June 9, regression tracker Thorsten Leemhuis sent a second prod to the NFSv4 maintainers, clearly growing impatient:

"Still no progress afaics. Feels like I'm missing something obvious or like I'm totally of track." Thorsten Leemhuis

He directed the question specifically at Igor Raits and NeilBrown, asking whether they were also waiting for the fix to make progress. The message was a top-posted follow-up to his earlier May 13 inquiry, which had asked whether the patch had "fallen through the cracks."

The answer came the very next day from NFSv4 maintainer Anna Schumaker, who confirmed the patch was already in her linux-next branch:

"The patch is in my linux-next branch here: https://git.linux-nfs.org/?p=anna/linux-nfs.git;a=commit;h=238e9b51aa29f48b6243212a3b75c8e48d6b96fd" "It'll be included when the merge window opens this weekend." Anna Schumaker

Thorsten quickly confirmed he could now see it in the freshly published -next release, with a touch of bemusement at the timing:

"Was a bit confused why I could not see in -next 90 minutes ago (that where I checked yesterday before prodding, too), but it turned up there in the new -next release that was published a few minutes ago. :-D" Thorsten Leemhuis

Background: The Bug and the Fix (Bridged from Prior Weeks)

The patch in question, submitted by Igor Raits on April 29, fixes a regression in the NFSv4 client where mkdir(2) could return spurious -EEXIST after a successful retry following an NFS4ERR_DELAY. The root cause was traced to commit dd862da61e91, which introduced a conditional gate around nfs4_handle_exception(), breaking the retry-state hygiene that other NFSv4 procedures rely on. Specifically, exception.retry remained set after a successful retry, causing the do-while loop in nfs4_proc_mkdir() to issue one extra CREATE RPC for an already-created directory name.

The fix is minimal — 5 lines changed in fs/nfs/nfs4proc.c (2 insertions, 3 deletions) — restoring the unconditional call to nfs4_handle_exception() after each loop iteration, matching the pattern used by nfs4_proc_symlink(), nfs4_proc_link(), and every other similar operation. The patch carries a Reviewed-by from NeilBrown and is tagged for stable backport.

Classification

  • Type: Bug fix (regression repair)
  • Component: NFSv4 client (fs/nfs/nfs4proc.c)
  • Impact: Userspace receives spurious -EEXIST from mkdir(2) after successful retry, causing intermittent failures in concurrent directory-creation workloads.

Conclusion

The thread effectively closes with the patch confirmed in linux-next and queued for inclusion in the next merge window. The fix addresses a real-world reliability issue for NFSv4 clients under high-concurrency workloads, where server-side auth/export cache refresh cycles trigger NFS4ERR_DELAY responses that previously led to phantom -EEXIST errors. The episode also serves as a reminder of how well-intentioned error-handling refactors can inadvertently break subtle retry-state invariants in complex protocol clients.
