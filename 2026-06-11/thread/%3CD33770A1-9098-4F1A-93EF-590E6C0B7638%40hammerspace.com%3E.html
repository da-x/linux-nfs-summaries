knfsd fair scheduling: v3/Flexfiles justification closes the design gap; Lever reframes as DoS surface

Summary

knfsd per-client fair scheduling: the v3 question gets answered, and the stakes get raised

Classification: This remains an RFC-stage feature proposal / protocol-adjacent scheduling change for the NFS server (knfsd/sunrpc), not a traditional client bug fix. The two in-window messages this week are short but consequential: they close the last open design question from last week (why v3 must be covered) and reframe the whole effort as a security-adjacent DoS mitigation rather than merely a fairness nicety.

Bridge from prior weeks

The thread was opened by Benjamin Coddington arguing that a client opening many TCP connections plus a large v4.1 slot table can saturate the whole nfsd thread pool and starve other clients, because "the xprt is the unit of fairness." Chuck Lever welcomed it as "a well-known shortcoming of NFSD" but proposed an alternative for NFSv4.1+: schedule per-session and manage each session's load by raising and lowering its slot table. Last week (prior summary) Coddington delivered the promised benchmark data — a one-connection "interactive" client drops to ~1/(K+1) of the pool when another client drives K connections, for both v3 and v4.1 — and Neil Brown proposed reusing the existing memory-pressure slot-reduction code as the mechanism. The thread ended with Brown's open question: "Can you remind me why v3 is important for you? Is it the lower state-management overhead, or something else?"

This week: the v3 gap closes, and the framing hardens

The two in-window messages are a tight exchange that resolves that open question.

Coddington answers Brown directly, giving the concrete production reason v3 cannot be left out:

"Flexfiles uses v3 in its data plane, and.."

He then dismisses the hope that a protocol tweak could make v4.1 match v3 for this workload:

"I don't think so - the stateless nature of v3 gives it distinct advantages (and disadvantages) over v4 for some use cases. That property can't be added to v4."

And he states the key architectural constraint that ties the fix back to a single shared pool:

"Also, because knfsd doesn't have different resource pools for each version we're going to want to continue to balance the pool for all versions exported."

Lever's reply is short but it shifts the framing of the entire effort. Quoting Coddington's shared-pool point, he elevates the issue from "fairness" to a security posture:

"At a higher altitude, I am treating this issue as a partial DoS surface, so it does indeed have to be addressed for all NFS versions to eliminate that surface."

Why this matters for the design

  • The v3 hole is now a hard requirement, not an open question. Last week's slot-table mechanism (reusing memory-pressure slot reduction) is inherently NFSv4.1+. Coddington's Flexfiles data-plane use of v3, plus Lever's "partial DoS surface" framing, means a complete solution must also cover stateless v3 — almost certainly via the source-address fallback identity that was floated in the opening RFC. The design tension from last week has been resolved in favor of covering all versions.
  • The motivation is now explicitly security-flavored. Lever's "DoS surface" language means the fix isn't just about interactive clients feeling snappier; it's about preventing one (possibly hostile or simply misconfigured) client from denying service to others across every exported version. That raises the bar for a per-version opt-in and pushes toward a default-on, all-versions guarantee.
  • The shared resource pool is the unifying constraint. Coddington's point that knfsd has no per-version pools means whatever mechanism is chosen must balance v3 and v4.x traffic in one queue — reinforcing that a source-address-based identity for v3 (the only identity available to stateless clients) is the natural complement to the v4.1 session/slot-table approach.

Conclusions and open threads

  • Resolved this week: why v3 matters (Flexfiles data plane; statelessness can't be added to v4) and that all versions must be covered (Lever's DoS-surface framing).
  • Still open from last week, now with firmer grounding: the concrete mechanism for v3 (source-address fallback vs. something else), tracking an effective thread maximum under dynamic pool growth and allocation failures, whether to add a shrinker to stop nfsd threads under memory pressure, and how LOCALIO workloads fit into the fairness model.
  • No patch has been posted — the discussion remains at the design/measurement stage, but it has narrowed considerably: a slot-table-based approach for v4.1+ plus an all-versions (v3-inclusive) identity mechanism, justified now as DoS mitigation rather than mere fairness.
