Week ending 2026-06-11

Week ending 2026-06-11 — linux-nfs overview

A busy week on the NFS list, dominated by (a) a high-severity client use-after-free that landed in mainline within the hour, (b) Jeff Layton's two big bugfix series for v7.3 (one of them entirely LLM-audited), and (c) continued churn on the CB_NOTIFY directory-delegation feature as it moves from v5 review into a v6 repost.

Critical bugs & client fixes

  • NFS client 7.1 use-after-free merged. Trond pulled a one-line fix by Dave Jones for a UAF in nfs_write_completion() (dereferencing hdr->req instead of the loop-local req) into Linus's tree within ~80 minutes of the pull request. See the pull thread.
  • New KASAN UAF in xprt_put reported, no reply yet. Shuangpeng Bai posted a well-documented slab-use-after-free in the SunRPC client transport lifecycle (rpc_free_client_work → xprt_put), with an RCU-deferred free on the other side and a reliable QEMU reproducer. Awaiting triage: report.
  • NFSv4 O_RDONLY|O_TRUNC delegation authorization hole. Benjamin Coddington posted a two-line fix (suggested by Trond) so that nfs_open_permission_mask() includes MAY_WRITE whenever O_TRUNC is set; otherwise the client's local open gate under-checks and a non-writer can truncate via a cached write delegation. Tagged for stable: patch.
  • NFSv4 mkdir(2) retry regression fix lands in linux-next. After a month of silence, Thorsten Leemhuis's prods paid off: Anna confirmed the 5-line nfs4_proc_mkdir() exception-state fix is in her -next branch and queued for the merge window. Thread.
  • LOCALIO nfsd_file ref leak spun off to the client tree. The patch that was PATCH 01/19 in Layton's nfsd series (a leaked nfsd_file pinning netns and preventing unmount) was re-routed through Anna/Trond as a standalone client-tree patch after Chuck's routing question. Patch.
  • SUNRPC NULL-check in rpcb_register_inet4/6 reviewed. Weiming Shi's 4-line fix for a kernel oops when rpc_sockaddr2uaddr() returns NULL got a quick Reviewed-by from Layton. Thread.
  • xprtsock callback visibility annotations. Runyu Xiao posted a [PATCH net] adding READ_ONCE/WRITE_ONCE to the shared sk_data_ready / sk_write_space callbacks that xprtsock swaps in and out — closing a stale-snapshot race during transport teardown. Patch.
  • Disputed one-line refcount fix. WenTao Liang's put_dreq() addition in nfs_direct_read_schedule_iovec() drew immediate skepticism from Trond, who couldn't see how the unbalanced io_count actually corrupts anything. Watch for a v2 or impact analysis: thread.
  • nfsrahead SIGBUS on aarch64. A fresh bug report documents a hard fault in the userspace nfsrahead helper when udev fires it against non-NFS block devices — an alignment-sensitive cleanup path that's silent on x86_64. Report.

Major trends

Layton's LLM-audited nfsd bugfix series resends as v2 (21 patches)

The week's biggest single submission: Layton's 21-patch "nfsd: more bugfixes" v2, resending his 19-patch v1 from June 9 with three new fixes, the localio patch dropped (now in the client tree), and LIFO teardown ordering cleaned up. Every patch carries Assisted-by: Claude:claude-opus-4-8 — a striking example of LLM-assisted kernel auditing producing a substantial maintainer-targeted series. Standout bugs include:

  • Grace-period DoS (PATCH 12): clients_still_reclaiming() mixes CLOCK_REALTIME and CLOCK_BOOTTIME, so the deadline is never reached — "any client can hold the server in grace indefinitely."
  • Netlink info leak (PATCH 01): stale opcnt lets the rpc_status dumpit read past an inline array into freed memory.
  • Module-text UAF on rmmod (PATCH 16): nlmsvc_ops published/cleared with plain stores; RCU + module owner fix.
  • OOB read via netlink (PATCH 15): a 16-byte NFSD_A_SOCK_ADDR with AF_INET6 triggers a 12-byte OOB read across three consumers.

CB_NOTIFY directory delegation: v5 deep-dive → v6 repost

The long-running RFC8881bis feature had its most substantive review week yet. Chuck Lever worked through v5 patches 04–10, surfacing a rename TOCTOU bug (a queued A→B event can encode as A→C if the dentry is renamed again before callback encoding), an RCU UAF on fi_deleg_file, and a missing lease-type check — all now folded into the v6 repost posted June 11. Lever immediately resumed with a new XDR enum-leak finding on patch 02/20 (NFS4ERR_FIRST_FREE not in the .x grammar). The series is explicitly targeted at v7.3, and Layton's gating condition stands: no merge until a complete client-side implementation exists to test against. The signed-filehandle fix in patch 20/20 (MAC appended to child filehandles on NFSEXP_SIGN_FH exports) is client-critical. Earlier this week Lever also punted the series to v7.3 and flagged that the NOTIFY4_* flags in the shared XDR header are a client prerequisite.

svcrdma contiguous-page regression: two failed fixes, revert lands

The 7.1 WRITE-throughput regression (73.9 → 30.3 GiB/s on Hammerspace's 400 Gb/s test rig) from commit 18755b8c2f24 was not fixed by either of Chuck Lever's two attempts this week: GFP_NOWAIT (tested in 42 minutes, no effect) and capping at PAGE_ALLOC_COSTLY_ORDER (made things worse — the cost just moved to folios_put_refs() on the release path). Lever concluded "the current folio allocator is not up to the task" and queued a revert in nfsd-next, with backport to 7.1.y after the v7.2 merge window closes.

NFSv4.1 slot-growth clamp RFC: Reviewed-by from both Brown and Layton

Chuck Lever's 5-patch RFC caps nfsd4_sequence()'s 20% on-demand slot growth at the sunrpc-reported thread ceiling, removing the perverse incentive where the busiest client gets a still-larger slot table. This is the server-side amplifier fix that complements Ben Coddington's per-client fair-queueing RFC, which this week got its v3 question answered (Flexfiles uses v3 in the data plane) and was reframed by Lever as "a partial DoS surface."

mTLS client identity: netns model wins, keyring unification rejected

The RFC for named per-mount mTLS identities advanced decisively: Sagi Grimberg endorsed network-namespace isolation ("I agree with network namespace isolation"), and Hannes Reinecke shot down the idea of unifying .nfs and .nvme keyrings (different key types, separate pending NVMe work). The security goal — a tlshd in one netns cannot read another's credentials — is now firmly established.

TLS session tags for NFSD: v1 posted, active review

Chuck Lever's 9-patch series delivers opaque string tags from tlshd to NFSD for certificate-policy-driven export authorization. Patch 1 (a privilege-check fix on the handshake DONE command) already has Reviewed-by from both Layton and Reinecke. The "tagset" data structure debate (sorted-array vs rbtree for intersection) is ongoing, and a v2 rebase is expected.

Other notable activity

  • netfs bvecq v3 — David Howells' 22-patch series replacing folio_queue with a segmented bio_vec[] chain, rebased to -rc7 and addressing prior review feedback. Includes a fscache write-streaming race fix and a significant cachefiles performance improvement (CACHEFILES_CONTENT_ALL).
  • nfsstat → netlink v3 — Layton's 4-patch series converting server-side statistics from procfs scraping to per-netns netlink, with a deliberate behavior change to make /proc/net/rpc/nfsd namespace-aware.
  • FMODE_NOWAIT for O_DIRECT reads (v2) — Dylan Yudaken's 2-patch series letting io_uring issue NFS O_DIRECT reads inline rather than punting to a background worker; scoped to read-only files initially.
  • Flexfiles FF_FLAGS_NO_IO_THRU_MDS fix — Mike Snitzer's series got its first external reply from Tigran Mkrtchyan at DESY, signaling HPC community interest.
  • FS_USERNS_DELEGATABLE still missing — Layton pinged twice about the containerized NFS mount fix that was "applied" in February but never reached a release; still needs bit renumbering and a merge-window slot.
  • NFSv3 nseconds validation debate — Synology's patch to reject out-of-range nseconds in the NFSv3 decoder was challenged by Lever: the error should be NFS3ERR_INVAL from the proc function, not GARBAGE_ARGS from XDR decode (which would also break the sattrguard3.obj_ctime comparison case). A v2 is expected.
  • nfs-utils thread-count fix committed — Scott Mayhew's patch handling -EIO when writing 0 to /proc/fs/nfsd/threads with no active listeners was committed by Steve Dickson.
  • NFSv4.2 latency question — Dan Shelton asked how NFSv4.2 read/write latency compares to iSCSI and P9FS on Linux; no developer reply yet in the window.
