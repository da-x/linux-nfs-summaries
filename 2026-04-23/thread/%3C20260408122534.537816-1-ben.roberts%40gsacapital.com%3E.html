pNFS: Deadlock Fix Withdrawn After Maintainer Review

Summary

This week concluded the discussion around a proposed bug fix for a deadlock in the Parallel NFS (pNFS) client's layout return path, specifically within pnfs_send_layoutreturn(). The patch, originally submitted by Ben Roberts of GSA Capital to address production deadlocks on RHEL 9 systems, was ultimately withdrawn after maintainers identified flaws in the underlying diagnosis relative to upstream code.

Evolution of the Discussion

Following last week's skepticism from maintainer Benjamin Coddington regarding whether the issue was reproduced on upstream kernels, Roberts returned this week with a detailed technical analysis and stack traces. He argued that while he could not easily reproduce the issue on upstream kernels due to production constraints, the evidence from his EL9 systems was compelling:

"The best evidence I have that this patch is needed, is that we were seeing this deadlock occur repeatedly under high loads and memory pressure last year before applying this patch locally. It was rolled out to all production systems in early Jan and we have not seen a single reoccurrence since." Ben Roberts

Roberts presented an LLM-assisted analysis suggesting that a memory allocation failure (kzalloc) in pnfs_send_layoutreturn() left the NFS_LAYOUT_RETURN_REQUESTED flag set, causing a second process to attempt operations on a corrupted layout state and hang indefinitely in rpc_wait_bit_killable().

Technical Rebuttal and Withdrawal

Benjamin Coddington reviewed the analysis but remained unconvinced that the race condition existed in upstream code. He pointed out that the flag Roberts claimed was left set is actually cleared earlier in the call chain:

"The kzalloc failure is definitely a rarely-used/tested path... but from what I can see it looks like every call to pnfs_send_layoutreturn() first calls pnfs_prepare_layoutreturn(), which already clears NFS_LAYOUT_RETURN_REQUESTED. I don't see how you can end up with another proccess seeing the flag." Benjamin Coddington

Accepting this feedback, Roberts withdrew the patch:

"Understood, thank you for the feedback here. I withdraw the patch request and will take this back to the drawing board." Ben Roberts

Final Conclusion

NFS maintainer Trond Myklebust confirmed the decision to reject the patch, noting that even if applied, the fix would be ineffective due to existing logic in the kernel:

"I had first applied this patch, however when looking more closely, it seems that it will also be defeated by the by the loop in pnfs_clear_layoutreturn_info() that checks if there are any layouts that need returning, and resets NFS_LAYOUT_RETURN_REQUESTED." Trond Myklebust

The thread highlights the complexities of diagnosing pNFS race conditions on distribution-specific kernels (like RHEL 9) that may diverge from upstream behavior. Roberts noted that his systems already include most of Trond's recent layout return improvements, suggesting the root cause may lie elsewhere or in a more subtle interaction not captured by the initial patch.
