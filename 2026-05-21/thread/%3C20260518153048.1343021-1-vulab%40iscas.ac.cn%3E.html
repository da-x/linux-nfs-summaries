nfsd UAF fix in nfsd4_lock() rejected: stateid already has two references

Summary

A Use-After-Free Claim That Didn't Survive Scrutiny

This week's thread is a short, sharp exchange about a proposed bug fix in the NFS server (nfsd) lock-handling code. It begins with a patch from Wentao Liang and ends — within hours — with Jeff Layton rejecting it on refcounting grounds. The discussion is squarely about nfsd internals rather than the client, but it touches the same lockowner/stateid machinery that clients interact with over NFSv4.

The proposed fix

Wentao Liang opened the thread on 2026-05-18 with a one-file patch to fs/nfsd/nfs4state.c (6 insertions, 1 deletion). His diagnosis:

"When processing a new lock request that fails with an error, nfsd4_lock() may call release_lock_stateid() on a newly created lock stateid. release_lock_stateid() can free the stateid via nfs4_put_stid() if it is the last reference, but the caller subsequently accesses lock_stp->st_mutex and lock_stp->st_stid, leading to a use-after-free."

His remedy was a reordering: move mutex_unlock() ahead of release_lock_stateid() and jump over the remaining lock_stp cleanup after the release, "since release_lock_stateid() already handles the final put. This ensures no further access to lock_stp after it may be freed." The patch carried a Fixes: tag pointing at commit 5db1c03feb00 ("nfsd: clean up lockowner refcounting when finding them") and was Cc'd to stable, signaling that the author believed this was a real regression worth backporting.

The rebuttal

Jeff Layton replied roughly an hour later with a flat rejection:

"This patch is not correct. It's not terribly obvious, but at the point of the start of the delta, lock_stp has a refcount of 2, so this problem can't happen."

He walked through the reference-count arithmetic that defeats the UAF scenario:

"1. nfs4_alloc_stid() — sets sc_count = 1 2. init_lock_stateid() line 8193 — refcount_inc(&stp->st_stid.sc_count) → sc_count = 2"

And his bottom line:

"I don't think there is a bug here."

Why it matters

The exchange illustrates a recurring theme in kernel lock-stateid work: the lockowner/stateid refcounting introduced by the 5db1c03feb00 cleanup is subtle enough that plausible-looking UAF reports can be generated against it, but the actual reference lifecycle — allocation plus an explicit increment inside init_lock_stateid() — keeps the stateid alive across the error path in question. Because Layton's reply was decisive and no further messages appeared in the thread this week (only two messages total), the patch is effectively dead: no Acked-by, no rework, no stable backport.

For readers tracking nfsd bug-fix activity, the takeaway is that this particular UAF report should be closed as a false positive — the nfs4_put_stid() inside release_lock_stateid() cannot drop the last reference while lock_stp still holds its own count from init_lock_stateid().
