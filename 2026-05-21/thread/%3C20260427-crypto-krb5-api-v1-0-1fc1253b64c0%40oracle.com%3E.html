NFS client maintainer acknowledges rpcsec_gss_krb5 crypto/krb5 migration

Summary

NFS Client Maintainer Acknowledges rpcsec_gss_krb5 Migration to crypto/krb5

Overview

Type: Major refactoring / Security improvement (Bug prevention)

This thread continues the discussion around Chuck Lever's 18-patch series to migrate the NFS client's rpcsec_gss_krb5 module from its private Kerberos 5 cryptographic implementation to the common kernel crypto/krb5 library. The series was originally posted on April 27th and has been under review since then.

Bridge to Prior Weeks

In the previous week (ending 2026-04-30), Chuck Lever submitted the full 18-patch series, which removes over 4,000 lines of duplicated cryptographic code from rpcsec_gss_krb5 and consolidates encryption, decryption, MIC generation, and MIC verification through the centralized crypto/krb5 library. Key technical changes included scatterlist conversion helpers, AEAD-based wrap/unwrap operations, and removal of per-enctype function pointer tables.

NFS maintainer Jeff Layton reviewed the series favorably Re: PATCH 00/18, providing a Reviewed-by tag and noting:

"Love that diffstat. Nice work!"

Chuck Lever confirmed the patches were applied to his nfsd-testing tree, but noted an outstanding request for feedback from NFS client maintainers Re: PATCH 00/18:

"An Acked-by: from one of the NFS client maintainers would be great too."

This Week's Activity

This week saw a single but significant message: Anna Schumaker, an NFS client maintainer, responded after testing the series in practice Re: PATCH 00/18:

"I finally had a chance to apply this and try it out. You can add my acked-by:"

She then provided the formal acknowledgment:

"Acked-by: Anna Schumaker anna.schumaker@hammerspace.com"

Significance for NFS Client Development

Anna Schumaker's Acked-by is a critical milestone for this series. The migration of rpcsec_gss_krb5 to crypto/krb5 represents one of the most substantial security-hardening efforts in the SUNRPC subsystem in recent years. By eliminating duplicated cryptographic implementations within the NFS client, the patchset:

  1. Reduces the audit surface for security-critical Kerberos operations in the NFS client
  2. Ensures automatic propagation of new encryption types and hardware offload capabilities from crypto/krb5 to SUNRPC consumers
  3. Simplifies maintenance by removing per-enctype dispatch tables and legacy skcipher/ahash handles

With Jeff Layton's Reviewed-by and Anna Schumaker's Acked-by, the series now has the necessary maintainer approvals for eventual upstream integration into the mainline kernel.

Final Conclusions

The discussion this week was brief but decisive: the NFS client maintainer tested the 18-patch series and provided formal acknowledgment. This completes the review cycle from the perspective of both NFS server (Layton) and NFS client (Schumaker) maintainers, paving the way for the series to be merged into the mainline kernel.
