nfsd IOModes: Benchmarking Disparities and DONTCACHE_LAZY Progress

Summary

This week, the discussion on nfsd input/output modes (iomodes) continued, focusing on the validity of benchmarking methodologies and the path forward for fixing the performance issues with RWF_DONTCACHE. Jeff Layton responded to critiques from Mike Snitzer regarding his test setup, while Chuck Lever highlighted the difficulty of reproducing high-performance results on smaller systems. The consensus remains that the current RWF_DONTCACHE implementation is flawed for heavy write workloads, and Layton's experimental DONTCACHE_LAZY approach offers a promising solution.

Key Technical Arguments

Benchmarking Methodology and Testbed Disparities

Mike Snitzer previously critiqued Jeff Layton's benchmarks for not exposing Memory Management (MM) subsystem bottlenecks, contrasting Layton's ~4-5 GB/s results with Jon Flynn's 72 GB/s achieved on a high-end testbed. This week, Layton acknowledged the disparity:

"That may very well be, but not everyone has a box as large as the one you and Jon were working with." Jeff Layton

Layton noted that his test used a single SSD, whereas Flynn's setup utilized 16 NVMe drives and RDMA. He conceded the core issue with RWF_DONTCACHE:

"Right. I think that's the big takeaway from this. Ignoring claude's rambling about changing default iomodes in the server, RWF_DONTCACHE just sucks for heavy writes. There is room for improvement there." Jeff Layton

Reproducibility Concerns

Chuck Lever emphasized the practical implications of this testing gap, noting that the inability to reproduce high-end results on smaller systems hinders development:

"Right, and this is kind of a blocker for us. Practically speaking, Jon's results are not reproducible. It would be immensely helpful if the MM-tipover behavior could be reproduced on smaller systems... so that Jeff and I can study the issue on our own systems." Chuck Lever

Path Forward for DONTCACHE

Layton proposed sending his DONTCACHE_LAZY patch to Snitzer and Flynn for testing on their high-end rig to see if it improves upon O_DIRECT performance. The experimental mode aims to fix the aggressive flushing behavior of RWF_DONTCACHE by:

  1. Skipping flushes if writeback is already in progress (mapping_tagged(PAGECACHE_TAG_WRITEBACK)).
  2. Capping nr_to_write to the number of pages just written, preventing full-file flushes.

Snitzer offered assistance in reproducing the tests, noting that Hammerspace still has access to a similar high-end lab setup:

"Hammerspace still has the same performance lab setup, so we can certainly reproduce if needed (and try to scale it down, etc) but unfortunately its currently tied up with other important work." Mike Snitzer

He also suggested documenting the test setup for broader community testing and jokingly proposed Jens Axboe as a potential tester given his access to high-performance hardware.

Evolution and Conclusions

The discussion evolved from a critique of benchmarking methods to a collaborative effort to validate the DONTCACHE_LAZY optimization on appropriate hardware.

  1. Bug Fix/Optimization Focus: The thread reaffirms that RWF_DONTCACHE has severe scalability issues for concurrent streaming writes due to aggressive flushing behavior, causing serialization and throughput collapse.
  2. Proposed Solution: The DONTCACHE_LAZY approach is viewed as a meaningful step forward. Layton intends to eventually merge these behaviors into the standard DONTCACHE implementation rather than maintaining a separate iomode long-term.
  3. Testing Collaboration: Snitzer's offer to test the patch on high-end hardware, combined with Lever's call for reproducible results on smaller systems, highlights the need for benchmarking that can both expose MM bottlenecks and be accessible to developers without massive server resources.

The consensus is that while RWF_DONTCACHE requires significant improvement, the lazy variant represents a promising direction for NFS server performance in streaming write scenarios. The next step involves testing Layton's patch on high-end hardware to validate its effectiveness against O_DIRECT.
