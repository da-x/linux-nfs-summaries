Clarification on netfs/cachefiles patch series structure

Summary

cachefiles: Clarification on the Structure of the netfs 26-Patch Series

Overview

This thread consists of a single exchange this week in which David Howells clarified the rationale behind the ordering of patches in his recently submitted 26-patch series for netfslib and cachefiles. The discussion is classified as a clarification regarding an ongoing patch submission that combines both bug fixes and a major architectural refactoring.

Context from Prior Weeks

As summarized in prior threads, David Howells submitted a formal 26-patch series on March 26, replacing folio_queue with segmented bio_vec[] chains (bvecq) across netfslib, cachefiles, AFS, CIFS, and other network filesystems. The first seven patches (01/26–07/26) are standalone bug fixes, while the remaining patches (08/26–26/26) constitute the core architectural changes. This structure was necessitated because the main refactoring patches depend on the prior fixes being applied first.

This Week's Discussion

The thread root originates from a question by Aditya Kammati regarding Patch 07/26, titled "cachefiles: Fix excess dput() after end_removing()." The inquiry was brief:

"Why are this bulk fixes"

David Howells responded on March 26, clarifying the dependency structure of the series:

"I'm not sure what you're asking. Are you wanting to know why the series begins with a collection of fix patches? If so, it's because the main patches (08-26) are dependent on them being applied first." — David Howells

Key Technical Arguments

Howells' explanation underscores a common practice in kernel development: when a large refactoring series is submitted, prerequisite bug fixes are often bundled at the beginning to ensure a clean foundation for subsequent changes. In this case, the seven fix patches address issues such as:

  • NULL pointer dereferences in netfs_unbuffered_write() on retry
  • Kernel BUGs in netfs_limit_iter() for ITER_KVEC iterators
  • Race conditions triggering VM_BUG_ON_FOLIO() in netfs_write_begin()
  • Error handling defects in netfs_extract_user_iter()
  • Read abandonment during retry loops
  • Excess dput() after end_removing() in cachefiles

These fixes are critical for NFS client development because they stabilize the underlying netfslib infrastructure upon which NFS fscache integration depends. Without these corrections, the architectural changes in patches 08–26 would be built on unstable ground.

Conclusion

This brief exchange serves as a clarifying note rather than a substantive technical debate. It highlights Howells' approach to patch series organization: prerequisite bug fixes are front-loaded to ensure that the main refactoring patches can be cleanly applied and reviewed. For NFS client developers, this means that the stability improvements in patches 01–07 will benefit NFS fscache operations even before the larger bvecq migration takes effect.
