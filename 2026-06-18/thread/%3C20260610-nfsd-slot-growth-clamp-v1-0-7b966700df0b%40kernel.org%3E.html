Slot-growth clamp RFC lands in nfsd-testing; Coddington reviews and plans fair-queue rebase

Summary

Slot-growth clamp RFC: review sign-off, merge into nfsd-testing, and the road to a fair-queue rebase

What happened this week

The thread's main action moved from patch-level review (last week) to consolidation and downstream planning. Two messages landed on June 15, both on the cover-letter thread, closing out the review phase and setting up the next step for Ben Coddington's per-client fair-queue dispatch series.

Benjamin Coddington weighed in late but with weight. In his reply to Chuck Lever's cover letter, Coddington — the author of the original fair-queueing RFC from which this series "grew out" — finally reviewed Lever's five-patch clamp. He was candid about the timing:

"I'm pretty late to review this, especially as it lands right in the same zone I was exploring. Sorry about that."

He then added his sign-off: Reviewed-by: Benjamin Coddington <bcodding@hammerspace.com> — a meaningful addition because the RFC's stated purpose is precisely the starvation scenario Coddington's fair-queue work targets at the dispatch layer. With this tag, the series now carries three Reviewed-by lines (Neil Brown, Jeff Layton, and Coddington).

Coddington also proposed a concrete follow-up: rebase his own fair-queue series on top of the clamp once it lands in nfsd-testing, then re-run the A/B harness with the clamp as the new baseline, and offer a Tested-by tag. He flagged the remaining work on that side:

"The work I have yet to do on the fair-queue series is to show a prototyped burst-allowance."

Chuck Lever confirmed the merge state. In his reply, Lever answered each of Coddington's points:

"No worries. These are sitting in nfsd-testing, and will be for a while since the merge window is now open."

He confirmed he had added Coddington's Reviewed-by tag, accepted the rebase plan ("Rebase on nfsd-testing whenever you are ready to post for me to merge"), and welcomed the offer of a Tested-by: "That would be great if you can!"

Nature of the change (recap)

As established last week, this is a bug fix / heuristic correction in the NFSv4.1 server (nfsd), not a protocol change or new feature. The series caps nfsd4_sequence()'s 20% on-demand slot growth at the sunrpc-reported thread ceiling (svc_serv_maxthreads()), per session, so that "a single busy NFSv4.1 client … can no longer monopolize the nfsd thread pool and starve its peers." The dynamic-slot machinery is otherwise left intact; clients still size sessions directly at CREATE_SESSION, so no client-side negotiation or behavior change is required.

Key technical arguments carried forward

The substantive design debate was settled last week and is not reopened this week, but the two points that anchored it remain the load-bearing ones for anyone reading the final series:

  • Per-session cap, not per-namespace. A shared budget "breaks at the floor" because slot 0 is never reclaimable; capping each session independently is "the natural choice."
  • Cap against the configured max, not the live thread count. Lever's rationale: "nfsd sizes its pool dynamically, and gating on the live count would deny a client resuming from idle the slots it needs before the pool scales back up."

Evolution of the discussion

  1. June 10–11 (prior week): Lever posts the 5-patch RFC; Neil Brown and Jeff Layton review within hours, each adding a Reviewed-by with minor nits (the unexplained from ?: 1 ternary in patch 2/5; a suggested kdoc note on slot-zero non-reclaimability in patch 4/5). Lever commits to address both before applying.
  2. June 15 (this week): Coddington adds his Reviewed-by, acknowledges the overlap with his own fair-queue work, and proposes rebasing + re-running the A/B harness. Lever confirms the series is already in nfsd-testing (the merge window being open) and accepts the follow-up plan.

Final conclusions

  • The RFC has three independent Reviewed-by tags (Brown, Layton, Coddington) and is already applied to nfsd-testing, where it will sit for the duration of the open merge window before heading upstream.
  • The next milestone is Coddington's fair-queue dispatch series rebased on this clamp, with a fresh A/B run through the harness and a Tested-by tag, plus a prototyped burst-allowance demonstration. That work remains outstanding.
  • For NFS client developers: no client-side change is required by this fix; the practical benefit is that a greedy NFSv4.1 client can no longer inflate its session toward 2048 slots on a server with far fewer threads, reducing pool starvation of peer clients. The larger per-client fairness question is deferred to Coddington's dispatch-layer series.
