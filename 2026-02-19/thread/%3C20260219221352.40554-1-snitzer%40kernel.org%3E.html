RFC: nfs4_acl passthru for NFSv4 reexport

Summary

NFS/NFSD: RFC for nfs4_acl Passthru in NFSv4 Reexport Scenarios

Classification: New Feature / Protocol Enhancement (RFC) Participants: Mike Snitzer, Chuck Lever, Trond Myklebust Key Message IDs:

  • Cover Letter: [id://20260219221352.40554-1-snitzer@kernel.org]
  • Chuck Lever's Review: [id://098b9502-8868-47c9-b164-be80bb11ae74@oracle.com]
  • Trond Myklebust's Comment: [id://4d1a2b195f0d2ece9d0db5524e69462d71d3b626.camel@kernel.org]

Overview

This week, Mike Snitzer submitted an 11-patch RFC series titled "NFS/NFSD: nfs4_acl passthru for NFSv4 reexport" to the linux-nfs mailing list. The primary goal of this work is to enable full support for NFS v4.1 ACLs (including both Discretionary Access Control Lists [DACL] and System Access Control Lists [SACL]) when an NFS v4.1 client accesses a filesystem that is reexported via NFSD v4.1 from an underlying NFS v4.2 mount.

Currently, knfsd imposes a lossy mapping on NFSv4 ACLs. This patchset introduces a "passthru" mechanism that allows the raw nfs4_acl structure to be passed directly through the reexport layer without conversion, preserving ACL integrity when the backend filesystem supports full NFS v4.1 ACL semantics.

Technical Arguments and Implementation Details

The patchset modifies both the NFS client (fs/nfs/) and NFSD (fs/nfsd/) subsystems. Snitzer noted in the cover letter:

"This patchset aims to enable NFS v4.1 ACLs to be fully supported from an NFS v4.1 client to an NFSD v4.1 export that reexports NFS v4.2 filesystem which has full support for NFS v4.1 ACLs (DACL and SACL)."

Key Changes:

  1. Export Infrastructure: Added EXPORT_OP_NFSV4_ACL_PASSTHRU flag to exportfs.h and new .setacl and .getacl methods to the export_operations structure, allowing filesystems to advertise their capability to handle raw NFSv4 ACLs.
  2. NFSD Enhancements:
    • Factored out nfsd_supports_nfs4_acl() into a shared header.
    • Implemented deferred decoding of ACL payloads using xdr_buf to avoid unnecessary memcpy operations when passthru is not required (optimized via static_key).
    • Added support for passing through DACL and SACL in reexport scenarios.
  3. NFS Client Enhancements:
    • Wired up the .setacl and .getacl hooks in the NFS client to utilize the upper-layer provided nfs4_acl pages directly in NFSPROC4_CLNT_SETACL and NFSPROC4_CLNT_GETACL calls.
    • Ensured that in reexport cases, __nfs4_get_acl_uncached() is used so the frontend client caches the ACL returned by the backend client.

Discussion Evolution

Chuck Lever's Concerns

Chuck Lever provided early feedback on the structure of the patchset and future extensibility:

"I haven't been a fan of these double-subsystem patch sets. Is there any way we can get this split into one set for NFSD and one for the NFS client, with as little code sharing between the two as possible?"

He also questioned the scope regarding POSIX ACLs:

"Do you have a plan for similar passthru support for the NFSv4.2 POSIX draft ACL extension?"

Mike Snitzer's Response

Snitzer acknowledged the difficulty in splitting the patches due to the interdependent nature of reexport logic:

"Open to suggestions, but the reexport nature of this patchset makes it tough for each half to be entirely standalone."

He explained that while NFSD patches could land independently, they would remain dormant until the NFS client sets the EXPORT_OP_NFSV4_ACL_PASSTHRU flag. Regarding POSIX ACLs, Snitzer admitted he had not prioritized them:

"Wasn't on my radar... I still have work ahead to understand all that you guys landed... but being POSIX I just paid attention to the mutual exclusion of your newer work versus nfs4_acl."

He highlighted the driving use case for v4.1 ACL passthru:

"Relative to NFS v4.1 ACL passthru, Hammerspace needing to support 4.1 access for the benefit of kerberos usage made adding 4.1 ACL passthru to 4.2 a compelling feature/requirement to implement."

Trond Myklebust's Perspective on POSIX ACLs

Trond Myklebust weighed in on the necessity of passthru for POSIX ACLs, arguing that the current need for passthru is specific to the lossy mapping imposed by knfsd on NFSv4 ACLs:

"The current need for a passthrough mechanism is being driven by the fact that knfsd unconditionally imposes it's own lossy mapping onto the NFSv4 ACLs."

He suggested that POSIX ACLs might not require similar treatment:

"I'm assuming that for POSIX ACLs, knfsd will just pass them through unmolested to the VFS layer. Subsequently, they will get passed to the NFS client via the POSIX ACL interface, which pass them on to any server that does support that kind of ACL, and fail if not."

Final Conclusions

The thread concluded with no formal acceptance or rejection of the RFC, but rather a clarification of design principles:

  1. NFSv4 ACL Passthru: The mechanism is deemed necessary for reexport scenarios involving NFSv4.1 ACLs due to knfsd's lossy mapping behavior. The implementation leverages new export operations to bypass conversion when the backend supports it.
  2. POSIX ACL Passthru: There is a consensus that POSIX ACLs may not require a similar passthru mechanism because they are expected to pass through the VFS layer without modification, unlike NFSv4 ACLs.
  3. Patch Structure: While splitting the patchset into separate NFSD and Client series was suggested, Snitzer indicated it is technically challenging due to dependencies. The discussion may continue at the upcoming Bake-a-thon.

This work represents a significant enhancement for NFS reexport architectures, particularly for environments requiring Kerberos authentication and strict ACL preservation across NFS version boundaries.
