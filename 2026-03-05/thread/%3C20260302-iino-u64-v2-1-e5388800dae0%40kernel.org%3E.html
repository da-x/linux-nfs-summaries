vfs: i_ino u64 v2 — Debate over Scaffolding Strategy and Patch Granularity

Overview

This thread captures the continuation of the debate surrounding Jeff Layton's [PATCH v2 001/110] vfs: introduce kino_t typedef and PRIino format macro introduced earlier this week. The broader series aims to widen inode->i_ino from unsigned long (32-bit on 32-bit architectures) to u64, an architecture-level VFS change that effectively resolves the longstanding 32-bit inode truncation problem for NFS and other network filesystems.

Following last week's discussion of v1 vfs: i_ino u64 series — v2 planning, bisection strategy, and tracepoint ABI debates, v2 was posted with 110 patches using temporary kino_t typedef and PRIino format macro scaffolding to ensure bisectability. This week's activity focused on a heated exchange between Christoph Hellwig and Jeff Layton regarding whether the scaffolding approach — or alternatively, a single giant patch — is preferable.

Key Technical Arguments This Week

The Scaffolding vs. Single-Patch Debate

The central argument this week revolved around patch granularity and the value of the temporary kino_t/PRIino scaffolding. Jeff Layton had previously outlined three options for handling the transition: (1) one giant patch, (2) accept interim build breakage, or (3) use typedef/macro scaffolding at the cost of extra churn. He favored option 3 for bisectability.

Christoph Hellwig pushed back strongly against the micro-splitting approach in his response on March 3:

"That's not actually a lot, especially for a patch that should be scriped and mechnical."

"I also really don't understand the backport argument. It's not like you could backport any of the split out patches individually anyway."

Hellwig's position is clear: if the change is mechanical and scripted, the resulting ~1000-line patch touching ~200 files should be acceptable as a single unit. He views the scaffolding as unnecessary complexity that adds churn without proportional benefit, given that individual patches cannot be backported in isolation regardless.

Christian Brauner's Pragmatic Stance

VFS maintainer Christian Brauner weighed in on March 4, expressing indifference to the organizational approach but highlighting a practical concern about bisection:

"I'm kinda surprised that we suddenly started caring about the amount of individual patches. I personally don't care either way. Do it in one giant patch if this moves us forward."

Brauner then articulated a key frustration with intermediate scaffolding approaches:

"I've done 1 and 3 and what you did. And I'd be really annoyed if during a bisect I start to get pointless build failures because someone did 2."

This remark underscores the tension between three strategies: (1) one giant patch, (2) accepting interim build breakage on certain architectures, and (3) scaffolding that maintains buildability but adds churn. Brauner's preference leans toward option 1 if it accelerates progress, provided it does not introduce bisect-unfriendly build failures in intermediate states.

NFS Client Development Context

While this specific thread focuses on VFS-level organizational strategy, the implications for NFS client development remain profound. As noted in last week's summary, once merged, this series enables:

  • Elimination of the nfs_inode.fileid field (redundant with inode->i_ino)
  • Removal of the enable_ino64 module parameter
  • Deletion of XOR-folding helpers like nfs_compat_user_ino64()
  • Cleanup of 32-bit inode hashing workarounds

The NFS client patches in this series include PATCH 011/110 (format string conversion to PRIino) and PATCH 065/110 (final cleanup replacing PRIino with %llu/%llx). No NFS maintainer weighed in during this week's window, though Layton noted he would seek Christian Brauner's opinion before respinning.

Classification

This discussion is about an architecture-level VFS change, not a traditional bug fix or new feature. However, it effectively resolves the 32-bit inode truncation problem that has plagued NFS and other network filesystems for years, making it functionally equivalent to a major bug fix for those subsystems.

Conclusions and Next Steps

  1. Christoph Hellwig remains unconvinced by the scaffolding approach, preferring either a single large patch or accepting interim build breakage on 32-bit architectures.
  2. Christian Brauner is indifferent to the organizational approach but prioritizes forward momentum and bisect-friendly outcomes. He explicitly warned against strategies that cause "pointless build failures" during bisection.
  3. Layton is awaiting Brauner's final opinion before deciding whether to respin. The debate remains unresolved at week's end.
  4. The NFS client benefits remain unchanged: regardless of organizational strategy, the downstream cleanup opportunities for the NFS client stand as a major win once the series is merged.
