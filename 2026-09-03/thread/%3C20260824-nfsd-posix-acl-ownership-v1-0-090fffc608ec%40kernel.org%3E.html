Jeff Layton reviews Lever's NFSD COMPOUND teardown fixes

Summary

This week's activity is a single review tag on Chuck Lever's two-patch bug-fix series for NFSD COMPOUND argument teardown. There is still no NFS client development (fs/nfs/ is untouched). The patches remain server-side repairs in fs/nfsd/, with client impact only as the party that can trigger the leak and as the users of a server that must not OOB-read its own rpc_status dump.

Prior week, in brief

On 24 August, Chuck Lever posted the series after Prabhakar Pujeri of Dell reported both defects in one combined patch. Lever split them for LTS routing:

"They are split here because they need different LTS routing. The rpc_status dump requires no privilege and is present in a default build. NFSv4 POSIX ACL support is experimental and disabled by default, so the ACL-related fix is mainline-only."

Patch 1 closes a TOCTOU in nfsd_nl_rpc_status_get_dumpit(): an unprivileged local user can race NFSD_CMD_RPC_STATUS_GET against a finishing COMPOUND and walk off the end of struct nfsd4_compoundargs. Patch 2 stops a POSIX ACL leak when OP_OPEN/OP_CREATE/OP_SETATTR are decoded but never executed, by chaining decoder ACLs for the life of the compound and giving struct nfsd_attrs its own posix_acl_dup() reference. Lever's commit message on the leak is blunt:

"A client can repeat an aborting compound to force the server to leak memory."

Nobody reviewed in that window.

This week: a Reviewed-by, with a typo

On 28 August, Jeff Layton replied to the cover letter with a one-line tag:

"Reviewe-by: Jeff Layton jlayton@kernel.org"

That is the entire in-window discussion. He quotes Lever's cover letter (the split, the rejection of Pujeri's original ownership transfer, and the decision to block the dump OOB rather than touch unreachable argp->opcnt assignments) and adds no further technical commentary. The tag is misspelled (Reviewe-by rather than Reviewed-by); maintainers who scrape review tags will need to treat it as a Reviewed-by by intent.

Layton's review is on the series as a whole (In-Reply-To the cover letter), not a per-patch Ack. There is still no Acked-by from Lever as maintainer, no follow-up v2, and no discussion of whether patch 1 has actually been queued for stable.

Client angle

Still no client patches, protocol changes, or mount-option work. The client-relevant facts from last week stand:

  • A remote NFSv4 client that aborts COMPOUNDs after XDR decode can leak POSIX ACLs on servers that enabled the experimental decoder.
  • Ordinary NFS mounts do not change; there is no client workaround for either bug.
  • The unprivileged rpc_status OOB is a local server-host issue, not an on-the-wire NFS client issue.

Where things stand at week close

  • Kind of change: two NFSD bug fixes. Not a feature, not a protocol change, not client code.
  • Review status: first external review arrives this week via Layton. Routing from last week is unchanged: patch 1 is the LTS candidate; patch 2 is mainline-only (Cc: stable+noautosel).
  • Not concluded: no merge notice, no stable queue confirmation, and no correction of the misspelled tag. Until the series is applied, a busy nfsd remains theoretically OOB-readable via rpc_status, and an aborting client can still leak POSIX ACLs on experimental-decoder builds.
