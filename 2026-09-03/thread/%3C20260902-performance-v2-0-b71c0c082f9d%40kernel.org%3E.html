RFC v2: NFS client lock-contention cuts, minus the pNFS deadlock

Summary

On 2 September, Chuck Lever posted RFC v2 of an eight-patch series that attacks lock contention in the Linux NFS client under a 4KB NFSv3 workload on 100GbE RDMA. This is not a protocol change. It is client performance work in net/sunrpc/ and fs/nfs/, plus a small kernel/workqueue.c helper so sunrpc/nfs can retune unbound queues without a module reload. Two of the v2 deltas are correctness: a send-bvec use-after-free in the queue_lock split, and the withdrawal of v1’s async-inline patch after it reopened a known pNFS deadlock.

The cover letter still frames the problem in CPU-profile terms, not IOPS:

"Under a 4KB NFSv3 workload on 100GbE RDMA, roughly 150 RPC worker threads drive the client, and lock contention dominates its CPU profile: up to 53% of non-idle cycles are spent in native_queued_spin_lock_slowpath."

Three locks account for that: reserve_lock on every XID allocation, queue_lock on every submit and completion, and the unbound worker pool lock on every enqueue and dequeue for rpciod, nfsiod, and xprtiod.

Workqueue maintainer Tejun Heo reviewed the new workqueue_set_affn_scope() helper the same afternoon. Lever replied the next day that the nits are already in an unposted v3. No other reviewer has spoken on the SUNRPC lock splits.

Prior week (brief)

v1 landed on 31 August as [PATCH RFC 0/8] with the same three lock axes and the same Tejun-suggested WQ_AFFN_SMT overrides. Nobody replied in that thread. Two days later Lever superseded it. The important client-development conclusions from that silent RFC are now explicit in the v2 cover:

  • v1’s patch 2 ran the first async RPC FSM states in the submitter to skip a rpciod hop. v2 drops it.
  • v1 exported three workqueue attribute helpers. v2 replaces them with a single setter.
  • v1’s queue_lock split had a send-bvec use-after-free. v2 fixes it (credited to sashiko).

An even earlier RFC tried to fix the pool-lock problem in the workqueue core by falling back to a finer affinity scope when an unbound queue collapsed to a single pod. Tejun rejected a finer global default and offered two alternatives: a sharded scope between CACHE and SMT, and a per-workqueue scope change on the NFS queues themselves. The first became WQ_AFFN_CACHE_SHARD. Lever’s cover letter explains why that is still not enough on the machines he cares about:

"The first became the WQ_AFFN_CACHE_SHARD default, whose 8-core shards still leave a 12-core single-LLC system with only two pools. This series does the second."

Patches 6–8 remain tagged Suggested-by: Tejun Heo.

What kind of change this is

  • Not a protocol change. Wire formats, XID semantics, and NFS versions are untouched.
  • Client performance work, with two important bug-adjacent fixes: the sashiko use-after-free in xprt_request_dequeue_xprt(), and the pNFS deadlock that forced the async-inline patch out.
  • A small workqueue-core API addition so modules can change affinity scope without resetting nice, cpumask, or strict affinity.

v2 changelog: what actually changed from v1

Lever lists six deltas in the cover letter:

"Changes in v2:

  • Fix send bvec use-after-free in xprt_request_dequeue_xprt() (sashiko)
  • Replace the three workqueue exports with workqueue_set_affn_scope()
  • Split the WQ_SYSFS patch into SUNRPC and NFS patches
  • Drop v1 patch 2: async completions in the submitter can deadlock
  • Correct the pool cost and SMT group wording in the scope patches"

The dropped patch is the headline client-development result of this RFC. Dispatching each new async task through rpciod still costs one enqueue and one dequeue per RPC, and v2 leaves that hop in place:

"Running an async task's first states in the submitter's context lets its completion callbacks run there as well. A pNFS read resent from rpc_release then waits for a layout segment that its submitter still holds. That is the deadlock commit 54e4a0dfa25d ("pNFS: Fix a deadlock between read resends and layoutreturn") fixed."

That is a hard constraint for anyone tempted to shave the rpciod hop off the NFS client hot path: do not inline async RPC startup. The workqueue bounce is load-bearing for pNFS.

A remaining race is acknowledged and left open. The scope change is applied after alloc_workqueue() has registered the queue in sysfs, so an administrator write that lands in between is overwritten. Closing that window needs workqueue_sysfs_register() exported and WQ_SYSFS dropped from the alloc calls. This series does not close it.

Patch 1: take XID allocation off reserve_lock

PATCH 1/8 is unchanged in intent from v1. xprt_alloc_xid() currently grabs reserve_lock just to increment a counter. Under the same 100GbE RDMA NFSv3 load, that lock is 1.06% of system-wide CPU in xprt_request_init as ~150 RPC workers serialize on it.

The invariant is that XID allocation does not need the slot table or the backlog:

"reserve_lock protects the slot table and backlog queue, but XID allocation is an independent operation that does not require synchronization with either."

This is the cheapest win in the series: a counter that never needed the lock it was sitting behind. No review comments landed on it this week.

Patch 2: split recv_lock out of xprt->queue_lock (and fix the UAF)

PATCH 2/8 is the lock that accounts for the 53% figure, and the one v2 must not be confused with v1. xprt->queue_lock currently protects two independent structures: the recv_queue rb-tree used for reply matching, and the xmit_queue list used for transmit draining. No hot path touches both in one critical section, but every RPC submit and every completion contends on the same lock. The RDMA completion worker running rpcrdma_reply_handler serializes against ~150 kworkers enqueuing receives and transmits.

The split is surgical:

"Introduce xprt->recv_lock for the receive path -- recv_queue operations, request lookup, receive-side pinning, and completion -- leaving queue_lock to the xmit_queue and the xprt_transmit drain loop."

Pinning is now per-queue. A request is pinned under the lock of the queue it was found through, so xprt_request_dequeue_xprt() drains pins once under each lock before it dequeues from that queue. That is where the sashiko use-after-free lives, and why this is the important client bug fix of the week:

"The transmit dequeue has to see a zero pin count under queue_lock: it frees the send buffer's bvec that a transmitter is iterating, and it aborts a partial send only if the request is still first in the queue."

A receive-side unpin now runs under recv_lock rather than the lock that publishes RPC_TASK_MSG_PIN_WAIT, so xprt_unpin_rqst() wakes the waiter whenever the count reaches zero instead of testing the flag.

A second, smaller shrink: move the rq_private_buf memcpy in xprt_request_enqueue_receive above the lock. Until the rb-tree insert publishes the request, the reply handler cannot see it, so the copy is safe unlocked and the submitter’s critical section collapses to the insert alone.

This is the core NFS-client lock-split in the series. Do not cherry-pick it from v1: v2 is the version that waits for a zero pin count under queue_lock before freeing the send bvec.

Patches 3–4: expose rpciod, xprtiod, and nfsiod in sysfs

v1 lumped the WQ_SYSFS change into one patch. v2 splits it on the module boundary.

PATCH 3/8 creates rpciod and xprtiod with WQ_SYSFS. An unbound workqueue’s attributes can be changed at run time only through /sys/devices/virtual/workqueue/, and a workqueue appears there only when it is created with that flag. Without it, affinity scope cannot be tuned without reloading the sunrpc module.

PATCH 4/8 does the same for nfsiod in fs/nfs/inode.c. Same rationale, NFS module instead of sunrpc.

These are plumbing for the SMT-scope overrides that follow, and for administrators who want to retune without a reload. They do not themselves change scheduling.

Patch 5: workqueue_set_affn_scope() — the only in-thread review

PATCH 5/8 is the workqueue-core piece, and the only patch that drew a reply this week. An unbound workqueue’s affinity scope can be changed only through sysfs. A module whose workqueue contends on the pool lock at the default scope has no in-kernel way to select a finer one, because alloc_workqueue_attrs() and apply_workqueue_attrs() are not exported. Lever argues against exporting them raw:

"Exporting them would also invite a caller to apply freshly allocated attributes, which resets the nice level, cpumask, and strict affinity the workqueue already carries."

The new helper copies the workqueue’s current attributes, replaces only the scope, and applies the result under wq_pool_mutex, as the sysfs affinity_scope store does. SUNRPC and NFS then call it at create time.

Tejun Heo’s review arrived seven minutes after the series. It is short, and it does not reject the helper — it loosens the premise and nits the implementation:

"BTW, please feel free to export them."

That is alloc_workqueue_attrs() / apply_workqueue_attrs(). Tejun is not requiring Lever to drop workqueue_set_affn_scope(); he is saying the raw exports are also acceptable. Style nits follow: no need to break the function signature across lines, and:

"Maybe alloc outside mutex and use alloc and mutex guards?"

Lever’s 3 September reply is the current state of the series:

"All applied to v3 (still unposted). Sorry all this has taken so long."

So v2 is already superseded in Lever’s tree. The NFS client patches themselves have not been re-posted, and Tejun has not yet acked the helper.

Patches 6–8: WQ_AFFN_SMT on rpciod, nfsiod, and xprtiod

These three patches are the payload Tejun suggested: override affinity scope on the NFS workqueues rather than changing the unbound default. v2 keeps all three and corrects the pool-cost wording.

PATCH 6/8 sets WQ_AFFN_SMT on rpciod. On a 12-core system, 30–40% of cycles are lost to native_queued_spin_lock_slowpath in the rpciod pool at WQ_AFFN_CACHE (one pool per LLC). WQ_AFFN_CACHE_SHARD barely helps: its 8-core shards split that machine into two pools of six. Lever’s justification for a per-queue override, rather than another global default fight, is explicit:

"Most UNBOUND workqueues never contend on the pool lock and profit from a coarser scope's cache locality. rpciod's sustained completion traffic makes the lock a first-order bottleneck, so the override belongs on this workqueue rather than in the system-wide default."

v2 is more honest about the cost than v1 was. v1 claimed idle kworkers are culled so extra pools cost little. v2 says:

"The cost is one pool per SMT group, or per CPU on a system without SMT, and each pool keeps up to two idle kworkers rather than culling its last ones."

That is the wording correction called out in the cover letter.

PATCH 7/8 does the same for nfsiod, and it is the patch with the client-visible latency numbers. On a single-socket system whose LLC fits in one CACHE_SHARD, every NFS I/O completion serializes on one nfsiod pool lock. Profiling 4KB random writes over NFSv3/RDMA with nconnect=3 shows that lock at 17% of CPU: 8% dequeuing work, 9% enqueuing follow-on work from rpciod and nfsiod workers. WQ_AFFN_SMT gives each SMT group its own pool, so queue_work_on() contends only with sibling threads. Enqueue contention disappears; dequeue contention drops to 1.4%. Throughput is unchanged (transport-limited), but the latency numbers are the client-visible result:

"the freed cycles cut submission latency variance by 67% (slat stdev 31.6 us to 10.5 us), IOPS stdev by 31%, and p99.9 completion latency by 11%."

v1 said “each CPU its own pool”; v2 says “each SMT group,” matching the WQ_AFFN_SMT semantics on systems that actually have SMT.

PATCH 8/8 applies the same WQ_AFFN_SMT override to xprtiod, which handles socket receive processing, error recovery, and connection lifecycle and contends on the unbound pool lock the same way rpciod does. One-line change in net/sunrpc/sched.c; no separate numbers.

Evolution and conclusions

This week’s thread is the series that actually has a chance to land, plus a workqueue-API nit-pick that has already been folded into an unposted v3. The technical argument is aimed squarely at NFS client development:

  1. XID allocation does not belong under reserve_lock. Make it atomic_t. Cheap, independent, 1% of CPU. Unchanged from v1, still unreviewed.
  2. queue_lock is doing two jobs. Split recv_lock for the reply rb-tree; leave queue_lock for transmit drain. This is the 53% lock. v2 is the version to take: it waits for a zero pin count under queue_lock before freeing the send bvec (sashiko UAF).
  3. Do not inline the first async RPC states. v1 proposed it to skip a rpciod hop; v2 withdrew it because completion callbacks in the submitter re-create the pNFS read-resend vs. layoutreturn deadlock of 54e4a0dfa25d. The hop stays. That is the most important client-correctness conclusion of the week.
  4. Workqueue pool locks are a first-order NFS client bottleneck on high-IOPS RDMA, and WQ_AFFN_CACHE_SHARD is too coarse on single-LLC 12-core boxes. Override rpciod, nfsiod, and xprtiod to WQ_AFFN_SMT rather than fighting for a finer global default. Cost: one pool per SMT group, each keeping up to two idle kworkers.
  5. Expect latency, not IOPS. On a transport-limited 100GbE RDMA client the win is submission-latency variance (−67%), IOPS stdev (−31%), and p99.9 completion latency (−11%).
  6. The workqueue helper is close but not acked. Tejun is fine with exporting the raw attribute functions as well, wants the alloc moved outside wq_pool_mutex, and wants scoped guards. Lever says that is already in v3, which has not been posted.

No Reviewed-by, no Acked-by, and no merge conclusion. The SUNRPC lock splits have not been reviewed at all this week. Treat v2 as the current public design, and expect a v3 that only retouches workqueue_set_affn_scope(). The sysfs registration race — scope applied after alloc_workqueue() has already advertised the queue — remains open by design.
