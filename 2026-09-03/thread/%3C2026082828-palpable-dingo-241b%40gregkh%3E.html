nfsd: hold cl_lock in client_has_state()

Summary

This week’s linux-nfs traffic around client_has_state() is a server-side bug fix, not an NFS client feature or protocol change. Cen Zhang (Microsoft Security FORGE Labs) posted a one-line locking patch for a KASAN-confirmed use-after-free in NFSD’s NFSv4 state machine, then spent the rest of the thread answering Greg Kroah-Hartman’s From: address policy. Chuck Lever applied the original patch to nfsd-testing anyway.

The race: unlocked openowner walk vs. nfs4_put_stateowner()

Zhang’s patch targets fs/nfsd/nfs4state.c. client_has_openowners() walks clp->cl_openowners and inspects so_stateids without clp->cl_lock. Meanwhile nfs4_put_stateowner() unhashes that openowner under cl_lock and frees it. A concurrent EXCHANGE_ID with mismatched credentials can therefore use-after-free the nfs4_openowner.

Zhang’s report is explicit:

"client_has_openowners() walks clp->cl_openowners and reads so_stateids without clp->cl_lock. nfs4_put_stateowner() unhashes that openowner under cl_lock and then frees it, so a concurrent EXCHANGE_ID with mismatched creds can use-after-free the nfs4_openowner."

The KASAN splat cited in the commit message lands in client_has_state() itself:

"BUG: KASAN: slab-use-after-free in client_has_state+0x10a/0x140
fs/nfsd/nfs4state.c:3718 client_has_openowners()
nfsd4_exchange_id
nfsd4_proc_compound
nfsd_dispatch
svc_process"

The proposed fix is correspondingly small: take clp->cl_lock for the duration of the openowner walk inside client_has_state(). The patch is tagged

Fixes: 4eaea1342507 ("nfsd: improve client_has_state to check for unused openowners")

with Cc: stable@vger.kernel.org, Reported-by: Xiang Mei (Microsoft), and a Microsoft Autonomous Code Security contact. In other words, this is a stable-worthy locking hole in the server’s notion of “does this NFS client still have state?”, not a client-side change and not a wire-protocol tweak.

There was no technical objection on the list this week to the lock placement, the diagnosis, or the Fixes tag. Review energy went to process, not to the race.

Process: gmail From: vs. applying a UAF fix

Greg Kroah-Hartman replied first, not with a code review but with a From: policy reminder:

"Please use your microsoft.com email address, and not a random gmail.com address. It's kind of odd that Microsoft would have to rely on Google to send kernel patches :)"

Zhang’s only message in this split is an apology and a pause on further postings:

"Sorry for making you raise this again. I will hold off on new patch submissions until my approved Microsoft email address is available, which should be soon."

Chuck Lever then asked Greg whether the existing patch should wait for a resend from a microsoft.com address:

"Greg, would you prefer that I hold off on applying this to nfsd-testing until Cen can post this one again?"

Greg declined to dictate:

"That's your call."

Outcome: applied to nfsd-testing

Lever took that as leave to land the UAF fix. On 29 August he applied the original gmail-posted patch to nfsd-testing:

"Applied to nfsd-testing, thanks!"

as commit 6882aca9f9cabfaec5ea23d1512f345fbd288b65 (nfsd: hold cl_lock in client_has_state()).

Bottom line for this week: the technical conclusion is that client_has_state() must hold cl_lock while walking openowners, closing a use-after-free on the NFSD client-state path during EXCHANGE_ID. The social conclusion is that Greg wants a microsoft.com From: on future Microsoft submissions, Zhang will wait for that address, and Lever still queued the locking fix rather than block a KASAN UAF on identity paperwork. No NFS client code, mount options, or protocol fields changed.
