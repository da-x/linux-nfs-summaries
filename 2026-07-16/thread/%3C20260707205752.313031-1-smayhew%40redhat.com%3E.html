nfs-utils mountd netlink fixes committed for 2.9.2-rc5

Summary

nfs-utils mountd netlink fixes: series landed in nfs-utils-2.9.2-rc5

This week's activity

The single development this week is the confirmation that Scott Mayhew's three-patch mountd netlink fixes series — reviewed and approved by Jeff Layton last week — has been merged upstream.

On Friday, 2026-07-11, nfs-utils maintainer Steve Dickson replied to the original cover letter with a terse but definitive:

"Committed... (tag: nfs-utils-2-9-2-rc5)"

This closes out the series. The fixes are now part of the nfs-utils-2.9.2-rc5 release tag and will ship in the next stable nfs-utils 2.9.2 point release.

What was committed (recap)

For readers who missed last week's detailed breakdown, the three patches were:

  1. configure: update check of system netlink headers — a two-line configure.ac fix so that nfsstat builds correctly against system netlink headers rather than the in-tree ones (which contained symbols not yet present in kernel headers).
  2. nfs.conf: add no-netlink option to exportd and mountd stanzas — documentation/configuration gap-filler allowing administrators to persistently disable netlink via nfs.conf instead of relying on a command-line flag.
  3. mountd/exportd: disable netlink when falling back to /proc — the most substantive fix, ensuring that when cache_flush() falls back to the /proc interface (e.g., because SELinux blocked the required CAP_NET_ADMIN), both sunrpc and nfsd netlink sockets are closed. Without this, mount requests could hang indefinitely on SELinux-enforced systems.

Significance for NFS client users

While these patches target server-side tooling (mountd/exportd), their impact on the NFS client experience is direct: a hung mountd on the server translates into stalled or failed mount operations on every connected client. The SELinux-related hang fix in particular is important for enterprise and RHEL/Fedora deployments where mandatory access control is standard.

Status

The thread is now complete — patches posted, reviewed, and committed. No further discussion is expected unless a regression surfaces during the 2.9.2 release cycle.
