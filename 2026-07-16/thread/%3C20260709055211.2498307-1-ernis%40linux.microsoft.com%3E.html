RDMA ib_device_attr int→u32 (v10): Romanovsky applies to rdma-next, closing the ten-revision saga

Summary

RDMA ib_device_attr int→u32 — v10 applied to rdma-next: the ten-revision type-correctness saga is over

This week at a glance

The story that has run from v1 (June 1) through v10 (July 9) reached its denouement on July 12, when RDMA maintainer Leon Romanovsky applied the patch to rdma-next and pointed to the upstream commit:

"Applied, thanks! [1/1] RDMA: Change capability fields in ib_device_attr from int to u32 https://git.kernel.org/rdma/rdma/c/eebd08d91ddbd6"

Just a few hours earlier, Romanovsky had laid out the reasoning for why no v11 was needed, addressing Erni Sri Satya Vennela's cover message directly:

"This patch touches too many areas to delay it further. Let's merge it now and improve it later, if needed."

That is the operative conclusion of the week: the patch lands as-is, with Shevchenko's optional polish notes (xmas-tree declaration order in ipoib_cm_dev_init(), the srq_size_set() error-handling split, and the open min_not_zero() refactoring question) deferred to a possible follow-up rather than blocking the merge.

Bridge from prior weeks

As summarized last week, v10 was posted on July 9 and received a formal Reviewed-by from Andy Shevchenko the same day, with Stefan Metzmacher's Acked-by for the smbdirect hunks carried since v8. The patch entered this week already in a clean state — no new technical objections emerged between review and application; Romanovsky's two messages are the maintainer decision and the applied-confirmation respectively.

What finally landed (and why it matters for NFS/RDMA)

The core defect, stable across all ten revisions: capability counter fields in struct ib_device_attr were declared as signed int despite being inherently non-negative, so drivers caching caps as u32 could implicitly narrow values above INT_MAX into negative numbers visible to the IB core. Vennela's v10 cover message framed it plainly:

"Change the signed int capability fields to u32 to match the underlying nature of the data. Also update consumers across the IB core, ULPs, NVMe-oF target, RDS, and NFS/RDMA so the new u32 values are not forced back through signed int or u8 via min()/min_t() or narrowing local variables."

The diffstat spans 27 files (129 insertions, 133 deletions). For this list, the NFS-over-RDMA hunks — stable since v5–v7 and unchanged in v10 — are the most operationally significant part of the series:

  • net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/frwr_ops.c — unsigned underflow guard: reorders the check before the subtraction so a device reporting zero max_sge_rd cannot produce an SGE count of UINT_MAX − 1 and break RDMA transport negotiation for NFS clients. This is the one hunk that fixes a real bug rather than a latent type mismatch, and it is the piece worth remembering from the series.
  • net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/svc_rdma_transport.c — min_t(int, …) → plain min() once both operands are unsigned.
  • net/sunrpc/xprtrdma/verbs.c / include/linux/sunrpc/svc_rdma.h — sc_max_send_sges to unsigned int, inline_sge_count to u32, and %d → %u in diagnostic prints.

These keep NFS/RDMA clients from mis-negotiating SGE limits when a hardware device reports large (but legitimate) capability values; they ride along with the broader type migration but would matter on their own if the rest of the series had stalled.

Classification

This is unambiguously a bug fix / type-correctness cleanup, not a new feature or protocol change. The wire protocol and on-the-wire semantics are untouched — Vennela confirmed in earlier revisions that all updated variables were checked for unsigned safety ("Yes I have checked that for all the variables I updated") — and Romanovsky's "improve it later, if needed" framing signals the maintainers view this as a correctness fix whose follow-up polish is genuinely optional.

Assessment

The thread is at its natural conclusion point: applied to rdma-next with a Reviewed-by (Shevchenko), an Acked-by (Metzmacher), and no outstanding objections. Romanovsky's "merge it now and improve it later" line is the maintainer's explicit decision to close the review cycle rather than chase v11 for Shevchenko's three non-blocking notes. For NFS/RDMA specifically, the frwr_ops.c underflow guard — preventing a real transport-negotiation failure on devices reporting zero max_sge_rd — is the hunk that lands with the most practical weight, and it now ships as part of commit eebd08d91ddbd6.
