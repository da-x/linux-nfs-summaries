knfsd IOPS Investigation: lwq Spinlock and nfsd_setuser Contention Identified; Patches Promised

Summary

knfsd Read IOPS Limits: Pinpointing lwq Spinlock and nfsd_setuser Contention Hotspots

Summary

Type: Performance investigation / Bug diagnosis leading to upcoming patches

Overview

This week's continuation of the ongoing knfsd read IOPS ceiling investigation [bridged from last week's summary on xpt_mutex contention] saw Daire Byrne and Chuck Lever drill deeper into lock contention profiles. After confirming that earlier patches addressing xpt_mutex were effective, the discussion shifted to two newly identified bottlenecks: the lwq spinlock in the SunRPC thread dispatcher and the group sort operation in nfsd_setuser. Chuck Lever committed to posting patches for both issues.

Bridging from Prior Week

Last week (ending 2026-01-29), the investigation identified the xpt_mutex in the SunRPC thread pool dispatch path as a significant bottleneck, with hold times up to 293ms and a mean wait time an order of magnitude worse than other locks. Chuck Lever noted:

"There's kind of no denying it, the xpt_mutex numbers are shitty. The maximum hold time on that mutex is 293379.40 usecs, with a mean hold time of nearly 16 usec, giving it the worst mean wait time by an order of magnitude." Chuck Lever

Patches to address xpt_mutex via flat combining were subsequently tested. This week, Daire Byrne reported back on those results and provided fresh profiling data.

This Week's Discussion: Two New Hotspots Identified

Confirmation of xpt_mutex Patch Effectiveness

Daire Byrne shared updated lockstat data after applying the patch series. Chuck Lever confirmed the patches were working as intended:

"The lockstat data shows that the patches are applied and that flat combining is reducing xpt_mutex contention, as designed." Chuck Lever

However, a new bottleneck emerged. Chuck observed:

"This time, the lwq spinlock in the thread pool dispatch path shows very high contention (246M events, 54k seconds total wait)." Chuck Lever

He requested a perf record profile to determine whether this spinlock was the throughput-limiting factor or if nfsd threads were spending time elsewhere.

Perf Analysis Reveals Two Contention Areas

After Daire Byrne provided perf data, Chuck Lever identified two significant contention areas:

"Thanks. The two significant contention areas are the lwq idle list in the SunRPC thread dispatcher and the group sort in nfsd_setuser. I'll post some patches to test in a day or two." Chuck Lever

The Role of Group Membership Complexity

Daire Byrne provided critical context regarding the nfsd_setuser hotspot. His environment uses --manage-gids with SSSD bridging to Active Directory, resulting in extremely large group memberships:

"For this workload, on the server, I am a member of 737 groups...." Daire Byrne

Chuck Lever confirmed this was directly relevant:

"Right, the group_sort() done for every nfsd_setuser() will be a significant overhead for you." Chuck Lever

Empirical Validation: Running as Root Improves IOPS

To isolate the impact of nfsd_setuser, Daire Byrne ran his 200-client fio workload as root (who belongs to only 4 groups) instead of his regular user account. The results were telling:

"Out of curiosity, I ran the 200 client fio tasks as 'root' (4 groups) instead of my user and it looks like nfsd_setuser drops out of the perf report (attached). Now it's all mostly lwq_dequeue, and the average iops performance (across multiple runs) increased from ~470k -> 500k iops/s. So a small but measurable difference." Daire Byrne

This experiment confirmed that nfsd_setuser's group sorting overhead was a measurable contributor to the IOPS ceiling, particularly in environments with complex AD-integrated group memberships. With that bottleneck removed, the remaining contention centered on lwq_dequeue in the SunRPC thread dispatcher.

Key Technical Arguments and Evolution

  1. From xpt_mutex to lwq spinlock: The investigation successfully addressed the initial xpt_mutex bottleneck via flat combining patches, but revealed a deeper issue in the lightweight queue (lwq) dispatch path's idle list management.

  2. nfsd_setuser as an environment-dependent hotspot: The group sort operation within nfsd_setuser was identified as a significant overhead for environments with large group memberships (e.g., AD-integrated setups via SSSD). This is not a universal bottleneck but can be material in enterprise deployments.

  3. Upcoming patches: Chuck Lever committed to posting patches addressing both the lwq idle list contention and the nfsd_setuser group sort overhead within days of this discussion.

Significance for NFS Server Development

This thread represents a methodical, data-driven approach to diagnosing knfsd performance limitations under high-concurrency random read workloads. Key takeaways:

  • The xpt_mutex fix is validated, but the underlying SunRPC thread dispatcher architecture continues to present contention challenges via the lwq spinlock.
  • nfsd_setuser group sorting is a previously under-appreciated performance factor for environments with large group memberships, adding measurable overhead to every NFS request requiring user context setup.
  • Chuck Lever's upcoming patches promise targeted improvements to both hotspots, potentially raising the aggregate IOPS ceiling beyond the current ~470–500K range observed in testing.
