nfsrahead: event-driven mountinfo monitoring replaces brittle polling

Summary

nfsrahead: Enable Event-Driven mountinfo Monitoring

Classification: Bug Fix / Robustness Improvement

This week saw a single-message patch submission from Aaron Tomlin addressing a race condition in the nfsrahead utility, a tool used to configure NFS client-side readahead settings. The patch replaces a brittle polling mechanism with an event-driven approach using the libmount monitoring API.

Problem Statement

The nfsrahead utility correlates a device number with a specific NFS mount point by parsing /proc/self/mountinfo. As Aaron Tomlin explained in his submission on February 11:

"The nfsrahead utility relies on parsing "/proc/self/mountinfo" to correlate a device number with a specific NFS mount point. However, due to the asynchronous nature of system initialisation, the relevant entry in mountinfo may not be immediately available when the tool is executed."

The existing implementation used a naive polling loop — retrying five times with a fixed 50ms delay, totaling only 250ms of wait time. Tomlin noted:

"This approach proves brittle on systems under high load or during distinctively slow boot sequences, where the population of the mount table may exceed this brief window. Consequently, nfsrahead fails to configure the readahead value."

Proposed Solution

The patch updates get_device_info() in tools/nfsrahead/main.c to leverage the libmount monitoring API. The new implementation:

  1. Initialises a monitor on /proc/self/mountinfo using mnt_new_monitor().
  2. Replaces the fixed polling loop with mnt_monitor_wait(), allowing the process to sleep until the kernel notifies userspace of a mount table change.
  3. Increases the maximum wait time to 10 seconds (MNT_NM_TIMEOUT), "significantly reducing the likelihood of a timeout failure whilst ensuring the tool returns immediately once the mount appears."
  4. Retains the original polling logic as a fallback should the monitor fail to initialise, preserving backward compatibility.

The patch adds 34 lines and removes 1 line in tools/nfsrahead/main.c.

Significance for NFS Client Development

This fix addresses a practical reliability issue for NFS clients deployed on systems with slow or variable boot sequences — such as embedded devices, virtual machines, or heavily loaded servers. The nfsrahead tool is critical for tuning readahead behavior on NFS mounts; failures to configure it can lead to suboptimal I/O performance. By switching from polling to event-driven monitoring, the utility becomes both more responsive (returning immediately when the mount appears) and more resilient (tolerating up to 10 seconds of delay).

Post-Window Outcome

Outside this week's window, Steve Dickson confirmed the patch was accepted and committed on February 24, tagging it for nfs-utils-2.8.6-rc2.
