NFSD: Revert deferred export cleanup to fix unmount regression

Summary

Discussion Overview

This thread centers on a patch by Yang Erkun to revert commit 48db892356d6 ("NFSD: Defer sub-object cleanup in export put callbacks"). The discussion focuses on whether reverting this change reintroduces a Use-After-Free (UAF) bug or if it is necessary to fix an unmount regression.

Key Technical Arguments

The Proposal: Revert Deferred Cleanup

Yang Erkun proposed the revert, arguing that the original commit introduced a regression related to unmounting (exportfs-ra-umount), which had previously been fixed by commit 69d803c40ede. Yang claimed that the UAF issue the original commit aimed to solve was actually resolved by commit e7fcf179b82d ("NFSD: Hold net reference for the lifetime of /proc/fs/nfs/exports fd").

"Commit 48db892356d6 ... introduces a regression that was already fixed by commit 69d803c40ede ... Therefore, reverting commit 48db892356d6 ... is necessary to fix this regression."

Jeff Layton's Safety Concerns

Jeff Layton raised significant concerns about the safety of the revert. He distinguished between two separate issues:

  1. The net reference lifetime issue (addressed by e7fcf179b82d).
  2. A concurrent UAF where cache_clean() drops references while RCU readers (c_show, e_show) iterate the cache.

Jeff argued that simply holding the net reference does not prevent cache_clean() from freeing sub-objects (like path and auth_domain) while an RCU reader is accessing them. If the revert applies, path_put() and auth_domain_put() are called immediately. While kfree_rcu() protects the main struct, it does not protect the sub-objects if they are freed directly.

"Does this re-introduce the UAF that 48db892356d6 was fixing?"

Resolution of the UAF Concern

Yang Erkun clarified that the sub-objects themselves (dentry, mnt, auth_domain) are also freed via RCU mechanisms (e.g., call_rcu in dentry_free, cleanup_mnt, etc.). Therefore, the rcu_read_lock() held by the readers does protect access to these sub-objects.

"No, something like release of dentry/mnt ek->ek_client->name all protected will call_rcu ... so rcu_read_lock/rcu_read_unlock for c_show/e_show can protect this trigger UAF."

Satisfied with this explanation, Jeff Layton provided his Reviewed-by:

"Got it. Thanks for the explanation. In that case, I'm fine with reverting this patch."

Integration with Ongoing Workqueue Refactoring

Chuck Lever noted a conflict with his own in-flight series, which replaces nfsd_export_wq with a workqueue managed in sunrpc.ko. He questioned whether his approach of deferring resource release via a WQ was still the correct solution.

"It appears to me our fixes conflict with each other... But my series continues to use a WQ to defer resource release for all the cache-related files in /proc/fs/nfsd. Is that the wrong solution?"

Yang Erkun responded that he preferred avoiding the workqueue if possible, as he did not believe deferring auth_domain_put was necessary. He offered to adapt his patch to Chuck's series but emphasized that the single-patch revert would be easier to backport to LTS kernels.

"Certainly, as long as it doesn't reintroduce the exportfs-ra-umount issue, it seems acceptable. However, I would rather avoid doing it."

Final Conclusions

  • Bug Fix Type: This is a revert to fix a regression (unmount hang/failure) introduced by a previous attempt to fix a UAF.
  • Safety: The revert is deemed safe because the sub-objects accessed by RCU readers are themselves freed via RCU, preventing UAFs.
  • Next Steps: Yang Erkun will likely need to adapt his patch to account for Chuck Lever's ongoing workqueue refactoring in sunrpc.ko, though there is a preference for minimizing complexity if the unmount regression is resolved.
