svcrdma: OOM Fix Validated and Accepted

Summary

This week concluded the review cycle for Chuck Lever's two-patch series addressing svcrdma workqueue contention and Out-Of-Memory (OOM) issues. The primary activity this week was Chuck Lever's acknowledgment of successful testing results provided by Mike Snitzer and Jonathan Flynn, effectively closing the discussion loop on the first patch of the series.

Background (Bridged from Prior Week)

As summarized last week, Chuck Lever submitted a series to optimize the svcrdma subsystem Introduced by Chuck Lever. The series targeted two issues:

  1. Patch 1: Eliminated unnecessary re-queuing of work items when releasing write chunk resources, reducing spinlock contention on svcrdma_wq.
  2. Patch 2: Deferred send context release to the nfsd thread via a lock-free list, removing workqueue contention from the Send completion path entirely.

Last week, Mike Snitzer reported that Patch 1 alone resolved critical OOM issues in large-scale testing environments Confirmed by Mike Snitzer, stating:

"You were correct: this patch alone eliminates the OOM (we tested with both 16K and then 4K read IO from 121 clients to 8 NFS servers, no measurable memory growth while testing)."

Snitzer also noted that Patch 2 could not be immediately tested due to kernel version constraints at the customer site but expressed confidence in its design.

This Week's Activity

This week, Chuck Lever responded to Snitzer's feedback, formally acknowledging the successful validation of the OOM fix and the testing contributions:

"Excellent news! Thanks to you both for testing." Chuck Lever

Lever also acknowledged Snitzer's note regarding the timeline for testing Patch 2, responding simply:

"Understood." Chuck Lever

Conclusions

This thread represents a bug fix and performance optimization for the NFS server-side RDMA subsystem (svcrdma). The key outcomes are:

  • Patch 1 is validated and accepted: It resolves OOM conditions under heavy load (121 clients, 8 servers) and reduces CPU overhead by eliminating redundant workqueue re-queuing. Mike Snitzer added his Reviewed-by tag and included a Tested-by tag for Jonathan Flynn.
  • Patch 2 is deferred for future testing: While not yet tested in production due to kernel version constraints, the design was positively received. Snitzer indicated it would be pulled in for testing once newer kernels are deployed at the customer site, which "might take a couple months."

The discussion highlights the collaborative nature of NFS client/server development, where real-world large-scale deployments provide critical validation for performance patches. The immediate impact is the resolution of OOM issues in production environments using Patch 1, with long-term architectural improvements expected from Patch 2 once validated.
