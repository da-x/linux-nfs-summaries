NFS/VFS: Resolving d_alloc_parallel() Deadlock and Locking Debates

Summary

This week, the discussion around Neil Brown's 19-patch series to lift directory lookups out of the exclusive inode lock (PATCH v3 00/19) reached a critical juncture. The focus shifted from broad architectural debates to resolving a specific deadlock bug in the VFS dcache code that was impacting NFS client testing. Al Viro and Neil Brown collaborated to identify and fix a bug in __d_move() related to waking the wrong dentry, which was causing deadlocks in NFS server operations.

Key Technical Arguments and Evolution

1. Root Cause of NFS Deadlock Identified

At the end of the prior week, Neil Brown reported a deadlock observed during testing:

"Also my testing has hit a problem with some sort of deadlock in the nfs server (so accessing an XFS filesystem). They are trying to unlink a file and are waiting in d_alloc_parallel() under reconnect_path. This is running generic/467. So better hold off this patchset until I have that understood" [id://177759308866.1474915.9708613530229799376@noble.neil.brown.name]

This week, Neil diagnosed the root cause, linking it directly to a bug in Al Viro's proposed d_alloc_parallel() refactoring:

"The two problems are actually one. __d_move() is called in d_splice_alias() with the target dentry often being in-lookup. reconnect_path() does exactly this and is expected to find an existing dentry for a directory and to splice that dentry to the in-lookup dentry it has. So the wakeup of the wrong dentry in __d_move() is causing the deadlock in nfsd." [id://177759843868.1474915.6100875324018019224@noble.neil.brown.name]

The bug stemmed from __d_wake_in_lookup_waiters() being called on the wrong dentry in __d_move(). Neil had previously flagged this:

"That code is wrong. It only makes sense to __d_wake_in_lookup_waiters() a dentry that we know was in-lookup, and in d_move, that is target... So we need __d_wake_in_lookup_waiters(target); in d_move" [id://177759308866.1474915.9708613530229799376@noble.neil.brown.name]

Al Viro confirmed the diagnosis:

"Yep." [id://20260501011132.GA3518998@ZenIV]

2. Decoupling d_alloc_parallel() from the Larger Series

Al Viro proposed separating the d_alloc_parallel() fix from the rest of Neil's patchset, arguing it had standalone value:

"Let's deal with d_alloc_parallel() first; it doesn't have to be tied into the rest of patchset. Does the variant I've posted + s/dentry/target/ in that call of __d_wake_in_lookup_waiters() trigger any problems in your testing? If it doesn't, let's get that part out of the way - it makes sense on its own and getting it into -next (I'm sitting on a bunch of fs/dcache.c patches, and it would fit there nicely) would be a good idea." [id://20260501011132.GA3518998@ZenIV]

Neil agreed: "OK" [id://177759959922.1474915.14496442965390503813@noble.neil.brown.name].

3. Testing Results and Next Steps

Neil confirmed that the fix resolved the immediate testing failure:

"generic/467 now passes repeatedly. I'm running the full set now. Shall I post the patch once all the generic/ tests pass? Or will you just use what you already have?" [id://177759994594.1474915.12208704330740243979@noble.neil.brown.name]

4. Naming and Design Concerns for d_alloc_noblock()

Al Viro raised concerns about the naming and semantics of Neil's d_alloc_noblock() function, arguing it was misleading:

"FWIW, your 'noblock' variant is a misnomer - it is a trylock analogue of d_alloc_parallel(), all right, but it might very well block; on allocations, if nothing else, and there's a chance of having that dput(dentry) in 'wouldblock' case coming right after the sucker ceased to be in-lookup and dropping the sole remaining reference. Which may block on real IO, final dput() being what it is..." [id://20260501011132.GA3518998@ZenIV]

Neil was amenable to renaming: "I'm happy to rename it to d_alloc_trylock()" [id://177759959922.1474915.14496442965390503813@noble.neil.brown.name].

5. Concerns About Lock Dropping Patterns

Al Viro expressed strong reservations about the pattern of dropping and regaining caller-held locks, citing historical race conditions:

"And I really dislike the 'drop and regain a caller-held lock' games - we'd been there many times and it had ended up with race galore again and again; see https://lore.kernel.org/all/20250623213747.GJ1880847@ZenIV/ for one recent example..." [id://20260501011132.GA3518998@ZenIV]

Neil acknowledged the concern but defended the approach as a temporary measure:

"I dislike them too. I doubt I can find solutions that either of us like, but they should be relatively short-lived. Once we push the locking down in the inode_operations the filesystem will be in a position to hold the lock only when it actually needs it (if at all). I'm confident that dropping the lock is safe." [id://177759959922.1474915.14496442965390503813@noble.neil.brown.name]

Final Conclusions

  • Bug Fix: The deadlock in NFS client testing was traced to a bug in __d_move() where the wrong dentry was being woken up. The fix involves changing __d_wake_in_lookup_waiters(dentry) to __d_wake_in_lookup_waiters(target).
  • Incremental Progress: Al Viro and Neil Brown agreed to decouple the d_alloc_parallel() refactoring from the larger series, allowing it to be merged independently into -next.
  • Testing Status: The specific test case (generic/467) that was failing now passes repeatedly. Full testing is ongoing.
  • Design Debates Continue: Concerns about lock-dropping patterns and API naming remain open, but are considered secondary to getting the core d_alloc_parallel() fix merged.

This discussion represents a bug fix within the context of a larger locking/protocol change effort. The resolution of the deadlock is a significant step forward for NFS client development, as it removes a blocker for the concurrency improvements that Neil's series aims to deliver.
